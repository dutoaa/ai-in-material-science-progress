## Field Progress

- Foundation models for materials are increasingly being specialized into stable, high-throughput tools for simulation, screening, and downstream fine-tuning.
- Chemical validity, phonon stability, and thermodynamic consistency are now being encoded directly into generative and screening pipelines rather than treated as post hoc filters.
- The community is building infrastructure for reproducible AI materials workflows, including benchmarking platforms, aligned databases, and open potentials.
- Multiscale and disorder-aware modeling is expanding from static property prediction toward temperature-dependent phase behavior, microstructure evolution, and kinetic phenomena.
- There is growing convergence between AI and electronic-structure theory, with models learning not only energies and forces but also Hamiltonians, charges, and spectroscopic observables.

## Open Questions

- How can foundation MLIPs be made simultaneously fast, transferable, and reliable for out-of-distribution chemistries and charge states?
- What is the best way to enforce thermodynamic, dynamical, and synthetic validity in generative materials design without suppressing novelty?
- How can heterogeneous experimental and DFT datasets be aligned in a principled way while preserving uncertainty and method dependence?
- Can multimodal models with structural semantics and symbolic reasoning consistently outperform geometry-only models on real discovery tasks?
- How should AI models be validated for multiscale phenomena such as microstructure evolution, disorder coupling, and finite-temperature phase transitions?
- What benchmark standards are needed to compare LLM-based hypothesis generation, design agents, and human-AI collaborative discovery workflows?

## Outlook

The trajectory in July 2026 suggests that materials AI is moving from proof-of-concept prediction toward integrated scientific infrastructure: foundation models, validity filters, data-alignment methods, and multiscale surrogates are making AI outputs more actionable for simulation and experimentation. The next wave will likely focus on end-to-end discovery loops that combine reasoning, generation, validation, and synthesis planning, while also demanding stronger uncertainty quantification and benchmark discipline. If these pieces continue to converge, AI will increasingly function not just as a predictor of materials properties, but as a practical partner in designing, ranking, and explaining candidate materials across chemistry, structure, and microstructure.
