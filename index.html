Graph Neural Networks for Accurate Quasiparticle Energy Levels in Large Supercells of Transition Metal Dichalcogenides

Rafael R. Del Grande, David A. Strubbe

The GW method is the one of the main workhorses of the materials science community to accurately calculate the electronic bandgap of materials, as it overcomes the gap underestimation problem from Density Functional Theory (DFT). Despite its accuracy, its computational cost becomes prohibitive when calculations have more than 10-100 atoms, which makes more difficult its use for defects, surfaces and moiré systems that demand large supercells. To overcome this problem, we developed a Graph Neural Network trained with smaller GW calculations which can make accurate predictions on large supercells at a small fraction of the computational cost. Our model uses as features the projection of DFT wavefunctions into localized atomic orbitals, while graph nodes represent the atomic positions and edges represent atom to atom distances, which captures the system geometry. We explore how to do "small AI", providing a workflow of how to create a personal and convenient dataset of GW calculations to train the model that can be applied to different materials so that the final user does not need to rely on massive datasets. We define what must be included in the training dataset to capture the physics of our objective simulation, and perform a Bayesian optimization of hyperparameters. In the case of monolayer MoS$_2$ with a sulfur vacancy our GNN models achieve high accuracy (MAE ranging 10 to 50 meV) with a simplified workflow applicable to other materials, accelerating accurate large electronic structure calculations.

DeepDOSReasoner: Physics-Grounded Reasoning for Density-of-States Prediction and Materials Discovery

Yingheng Wang, Francesco Ricci, Zhilong Wang, Tao Yu, Junwen Bai, Xiangyun Lei

Predicting a material's behavior from its atomic structure is a longstanding goal of the physical sciences. The electronic and phonon densities of states (eDOS and phDOS) govern key electronic, optical, vibrational, and thermodynamic properties, yet their calculation using density functional theory (DFT) is costly: about 60% of Materials Project entries lack eDOS and over 80% lack phDOS. Existing crystal-to-spectrum models rely on pooled structural representations and pointwise regression, which blur spectral features and violate total-state conservation. Here we introduce DeepDOSReasoner, a physics-grounded framework that decodes spectra through atom-to-energy source attention and refines the normalized DOS through learned continuity-equation transport, enforcing nonnegativity and total-state conservation by construction. DeepDOSReasoner achieves the highest prediction accuracy across all evaluated metrics for both eDOS and phDOS, recovers element-resolved contributions without supervision, and most reliably identifies rare valence-band (VB) gaps. In a prospective screen of 8,106 uncharted materials validated by new DFT calculations, its predictions remain the most accurate among the methods evaluated, with 25 of its top 50 candidates confirmed as VB-gap materials. Three case studies spanning substituted compounds, photovoltaic chalcogenides, and a high-entropy alloy demonstrate transfer across materials classes. Ablations confirm that both stages are necessary, while the model retains comparable accuracy when trained on only 25% of the data. Predicting spectra in milliseconds enables exhaustive screening; we release predictions filling the missing eDOS for 91,405 and phDOS for 127,768 Materials Project entries. DeepDOSReasoner provides a blueprint for integrating machine learning with physical reasoning toward high-throughput spectral screening and autonomous materials discovery.

Strain-Induced Tetragonal-to-Cubic Transition in MAPbI$_3$ from Machine-Learned Molecular Dynamics

Daan Holleman, Luuk Klein Nagelvoort, Monica Morales-Masis, Linn Leppert

Metal-halide perovskites are promising photovoltaic absorbers whose optoelectronic properties are highly tunable. Epitaxial strain offers a route to stabilize desirable phases, tune band gaps, and reduce energetic disorder, but rational strain engineering requires predictive models. Static, zero-temperature "strained-bulk" density functional theory (DFT) calculations, successful for oxide perovskites, neglect the dynamic disorder and anharmonicity that govern halide perovskites at ambient temperature. Here, we show that molecular dynamics (MD) based on machine-learned force fields trained on-the-fly on DFT reproduces the structural and optoelectronic response of methylammonium (MA) lead iodide (MAPbI$_3$) to epitaxial strain at 300 K. We simulate bulk and free-standing slab models across experimentally accessible biaxial strains by locking the in-plane lattice vectors in nanosecond-long simulations. We predict a tetragonal-to-cubic transition at $\sim$0.5% tensile strain, marked by $c/a = 1$ and the loss of the $a^0a^0c^-$ tilting pattern, in agreement with experiments on epitaxial MAPbI$_3$ films on KCl. MA cations retain preferential orientations in the strain-induced cubic phase, unlike in its temperature-induced counterpart. Bulk and slab models yield consistent trends, with surface effects decaying within a few octahedral layers. Band gaps computed as ensemble averages over MD snapshots blue-shift under tensile strain, consistent with experiment, whereas static calculations on averaged structures predict a red shift. Our study positions MD with constrained lattice vectors as a method for predicting finite-temperature strain phase diagrams and guiding the epitaxial design of perovskite absorbers.

AdsNet: A Graph Neural Network for Direct Adsorption Energy Prediction

Changzhi Ai, Frank Abild-Pedersen, Johannes Voss

Fast and accurate prediction of adsorption energies is critical for high-throughput computational catalyst screening, existing methods compromise either on evaluation speed (full-graph machine learning potentials) or geometric sensitivity (coordination-based models like the alpha-scheme). Here, we present AdsNet, an equivariant graph neural network that directly predicts adsorption energies in a single forward pass, which operates on a local graph restricted only to adsorbate atoms and its immediate surface neighbors within a cutoff radius. This local graph formulation decouples computational complexity from total system size while encoding continuous geometric features (interatomic distances and directional vectors) enabling sensitivity to subtle variations in local bonding environments. AdsNet adopts a message-passing architecture with scalar and vector node features over multiple interaction layers. Trained on AdsTraj, a dataset of 33,920 density functional theory (DFT) calculated structures spanning 26 transition metals and 6 adsorbates (H*, C*, O*, CO*, OH*, HOCO*), the model achieves an adsorption energy root-mean-square error (RMSE) of 0.032 eV and a force RMSE of 0.047 eV/A. Analytic forces obtained via automatic differentiation enable structure relaxation through a projected gradient descent algorithm with adaptive line search, eliminating entire geometry optimizations. Additionally, AdsNet uniquely decomposes predicted adsorption energies into contributions from individual atoms within the local adsorbate graph - an interpretability feature unattainable with conventional machine-learning potentials. AdsNet thus establishes an accurate, highly efficient, and interpretable framework for large-scale adsorption energy prediction and local interface relaxation across various material surfaces.

Lattice Thermal Conductivity of MgO and the MgSiO$_3$ Polymorphs Bridgmanite and Post-Perovskite at Lower-Mantle Conditions Using Fine-Tuned MACE Potentials

Amrendra Kumar Tiwari, Sandro Jahn

Heat flow across the core-mantle boundary (CMB) depends on the lattice thermal conductivity ($κ_{\mathrm{latt}}$) of lowermost-mantle minerals, yet calculated CMB values for MgO differ severalfold, and high-temperature estimates of the conductivity increase across the bridgmanite-post-perovskite transition range from about 20 to 50%. Direct solutions of the linearized phonon Boltzmann transport equation (LBTE) require thousands of density functional theory (DFT) force evaluations per thermodynamic state. Here we replace these evaluations with phase-specific, fine-tuned MACE potentials for MgO, MgSiO3 bridgmanite (Pv), and MgSiO3 post-perovskite (PPv). The potentials reproduce DFT energies, forces, stresses, pressure-volume relations, and compressed-state phonons for all three phases. For MgO at 0 GPa, replacing MACE forces with DFT forces in otherwise identical transport calculations changes $κ_{\mathrm{latt}}$ by 0.2% at 300 K and 1.9% at 2000 K. PPv is 44-55% more conductive than Pv at the same pressure and temperature and strongly anisotropic, with $κ_{[100]}/κ_{[010]}$ ranging from 1.7 to 2.1. The quasi-harmonic phase boundary has a Clapeyron slope of $(7.4 \pm 0.1)$ MPa/K and intersects a model geotherm at 116.2 GPa and 2660 K, where the transition raises the silicate conductivity by 47%, to 8.6 W/(m K). Heating in the basal thermal boundary layer lowers the MgO and PPv conductivities to 37.3 and 6.7 W/(m K) at the CMB, where Hashin-Shtrikman bounds of 9.5-11.4 W/(m K) for an Fe-free aggregate of 80 vol% silicate and 20 vol% MgO encompass the 10 W/(m K) used in earlier heat-flow estimates. The density-temperature parameterizations provide an intrinsic end-member reference for lower-mantle thermal models.

Photocatalytic Water Splitting in GeC/ReS$_2$ van der Waals Heterostructures

Parisa Beyranvand, Ismaeil Abdolhosseini Sarsari, Noejung Park

Photocatalytic water splitting is considered as one of the most promising approaches for the green hydrogen production. But the rapid recombination of photogenerated electron-hole pairs severely inhibits its photocatalytic efficiency. Construction of van der Waals heterostructures is a good way to suppress this recombination. However, in staggered band heterostructures, the charge transfer could occur either through the type-II or the direct Z-scheme mechanism. Therefore it is very important to identify which mechanism is dominant. In the present work, we systematically study the GeC/ReS$_2$ heterostructure using first-principles density functional theory (DFT). The system exhibits good thermodynamic, dynamic and thermal stability and an indirect band gap of 1.38 eV which greatly enhances the visible-light absorption. We find that the direct Z-scheme mechanism is the dominant interfacial charge transfer by analysing band alignment and Gibbs free energy pathways. The oxygen evolution reaction (OER) can be driven spontaneously in the pH range $4 \le \text{pH} \le 14$ under light irradiation for both type-II and Z-scheme pathways but the energy barrier is much lower for the Z-scheme mechanism. Moreover, the type-II mechanism is not suitable for hydrogen evolution reaction (HER), but the Z-scheme mechanism can promote HER well in the entire pH range ($0 \le \text{pH} \le 14$). Remarkably, the strain-free (pristine) condition exhibits a solar-to-hydrogen (STH) efficiency of 41.40\%, well above the 10\% threshold for commercial viability. The STH efficiency is further enhanced to 60.68\% under a tensile strain of +6\%. To summarise, the Z-scheme strain-tunable GeC/ReS$_2$ heterostructure is a promising and efficient photocatalyst for overall water splitting.

Atomistic insight into defect-mediated boron redistribution and high-temperature strengthening in TiAl alloys

Xiang Meng, Shuhei Shinzato, Zhiqiang Zhao, Linfeng Yu, Jian Guo, Liangli Chu

Boron (B) microalloying can effectively refine microstructures of as-cast TiAl alloys and regulate their mechanical properties. Yet, how dilute B exists in TiAl and interacts with defects and how these interactions govern high-temperature deformation remain elusive. Here, we systematically elucidate defect-mediated B redistribution in TiAl alloys and its consequences for their deformation using hybrid grand-canonical Monte Carlo/molecular dynamics simulations augmented with a newly developed high-fidelity machine-learning potential. As a typical product of B microalloying, borides are markedly stiffer than the TiAl matrix and act as brittle reinforcing phases, while borides/matrix interfacial adhesion depends on surface termination and stacking sequence. In the bulk, interstitial B tends to be trapped at Ti-rich octahedral sites by high migration barriers but can kinetically evolve into substitutional dopants upon approaching Al vacancies. Increased tensile stress and vacancy concentration further promote B enrichment in the bulk. B is enriched at semi-coherent $γ/α_2$ boundaries and preferentially partitions into the $α_2$ phase. In polycrystalline TiAl, B accumulates primarily in disordered regions, raising the average flow stress by 9.4% at 1 at.% B and 900 K. This strengthening is accompanied by reduced shear strain accumulation. Simulated microstructural evolution and stacking-fault energetics indicate that B impedes dislocation glide via local pinning and suppresses deformation twinning, thereby increasing resistance to plastic deformation. By linking solute redistribution to defect interactions and deformation, these findings establish an atomistic framework for understanding and designing high-performance alloys based on interstitial solutes.

Diagnostic Accuracy Is Not Deployment Value: Decision-Aligned Reliability Evaluation for Foundation MLIPs in Materials Discovery

Harry Sevi, Joonatan E. M. Laulainen

In AI-guided materials discovery, machine-learning models increasingly decide which candidate materials should receive expensive first-principles or experimental evaluation. Standard reliability metrics such as uncertainty, calibration, novelty, and ranking accuracy evaluate properties of the predictions, but success on these metrics does not necessarily imply a better downstream screening decision. We study this mismatch for foundation machine-learned interatomic potentials (MACE-MP, CHGNet, Orb V3) used to rank candidate crystal structures before DFT verification. We evaluate three representative reliability interventions (novelty detection, sparse DFT calibration, and chemistry-level ranking audits) using a deployment objective that measures how much DFT can be avoided while maintaining a required recall of near-hull candidates. Across all three, a reliability signal can succeed on its immediate target without improving the final screening decision: novelty detects distribution shift but weakly ranks failure severity; calibration can improve pooled ranking metrics while leaving screening unchanged or degrading it; and sparse audits identify MLIP-chemistry combinations with poor ranking reliability without yielding a robustly beneficial routing policy. A two-oracle analysis further shows that the source of failure depends on the screening decision itself: under formation-energy screening, even perfect knowledge of average ranking reliability can select the wrong model, whereas in the evaluated hull-aligned variant the oracle target becomes decision-aligned but finite-budget audits still fail to realize the value. Reliability should be evaluated relative to the decision it supports: the relevant question is not whether a model is uncertain or inaccurate, but whether acquiring and acting on additional information will improve the final discovery decision enough to justify its cost.

Native vacancy energetics and association in TiN and CrN: chemical potential dependence, clustering, and surface effects

Barsha Bhattacharjee, Emilia Olsson

Native vacancy populations in transition-metal nitride coatings reflect vacancy formation, association, and depth-dependent stability. Using density functional theory (DFT), we compare these effects in rocksalt TiN and CrN in bulk and at (001) surfaces, within phase-stability-constrained chemical potential ranges. In TiN, $V_\mathrm{N}$ is favoured under N-poor and $V_\mathrm{Ti}$ under N-rich conditions; in CrN, $V_\mathrm{N}$ remains preferred throughout its stability range. Association energies are referenced to corresponding isolated vacancies where positive values mean association lowers the energy and negative values favour separation. Mean bulk association energies for mixed metal-nitrogen pairs are $+0.132$ eV in TiN and $+1.053$ eV in CrN. At (001) surfaces, $V_\mathrm{N}$ in TiN prefers the surface and $V_\mathrm{Ti}$ the first subsurface layer. In CrN, $V_\mathrm{N}$ has similar formation energies at the surface and first subsurface layer, with a slight preference for the latter; $V_\mathrm{Cr}$ slightly prefers the second subsurface layer. Surface cluster energies are referenced to separated vacancies at their preferred depths. The compact $2V_\mathrm{Ti}+2V_\mathrm{N}$ cluster in TiN has favourable association in bulk ($+0.217$ eV per vacancy) but unfavourable association at the surface ($-0.346$ eV per vacancy). By contrast, $2V_\mathrm{Cr}+2V_\mathrm{N}$ in CrN remains stabilised, with its association energy decreasing from $+1.188$ to $+0.431$ eV per vacancy. CrN's stronger association occurs without larger structural distortions and is accompanied by distinct Cr-centred magnetic responses.

A macroscopic-atom spectral theory of grain-boundary segregation in multicomponent alloys

Dennis Boakye, Chuang Deng

Predicting grain-boundary segregation in multicomponent alloys remains challenging because existing spectral approaches require alloy-specific atomistic calculations to resolve segregation-energy distributions and correlations across non-equivalent boundary sites. Here, we develop a transferable macroscopic-atom spectral theory that predicts the joint segregation behavior of multiple species from physically interpretable elemental descriptors. The theory is founded on the finding that local strain and bond deficit account for 91\% of the site-to-site co-variation of 40 solutes in a density-functional-theory (DFT) Al polycrystal. Linked to atomic size and surface enthalpy, these coordinates allow prediction of individual segregation spectra and their cross-species correlations, which determine whether species compete for or share grain-boundary sites. A closed-system formulation unifies host-element redistribution and dilute-solute segregation, couples metallic and light-element sublattices, and accounts for bulk compound formation and solute pairing. Calibrated using binary spectra only, the theory predicts unseen-host spectral means with a correlation coefficient of 0.81, outperforms embedded-atom spectra against DFT benchmarks, and ranks site competition across 870 solute pairs with a Spearman coefficient of 0.90. Without fitting to multicomponent observations, it reproduces 11 of 12 equilibrium Fe ternaries and 49 of 55 Mg ternaries. Predictions recorded before simulations of an 82,699-atom polycrystal identify site competition as the baseline behavior and metal-metalloid pair chemistry as a source of deviations requiring atomistic resolution. These results establish a physically grounded route to screening grain-boundary chemistry in multicomponent alloys, including steels and high-entropy alloys.

Mixing and Preserving Properties of Non-van der Waals 2D Materials in Sandwich-like Heterostructures

Anastasiia Nihei, Rico Friedrich

Heterostructure interfaces created by stacking two-dimensional (2D) materials outline an appealing route towards achieving advanced electronic and magnetic functionalities at the nanoscale. Non-van der Waals (non-vdW) sandwich-like heterostructures (HSs) are build out of non-vdW 2D materials derived from non-layered crystals and establish a new frontier enabling strong interfacial coupling. Employing ab initio density functional theory (DFT), we investigate two classes of such trilayer HSs: purely non-vdW assemblies and hybrid vdW/non-vdW/vdW stacks. We show that non-vdW sandwiches undergo pronounced geometric reconstruction forming chemically bonded interfaces with large absolute binding energies up to an order of magnitude larger than those of conventional vdW HSs. These strong interactions drive extensive electronic hybridization, band gap renormalization, and tunable interlayer magnetic coupling. In contrast, vdW encapsulation largely protects the intrinsic atomic and electronic structure of the non-vdW sheet from the environment , while enabling charge transfer doping of the vdW layers. Our results thus establish sandwich HSs a versatile interface design platform, spanning interaction regimes from weakly perturbed encapsulation to strongly interacting chemically bonded systems with emergent electronic and magnetic behavior.

MatHarness couples three mechanisms: execution robustness through pre-flight verification, expert-distilled skills, and just-in-time auditing; epistemic adaptability through persistent research artifacts and an evolving scientific mental model; and collective intelligence through a heterogeneous multi-agent round table under targeted human oversight.
