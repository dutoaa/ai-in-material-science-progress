PaddleMaterials: An End-to-End Deep Learning Toolkit for Materials Science

Xuwei Liu, Zhimin Zhang, Xiaoguang Hu, Zhongwei Liu, Yuanhui Sun, Jun Huo

Machine learning supports materials design, atomistic simulation and experimental characterization, yet models and workflows remain fragmented. We present PaddleMaterials, an open-source framework integrating heterogeneous materials-learning tasks while preserving task-specific representations and scientific semantics. It unifies data processing, training, evaluation, pretrained-model management and workflows across six task classes: property prediction, structure generation, machine-learning interatomic potentials, electronic-structure fields, spectrum elucidation and microscopy image processing. Task interfaces record physical quantities, units, aggregation conventions and references, making cross-task composition verifiable. A shared compiler backend enables switching from dynamic-graph to compiled execution through one setting, using graph optimization, operator fusion and hardware-targeted code generation without changing model definitions. Across 65 model instances in 11 groups, compiled execution accelerated every instance in eight groups, with group median speedups of 11%-224% and group maximum speedups of 11%-336%. Case studies on LiMnO2 and toluene link structure generation or spectrum elucidation, interatomic-potential relaxation, property prediction and electron-density prediction, using same-geometry density functional theory (DFT) calculations as references. For LiMnO2, predicted formation energies for generated and relaxed structures differed from same-geometry PBE+U references by at most 7 meV per atom, while the computational cost of model steps was less than one-twentieth of a DFT single-point calculation. PaddleMaterials extends unified materials workflows beyond conventional property, potential and structure-generation tasks while supporting verifiable cross-task operations and compiled execution.

Towards Multiscale Molecular Representation Learning through Geometry-Based Fragmentation for Polymer Property Prediction

Makoto Takamoto, Chaitanya Sampara, Viktor Zaverkin, Daniel Oñoro-Rubio, Mathias Niepert

It is well established that molecular substructures can contribute unequally to chemical and physical properties, with specific motifs or side chains often playing dominant roles. Fragment-based representations have therefore emerged as an effective strategy for incorporating chemically meaningful inductive biases, as exemplified by molecular fingerprints and fragment-based attention mechanisms. However, many existing fragmentation-based machine learning methods rely on predefined chemical rules, which may yield suboptimal fragmentation for previously unseen or chemically unfamiliar structures and often overlook the importance of multiscale molecular representations. To address these limitations, we formulate molecular fragmentation as a radius-constrained optimization problem inspired by the k-center formulation, where the number of clusters is not fixed a priori but emerges from the specified radius, and propose a geometry-based fragmentation method that partitions arbitrary molecular or material structures using largely geometric information, without relying on predefined chemical fragmentation rules. The method provides flexible control over fragment size, enabling the construction of multiscale fragment representations for scale-adaptive modeling. Building on this framework, we develop a geometric fragment-based attention mechanism that integrates multiscale structural information and incorporate it into a multimodal machine learning model combining p-SMILES representations with three-dimensional (3D) conformational information. Experiments on a benchmark polymer dataset show that incorporating 3D conformational information improves prediction accuracy for several properties over models based exclusively on p-SMILES representations. Explainable artificial intelligence analyses further provide insights into the physical relevance of the learned representations.

SteelAgent: LLM-Orchestrated, Physics-Informed Steel Property Prediction with Audited Generalization and Calibrated Uncertainty

Aleksandr Volkov, Roman Sultimov, Mikhail Kuzin, Yury Maximov

Steel property models can perform well on random test splits even when they extrapolate poorly to unfamiliar alloys. SteelAgent couples metallurgical descriptors and machine-learning models with a language-model interface for property prediction, uncertainty reporting and inverse design. We examine how the choice of held-out unit changes the conclusions drawn from SteelBench tensile-strength evaluations. For gradient boosting, pooled out-of-fold $R^2$ fell from 0.859 on a random split to 0.113 when steel families were held out. Empirical coverage of nominal 90% prediction intervals ranged from 91.0% under random splitting to 37.6% on held-out sources. The effect of physics-derived features depended on the evaluation. They increased gradient-boosting $R^2$ by 0.2534 in a 17-family holdout analysis and decreased it by 0.358 in a separate four-grade laboratory analysis. We report group-level errors and a synthetic regression experiment illustrating how calibration can deteriorate on held-out groups. These results motivate reporting the evaluation population alongside a property estimate or compliance score.

Spectral symmetry fingerprints in equivariant representations

Jiacheng Cen, Anyi Li, Ning Lin, Zihe Wang, Ji-Rong Wen, Wenbing Huang

Symmetry governs the organization of physical structures, while symmetry breaking reveals their transformations. In geometric deep learning, symmetry imposes rank constraints on equivariant representations, limiting the expressive capacity of equivariant graph neural networks (GNNs). Here we show that these constraints can instead be exploited: the singular-value spectra of equivariant representations provide a unified fingerprint of structural symmetry. We introduce spectral symmetry fingerprints (SymFP), which enable discrete point-group identification through rank-template matching and continuous symmetry analysis by quantifying spectral energy beyond target rank constraints. SymFP is orientation invariant and architecture agnostic, enabling a parameter-free GNN implementation. Its spectra can be reused across multiple target groups, and the resulting leakage scores satisfy subgroup monotonicity. Across synthetic structures, molecules, coordination complexes and large biological assemblies, SymFP accurately identifies point groups, tracks controlled symmetry breaking and reveals local and degree-resolved symmetry changes that can be obscured by global geometric measures. These results establish the spectral structure of equivariant representations as a general framework for characterizing symmetry across physical systems.

An All-Atom Model of the Resorcinol-Formaldehyde Aerogel Skeleton from DFT and Molecular Dynamics: Validation and Iron Incorporation

Shoeb Athar, Jérémy Guazzagaloppa, Philippe Jund

Resorcinol-formaldehyde (RF) aerogels are lightweight, ultra-porous thermal superinsulators whose complex amorphous structure has not yet been described at the all-atom level. Here, we build an all-atom model of the RF aerogel skeleton by combining density functional theory (DFT) and classical molecular dynamics (MD), validate it against experimental infrared and density measurements, and use it to study the incorporation of iron dopants. The RF base unit was optimized with Gaussian, DMol$^{3}$ and SIESTA, and its calculated infrared spectrum agreed with experiment. Comparing classical force fields with DFT for branched systems of two to four base units (140-278 atoms) led to the choice of COMPASS II. A 15,012-atom system of 54 branched RF units, not cross-linked to each other, aggregated into a single cluster in the canonical (NVT) ensemble and reached an equilibrium density of 1.296 g.cm$^{-3}$ in the isobaric-isothermal (NPT) ensemble, in agreement with the experimental skeletal density (1.3 g.cm$^{-3}$). Then, 5 wt$\%$ iron (109 atoms) was added to the equilibrated structure: during 1 ns of NPT simulation, the fraction of iron atoms embedded in the skeleton rose from 63$\%$ to 71$\%$, and the Fe-C radial distribution function peaked at 3 Å. The model thus provides an experimentally supported starting point for screening fillers and dopants in RF gels, with prospective application to their thermoelectric functionalization, which we have previously demonstrated experimentally with basalt and PANOX fibres. Future extensions could address skeleton properties relevant to thermal insulation and the pyrolysis of RF gels into carbon and metal-doped carbon aerogels.

Site-orbit masked twin-branch Transformer for symmetry-constrained crystal generation

Junchi Liu, Haoran Si, Luo Yan, Sergei Tretiak, Liujiang Zhou

Generative models for crystal structure prediction (CSP) have expanded the search space for novel materials and improved screening efficiency. However, simultaneously generating stable and novel structures remains challenging because of the complex structural and physical constraints governing crystals. To address this challenge, we propose site-orbit masked twin-branch Transformer model (ST2M), a symmetry-guided discrete--continuous diffusion framework incorporating degree-of-freedom masks and conditioned on Wyckoff patterns. ST2M represents crystal structures as sequences of independent occupied Wyckoff orbits and employs a twin-branch Transformer to jointly denoise discrete and continuous variables. In our experiments, ST2M achieves a structural validity of 96.37\%. For known patterns in dataset, 23.86\% of generated structures simultaneously satisfy stability, uniqueness, and novelty criteria, representing a relative improvement of 68.15\% over the best-performing baseline. First-principles calculations identify a total of 21 previously unreported stable structures among selected candidates with low predicted energies. These results demonstrate that ST2M can generate chemically viable crystal candidates under prescribed crystallographic constraints and generalize to unknown Wyckoff patterns, providing a practical route to accelerated materials discovery.

PolyGPT: A Decoder-Only Transformer for Polymer Electrolyte Design

Tanmay Sarkar Akash, Siddhartha Das

Designing polymer electrolytes requires control over both material properties and chemical structure. Here, we present PolyGPT, a Transformer with 38.3 million parameters that generates polymer repeat units from optional property targets, requests to include or exclude a specified chemical feature, and an electrolyte-domain condition. Using the model selected by validation loss, we compared unconditional generation with requests for electrolyte-associated structures, a low glass-transition temperature (Tg), or both. Unconditional and electrolyte-only generation achieved structural validity of 99.46% and 99.59%, respectively. However, electrolyte conditioning increased repetition: uniqueness among valid outputs fell from 99.99% to 51.74%. At a Tg target of 246.11 K, we compared generation with and without electrolyte conditioning, and adding the electrolyte condition slightly reduced the predictor-estimated mean absolute error from 26.97 to 26.07 K, but the fraction of all attempts yielding distinct valid polymers within 50 K of the target fell from 82.35% to 32.31%. When we also required absence from the training catalog, these yields were 59.90% and 19.71%, respectively. The lower yield persisted with a tighter 25 K tolerance. Domain conditioning also weakened control of inclusion requests: the mean fraction of attempts producing valid polymers containing the requested chemical feature fell from 94.27% to 31.27%. Across nine checkpoints, earlier models sometimes produced more distinct, catalog-unseen polymers meeting the Tg target: 10,000 checkpoint model with electrolyte conditioning and 30,000 checkpoint model without it. Together, these results show why property error alone is insufficient for evaluating conditional polymer generation. It must be considered alongside repetition, training-catalog novelty, and whether combined design requests are satisfied.

Thermally Conditioned Time-Series Forecasting of Grain Growth in Polycrystalline Materials

Eliane Younes, Elie Hachem, Marc Bernacki

Grain growth during heat treatment strongly affects the microstructure of polycrystalline materials and, consequently, their mechanical properties. Although numerical methods such as front-tracking or front-capturing simulations can accurately describe grain growth, their high computational cost limits their use for long or non-isothermal thermal cycles. In previous work, grain growth was successfully predicted under isothermal conditions by treating grain size distributions as time series and forecasting their evolution using recurrent neural networks, allowing complete grain growth trajectories to be generated in seconds rather than through costly PDE-based simulations. In the present work, this forecasting framework is reformulated to handle non-isothermal conditions by explicitly incorporating thermal information into the model architecture. Grain size distributions are encoded using a convolutional neural network, thermal descriptors are embedded through a dedicated multilayer perceptron, and temporal evolution is predicted using a recurrent network. Instantaneous temperature and thermal rate are provided at each time step, allowing the model to adapt its predictions to changing thermal conditions. The framework is trained and evaluated on datasets generated from validated front-tracking simulations across a wide range of non-isothermal thermal paths. The results show that the thermally conditioned model accurately reproduces grain growth dynamics under complex thermal histories, achieving, in k-fold cross-validation over all simulations, mean relative errors of 2.99% (one-hour cycles) and 4.51% (four-hour cycles) with coefficients of determination exceeding 0.99, and mean relative errors between 1.91% and 14.66% on ten simulations following four unseen thermal histories of increasing complexity.

Evolutionary Design of Interpretable Spatial Descriptors for Structure-Property Relationship Discovery

Aditya Raghavan, Sergei V Kalinin

Structure property prediction is a classical task in machine learning, ranging from molecular activity and materials properties to local functional responses measured in microscopy. For image-based regression, two choices define the information available to the predictor: the size of the image neighborhood and the spatial resolution retained within it. A small neighborhood can omit relevant surrounding structure, whereas a large, finely sampled neighborhood can introduce redundant or irrelevant information. Here, we develop a framework for constructing spatial descriptors for prediction of local spectra and hysteresis loops. We benchmark regression models, use SHAP maps to examine their spatial attribution, and explore the effect of the patch size and compression to establish the relationship between predictive accuracy and descriptor dimensionality. We then use a genetic algorithm to optimize spatial partitions and the assignment of mean, minimum, and maximum pooling operations. Each retained region contributes an explicit measurement of the image, allowing the search to balance prediction error against the number of descriptor values. Cartesian and polar constructions provide complementary ways to organize this information. The resulting descriptors make it possible to examine where spatial detail is needed, where regional summaries are sufficient, and how these choices depend on the predictor and the measured response. The same approach can be used for molecules and solids once the genetic algorithm rules for these are established.

Equivariant Deep Learning in Direct-Product Space for Electronic-Structure Hamiltonian Prediction

Shi Yin, Zujian Dai, Xinyang Pan, Lixin He

Most existing equivariant architectures for Hamiltonian prediction propagate features in direct-sum spaces of irreducible spherical tensors, whereas the target Hamiltonian is naturally an operator block in a direct-product representation over local orbital and spin bases. This representation mismatch fragments contiguous Hamiltonian blocks, obscures direct manipulation of orbital couplings, and organizes features into irregular angular-momentum components that require numerous transformations and are poorly aligned with efficient hardware execution and memory management. To address both the representation mismatch and computational bottleneck, we propose DP-Learner, an equivariant learning framework that performs message passing directly on Hamiltonian-like operator blocks in the direct-product space. Theoretically, we propose Lie-generator-based directional embedding and invariantly weighted left--right products and contractions that preserve equivariance to SO(3), SU(2), and spatial inversions, without explicit Clebsch--Gordan decomposition or inverse reconstruction. Methodologically, we instantiate this principle as a Transformer architecture, in which Hamiltonian-like feature matrices or tensors evolve through equivariant operators in the direct-product space, while non-linear invariant scalar routing controls structured gating over matrix units, directional propagation, attention, and environment-embedded context interactions. We further construct MP-HAM-110K, a large-scale Hamiltonian dataset comprising more than 110,000 material structures covering 68 elements with both Hamiltonian and wavefunction data. Experiments on MP-HAM-110K and Materials-HAM-SOC demonstrate that DP-Learner dramatically improves Hamiltonian prediction accuracy and inference efficiency over the state-of-the-art method.

Complex Martensitic Microstructures in ${\rm Ti}_{76}{\rm Nb}_{22}{\rm Al}_{2}$

John M. Ball, Francesco Della Porta, Tomonari Inamura

We analyze the unusual microstructure resulting from the cubic to orthorhombic phase transformation in the shape-memory alloy ${\rm Ti}_{76}{\rm Nb}_{22}{\rm Al}_{2}$. This seems to represent an approximate experimental realization of a deformation having a finite number of incompatible deformation gradients, which has been known to exist only as a theoretical possibility. Because the middle eigenvalue $γ$ of the transformation strain is very close to 1, the phase transformation in this alloy takes place via nucleation of exact austenite-martensite interfaces rather than, as usual for other alloys, by classical austenite-martensite interfaces separating the austenite from a finely-twinned laminate of martensite. According to the moving mask approximation of Della Porta this leads to the question whether a microstructure can be formed from the set $H$ of the twelve matrices ${\mathbf V}_i$ on the martensite energy wells that are rank-one connected to the identity, even though these matrices are mutually incompatible. However there is no deformation satisfying the same boundary condition whose gradient belongs to $H$ almost everywhere. An additional prediction, confirmed experimentally, is that the macroscopic deformation gradient equals the identity everywhere. We also analyse the approximate compatibility of the ${\mathbf V}_i$. Although mutually incompatible, they are nearly compatible, with some pairs much more nearly compatible than others, these pairs corresponding to interfaces that are more frequently observed.
