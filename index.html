Generative Design and Graph Neural Network Screening of Novel Bimetallic Alloy Catalysts for the Hydrogen Evolution Reaction

Vipin Kurian Elavunkel, Sylvain Le Tonquesse, Hadrien Vroylandt

Identifying earth-abundant catalysts for the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) is a central challenge in scalable green hydrogen production. We present a computational pipeline that couples diffusion-based generative modelling, machine-learning force-field relaxation, and graph neural network (GNN) property prediction to discover bimetallic HER catalysts beyond the structures and stoichiometries covered by existing adsorption databases. A generative model (MatterGen) is fine-tuned on bulk alloy structures labelled with their minimum hydrogen adsorption energy and steered towards adsorption energies near zero. From the generated pool we retain the stable structures that are novel bimetallic alloys: binary compositions built from the training-set metals but at stoichiometries or structures absent from that database. Each candidate's low-index facets are ranked by a machine-learning-force-field (MatterSim) surface-energy screen, and only the most stable terminations are converted into explicit slab models with hydrogen adsorbed at all high-symmetry surface sites and relaxed with the same force field. Two GNN surrogates (MEGNet and ALIGNN), trained on a high-throughput density functional theory (DFT) dataset, then screen the resulting slab candidates. Because the two models encode the local environment through different inductive biases, we retain only the alloys on which both agree, reducing the influence of any single model. This consensus screening, combined with a MatterSim energy-difference triangulation, resulted in a shortlist of near-thermoneutral bimetallic candidates. Eight are validated with plane-wave DFT, and four of them, Pd5Au, Pt5Rh, Cu5Pt3, and Pd13Ru3, bind hydrogen within the thermoneutral window. All eight alloys are dynamically stable by phonon calculations. These four alloys are highlighted as DFT-validated bimetallic HER candidates for experimental follow-up.

PaddleMaterials: An End-to-End Deep Learning Toolkit for Materials Science

Xuwei Liu, Zhimin Zhang, Xiaoguang Hu, Zhongwei Liu, Yuanhui Sun, Jun Huo

Machine learning supports materials design, atomistic simulation and experimental characterization, yet models and workflows remain fragmented. We present PaddleMaterials, an open-source framework integrating heterogeneous materials-learning tasks while preserving task-specific representations and scientific semantics. It unifies data processing, training, evaluation, pretrained-model management and workflows across six task classes: property prediction, structure generation, machine-learning interatomic potentials, electronic-structure fields, spectrum elucidation and microscopy image processing. Task interfaces record physical quantities, units, aggregation conventions and references, making cross-task composition verifiable. A shared compiler backend enables switching from dynamic-graph to compiled execution through one setting, using graph optimization, operator fusion and hardware-targeted code generation without changing model definitions. Across 65 model instances in 11 groups, compiled execution accelerated every instance in eight groups, with group median speedups of 11%-224% and group maximum speedups of 11%-336%. Case studies on LiMnO2 and toluene link structure generation or spectrum elucidation, interatomic-potential relaxation, property prediction and electron-density prediction, using same-geometry density functional theory (DFT) calculations as references. For LiMnO2, predicted formation energies for generated and relaxed structures differed from same-geometry PBE+U references by at most 7 meV per atom, while the computational cost of model steps was less than one-twentieth of a DFT single-point calculation. PaddleMaterials extends unified materials workflows beyond conventional property, potential and structure-generation tasks while supporting verifiable cross-task operations and compiled execution.

Quantifying out-of-distribution data severity with Wasserstein distances: benchmarking large language models for alloy property prediction

YuJie Liu, Dezhen Xue, Jun Sun, Xiangdong Ding, Zhibin Gao, Turab Lookman

As general-purpose Large Language Models (LLMs), such as Gemini and DeepSeek, exhibit increasingly advanced reasoning capabilities, a critical question arises: how do their property prediction abilities compare to those of domain-specific pretrained materials models? In this study, we evaluate predictions for Ti, steels, high-entropy alloy (HEA) and Al, demonstrating that general-purpose LLMs surprisingly outperform SteelBERT, MatSciBERT, and SciBERT. To further enhance performance, we introduce a hybrid approach that utilizes numerical priors from a prior model, such as CatBoost or SteelBERT, to guide the general LLMs (Deepseek V3.1, Gemini-2.5-Flash). By inputting these initial estimates from a prior model as prompts, the LLM acts as a reasoning corrector, achieving accuracy superior to the prior model and "unguided mode". Furthermore, we investigate the impact of data strategy using a similarity-based metric, revealing that data quality dominates quantity; using merely 5% of high-similarity reference data of training set as input yields performance similar to the results of using 100% of training set. Additionally, the LLMs demonstrate excellent uncertainty calibration, where self-reported "high" confidence strongly correlates with minimal deviation from ground truth. Our findings suggest that synergizing general-purpose LLMs with prior model numerical baselines and high-quality data curation creates a cost-effective and accurate paradigm for materials discovery, reducing the need for expensive domain-specific pretraining.

An All-Atom Model of the Resorcinol-Formaldehyde Aerogel Skeleton from DFT and Molecular Dynamics: Validation and Iron Incorporation

Shoeb Athar, Jérémy Guazzagaloppa, Philippe Jund

Resorcinol-formaldehyde (RF) aerogels are lightweight, ultra-porous thermal superinsulators whose complex amorphous structure has not yet been described at the all-atom level. Here, we build an all-atom model of the RF aerogel skeleton by combining density functional theory (DFT) and classical molecular dynamics (MD), validate it against experimental infrared and density measurements, and use it to study the incorporation of iron dopants. The RF base unit was optimized with Gaussian, DMol$^{3}$ and SIESTA, and its calculated infrared spectrum agreed with experiment. Comparing classical force fields with DFT for branched systems of two to four base units (140-278 atoms) led to the choice of COMPASS II. A 15,012-atom system of 54 branched RF units, not cross-linked to each other, aggregated into a single cluster in the canonical (NVT) ensemble and reached an equilibrium density of 1.296 g.cm$^{-3}$ in the isobaric-isothermal (NPT) ensemble, in agreement with the experimental skeletal density (1.3 g.cm$^{-3}$). Then, 5 wt$\%$ iron (109 atoms) was added to the equilibrated structure: during 1 ns of NPT simulation, the fraction of iron atoms embedded in the skeleton rose from 63$\%$ to 71$\%$, and the Fe-C radial distribution function peaked at 3 Å. The model thus provides an experimentally supported starting point for screening fillers and dopants in RF gels, with prospective application to their thermoelectric functionalization, which we have previously demonstrated experimentally with basalt and PANOX fibres. Future extensions could address skeleton properties relevant to thermal insulation and the pyrolysis of RF gels into carbon and metal-doped carbon aerogels.

Site-orbit masked twin-branch Transformer for symmetry-constrained crystal generation

Junchi Liu, Haoran Si, Luo Yan, Sergei Tretiak, Liujiang Zhou

Generative models for crystal structure prediction (CSP) have expanded the search space for novel materials and improved screening efficiency. However, simultaneously generating stable and novel structures remains challenging because of the complex structural and physical constraints governing crystals. To address this challenge, we propose site-orbit masked twin-branch Transformer model (ST2M), a symmetry-guided discrete--continuous diffusion framework incorporating degree-of-freedom masks and conditioned on Wyckoff patterns. ST2M represents crystal structures as sequences of independent occupied Wyckoff orbits and employs a twin-branch Transformer to jointly denoise discrete and continuous variables. In our experiments, ST2M achieves a structural validity of 96.37\%. For known patterns in dataset, 23.86\% of generated structures simultaneously satisfy stability, uniqueness, and novelty criteria, representing a relative improvement of 68.15\% over the best-performing baseline. First-principles calculations identify a total of 21 previously unreported stable structures among selected candidates with low predicted energies. These results demonstrate that ST2M can generate chemically viable crystal candidates under prescribed crystallographic constraints and generalize to unknown Wyckoff patterns, providing a practical route to accelerated materials discovery.

Atomistic Insights into MOCVD Growth of GaAs using a Closed Loop Autonomous System Combining Directed Reinforcement Learning and Kinetic Monte Carlo Simulations

Hrishi V. Harsora, Ashutosh Krishna Amaram, Raghavan Ranganathan, Tarun K. Agarwal

Conventional process optimization of the Metal-Organic Chemical Vapor Deposition (MOCVD) growth parameters to achieve high-quality thin films relies on iterative, resource-intensive experimental runs. While predictive modeling can circumvent this cost and effort, simulating thin-film growth is constrained by the vastly different length and time scales of the deposition process. While continuum (e.g., Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)) and first-principles methods (e.g., Molecular Dynamics (MD)) struggle to balance experimental length/time scales and atomistic accuracy, Kinetic Monte Carlo (KMC) bridges this divide by resolving atomistic events whilst remaining tractable at experimental length and time scales. However, the accuracy of KMC relies on kinetic rate parameters that require costly density functional theory (DFT) calculations. In order to circumvent this, we present a framework that calibrates the rate parameters directly against experimental growth data. A forward KMC growth model is coupled in a closed loop with a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) optimizer that navigates the high-dimensional parameter space, with the search steered by the degree of rate control of each reaction rate parameter. The approach invokes no material-specific assumptions and hence, any deposition process whose forward kinetics can be reduced to a set of rate-controlled events is amenable to the same treatment. We demonstrate it for the MOCVD of GaAs, where the calibrated model captures the characteristic temperature dependence of the measured growth rate. The framework is also able to recover the three well known kinetic regimes characterized by change in the rate determining step from precursor chemisorption to desorption. As a result, the framework converts routinely measured growth data into a mechanistically interpretable model of the deposition process.

Triboelectric nanogenerators across length scales: linking materials, multiscale modelling and applications

MD Tanzib Ehsan Sanglap, Ans Al Rashid, Abdullah Sayam, Injamamul Arief, Andrei G. Shvarts, Lukasz Kaczmarczyk

Triboelectric nanogenerators (TENGs) convert low-frequency mechanical energy into electricity and enable self-powered sensing. Their performance depends on interactions among material properties, surface morphology, contact mechanics, charge generation and external circuits. This review examines these interactions across length scales to assess how material and interface descriptions support predictive device design. Polymeric, inorganic, composite and natural materials are discussed in relation to charge generation and retention, mechanical compliance, durability and application requirements. Modelling approaches span molecular dynamics and first-principles calculations, rough-surface contact mechanics, device-scale electrostatics and equivalent circuits. Their capabilities are assessed through the inputs they require, the quantities they predict and the experimental evidence used for validation. Particular attention is given to prescribed surface charge density, idealised geometries and nominal contact-area assumptions, which constrain predictions under realistic loading conditions. Applications are organised by macroscale, milliscale and microscale device dimensions to examine requirements for materials, architecture and integration. The review identifies evolving real contact area and contact-dependent charge generation and decay as critical links between interfacial behaviour and electrical output. A staged validation strategy is proposed for contact mechanics, electrostatics and circuit response, with independent testing beyond calibration conditions. These priorities guide the development of multiscale models and reliable TENG systems.

Agentic inverse design of spatially programmed materials

Denisse Córdova Carrizales, Abhijatmedhi Chotrattanapituk, L. Dagny Davis, Jeremy Ramsey, Ryotaro Okabe, Mouyang Cheng

Spatially programmed functional materials, from functionally graded alloys and metamaterials to quantum superlattices, use their spatial structure to tailor physical properties. However, translating a desired spatial structure into a practical fabrication recipe remains challenging due to the complex relationship between processing conditions and resulting material structures. Here we show that a large language model (LLM) agent can inverse-design such fabrication recipes by using a physics-based simulator of ion-matter interactions as a tool. Given an arbitrary depth profile of implanted ions or vacancies, the agent selects the ion species, energies, incident angles, and target material layers; writes, runs, and interprets simulations; and iteratively refines the recipe until the simulated profile matches the target, with minimal human input. Our work demonstrates physics-guided agentic inverse design as a practical approach to making the spatial structure of materials a programmable variable, opening new opportunities to explore emergent physical phenomena and functional phases of matter.

Complex Martensitic Microstructures in ${\rm Ti}_{76}{\rm Nb}_{22}{\rm Al}_{2}$

John M. Ball, Francesco Della Porta, Tomonari Inamura

We analyze the unusual microstructure resulting from the cubic to orthorhombic phase transformation in the shape-memory alloy ${\rm Ti}_{76}{\rm Nb}_{22}{\rm Al}_{2}$. This seems to represent an approximate experimental realization of a deformation having a finite number of incompatible deformation gradients, which has been known to exist only as a theoretical possibility. Because the middle eigenvalue $γ$ of the transformation strain is very close to 1, the phase transformation in this alloy takes place via nucleation of exact austenite-martensite interfaces rather than, as usual for other alloys, by classical austenite-martensite interfaces separating the austenite from a finely-twinned laminate of martensite. According to the moving mask approximation of Della Porta this leads to the question whether a microstructure can be formed from the set $H$ of the twelve matrices ${\mathbf V}_i$ on the martensite energy wells that are rank-one connected to the identity, even though these matrices are mutually incompatible. We show that when $γ=1$ and the transformation is volume preserving, such a microstructure is possible that does not deform the grain boundary. This is a consequence of $H$ forming a degenerate $T_{12}$ configuration in the sense of Kirchheim. In fact there are such microstructures supported on just 8 of the 12 matrices in $H$. However there is no deformation satisfying the same boundary condition whose gradient belongs to $H$ almost everywhere. An additional prediction, confirmed experimentally, is that the macroscopic deformation gradient equals the identity everywhere. We also analyse the approximate compatibility of the ${\mathbf V}_i$. Although mutually incompatible, they are nearly compatible, with some pairs much more nearly compatible than others, these pairs corresponding to interfaces that are more frequently observed.

Thermally Conditioned Time-Series Forecasting of Grain Growth in Polycrystalline Materials

Eliane Younes, Elie Hachem, Marc Bernacki

Grain growth during heat treatment strongly affects the microstructure of polycrystalline materials and, consequently, their mechanical properties. Although numerical methods such as front-tracking or front-capturing simulations can accurately describe grain growth, their high computational cost limits their use for long or non-isothermal thermal cycles. In previous work, grain growth was successfully predicted under isothermal conditions by treating grain size distributions as time series and forecasting their evolution using recurrent neural networks, allowing complete grain growth trajectories to be generated in seconds rather than through costly PDE-based simulations. In the present work, this forecasting framework is reformulated to handle non-isothermal conditions by explicitly incorporating thermal information into the model architecture. Grain size distributions are encoded using a convolutional neural network, thermal descriptors are embedded through a dedicated multilayer perceptron, and temporal evolution is predicted using a recurrent network. Instantaneous temperature and thermal rate are provided at each time step, allowing the model to adapt its predictions to changing thermal conditions. The framework is trained and evaluated on datasets generated from validated front-tracking simulations across a wide range of non-isothermal thermal paths. The results show that the thermally conditioned model accurately reproduces grain growth dynamics under complex thermal histories, achieving, in k-fold cross-validation over all simulations, mean relative errors of 2.99% (one-hour cycles) and 4.51% (four-hour cycles) with coefficients of determination exceeding 0.99, and mean relative errors between 1.91% and 14.66% on ten simulations following four unseen thermal histories of increasing complexity.

Evolutionary Design of Interpretable Spatial Descriptors for Structure-Property Relationship Discovery

Aditya Raghavan, Sergei V Kalinin

Structure property prediction is a classical task in machine learning, ranging from molecular activity and materials properties to local functional responses measured in microscopy. For image-based regression, two choices define the information available to the predictor: the size of the image neighborhood and the spatial resolution retained within it. A small neighborhood can omit relevant surrounding structure, whereas a large, finely sampled neighborhood can introduce redundant or irrelevant information. Here, we develop a framework for constructing spatial descriptors for prediction of local spectra and hysteresis loops. We benchmark regression models, use SHAP maps to examine their spatial attribution, and explore the effect of the patch size and compression to establish the relationship between predictive accuracy and descriptor dimensionality. We then use a genetic algorithm to optimize spatial partitions and the assignment of mean, minimum, and maximum pooling operations. Each retained region contributes an explicit measurement of the image, allowing the search to balance prediction error against the number of descriptor values. Cartesian and polar constructions provide complementary ways to organize this information. The resulting descriptors make it possible to examine where spatial detail is needed, where regional summaries are sufficient, and how these choices depend on the predictor and the measured response. The same approach can be used for molecules and solids once the genetic algorithm rules for these are established.

This work thus contributes towards the broader theoretical and data-driven framework for studying the lattice dynamics of solids.

Thermodynamic Underpinnings Affecting Compressive and Tensile Plasticity in Refractory Compositionally Complex Alloys

Eric A. Lass, Carla Joyce C. Nocheseda, Christopher Tandoc, Yong-Jie Hu, Peter K. Liaw

Refractory compositionally complex alloys (R-CCAs) show great promise as next generation structural materials for extreme conditions, but alloys with excellent high temperature mechanical properties most often exhibit virtually zero room temperature tensile ductility, hindering potential commercial application. Several semi-empirical metrics have been employed for predicting plasticity in R-CCAs, including valence electron concentration, Pugh ratio, Rice criteria, and the D-parameter. While many currently available ductility metrics correlate very well when compressive plasticity is considered, only the density functional theory (DFT) based D-parameter shows a strong correlation in tension. A new ductility metric is developed using CALculation of PHAse Diagrams (CALPHAD) thermodynamics, Gamma, with includes the free energy difference between the body-centered cubic (BCC) and hexagonal close-packed (HCP) phases, the interfacial free energy of a (110) BCC surface, and an electronegativity-corrected lattice distortion. The Gamma parameter exhibits better correlation with tensile ductility than D-parameter and performs equally well in compression. While this improvement is modest, a thermodynamically grounded ductility metric is clearly required to capture intrinsic ductility behavior. Extrinsic contributions to brittle behavior in R-CCAs, namely interstitial oxygen content, are also briefly discussed. Combining the Gamma ductility parameter with CALPHAD-calculated oxygen solubility is proposed as a first order attempt to capture both intrinsic and extrinsic contributions to ductile behavior in R-CCAs.
