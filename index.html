Theoretical Investigation of Oxygen-Vacancy Diffusion in SrTiO$_3$ using Neural Network Potentials

Kazutaka Nishiguchi, Ryota Yamamoto, Seiichiro Ten-no

Oxygen-vacancy formation and diffusion play important roles in determining the electronic and atomic transport properties of SrTiO$_3$ (STO). Accurate analysis of such point defects requires sufficiently large-scale and long-time molecular dynamics (MD) simulations; however, direct investigations of oxygen-vacancy dynamics using {\it ab initio} MD (AIMD) based on density functional theory (DFT) are limited to relatively small systems and short time scales because of their high computational cost. In this study, we develop a neural network potential (NNP) for pristine and oxygen-deficient STO using a reference dataset generated from AIMD simulations. The trained NNP model accurately reproduces the DFT reference energies, atomic forces, and virials for both pristine STO and STO containing an oxygen vacancy. The NNP also reproduces the supercell-size dependence of the total energies and oxygen-vacancy formation energies, including the formation energy extrapolated to the dilute limit. Large-scale and long-time MD simulations using the NNP (NNP-MD) are then performed to investigate oxygen-vacancy diffusion. The resulting oxygen-vacancy diffusion coefficients show substantially better agreement with experimental data than those obtained from conventional classical MD simulations. An Arrhenius analysis yields an oxygen-vacancy migration enthalpy of 0.64 eV, in close agreement with the experimental value of 0.67 eV. These results demonstrate that NNPs trained on first-principles data provide an accurate and computationally efficient framework for investigating defect formation and long-time vacancy transport in perovskite oxides.

Markus E. Gruner, Yulia Klunnikova, Mike J. Bruckhoff, Konstatin P. Skokov, Olga N. Miroshkina

The martensitic transformations in stoichiometric Ni$_2$MnGa and related Heusler alloys are governed by a delicate interplay between electronic structure, magnetism, and lattice dynamics. While density functional theory (DFT) has successfully mapped the energy landscape of modulated martensites, it remains computationally prohibitive for capturing finite-temperature dynamics across relevant length and time scales. Here, we bridge this gap by constructing DFT-trained machine-learning force-fields (ML-FF) interpolated from ab initio molecular dynamics. We demonstrate that ML-FF trained on DFT data can reproduce key features of Ni$_2$MnGa, including the soft TA$_2$ phonon and a preference for nanotwinned martensites. Using classical molecular dynamics simulations, we explore the impact of adaptive nanotwinning on the austenite-martensite transition, which facilitates the dynamics of both, forward and backward transformation. We demonstrate, that the negative twin boundary energy along the entire transformations path predicted by DFT for $T=0$, supports the formation of ordered modulated martensites at finite temperatures. Our results pave the way for large-scale simulations of magnetic shape memory alloys with near-DFT accuracy.

Comprehensive Workflow for Uncertainty-Aware Ionic Liquid Solvent Discovery

Alexander Lobo, Urvi Awasthi, Leonid Zhukov

Designing synthesizable materials with targeted properties remains one of the central objectives in materials sciences. Ionic liquids (ILs) are well suited to this goal due to their "tunability", where physical and chemical properties can be precisely customized by modifying their molecular structure, specifically by varying the combination of cations and anions. At the same time, current data-driven property models for ionic liquids often degrade in scarce, noisy, and uneven data regimes, where measurements cover only a small fraction of chemical space and a limited range of thermodynamic conditions. This makes out-of-distribution screening brittle: even when point predictions look plausible, it is difficult to compare candidates consistently, quantify downstream implications, or trust rankings enough to guide molecular discovery. We present a property-prediction and optimal candidate selection workflow that treats data processing, deep learning modeling, and physics-based calculations as a coupled system, linking property prediction with uncertainty estimation and physics-based filtering. By tightening the link between predicted properties and the criteria used for down-selection, the workflow supports reliable out-of-distribution ranking and enables prioritization of previously untested ionic liquid candidates with favorable predicted trade-offs. We demonstrate the application of our workflow by designing novel ionic liquids for CO2 capture from flue gas, predicting their properties and selecting Pareto-optimal candidates based on CO2 solubility, IL viscosity and working capacity.

Accurately Generalizing Deep-Learning Hamiltonian Methods to Plane-Wave Density Functional Theory

Chuntian Wu, Jiaxiang Li, Qiang Xu, Xuecheng Shao, Congwei Xie, Yu Xie

Deep neural networks that predict density functional theory (DFT) Hamiltonians from material structures offer a route to efficient electronic-structure calculations. However, extending localized atomic-orbital (AO) Hamiltonian learning to plane-wave (PW) calculations with hybrid functionals requires a compact AO representation that preserves the effects of nonlocal operators. Here we propose a projectability-guided construction of nonorthogonal AO Hamiltonians from a small, selected set of PW eigenstates and eigenvalues. High-projectability states are retained, and selected higher-energy states complete the AO subspace, avoiding an empirical shift and its arbitrary contribution to the learning targets. Because the construction uses PW eigenstates and eigenvalues, it incorporates nonlocal contributions present in the reference calculation without explicitly reconstructing their AO matrix elements. Benchmarks for MoS2 and sodium phases show accurate reproduction of PW bands with smaller AO bases than real-space reconstruction. The resulting PBE and HSE Hamiltonians can be learned by equivariant neural networks, connecting PW reference calculations with electronic-structure prediction. This approach broadens the range of PW DFT calculations available for AO Hamiltonian learning and enables compact, accurate training targets for developing more widely applicable electronic-structure models.

Composition-controlled optical fingerprints and anisotropy of self-trapped excitons in double perovskites

Simone Grillo, Offir Zachs Maishlos, Liya Koren, Rotem Strassberg, Rachel Lifer, Edi Maizenberg

Double perovskites (DPs) offer a compositionally versatile platform for photocatalysis and optoelectronics, but their soft lattices favor strong exciton-phonon coupling and self-trapped excitons (STEs), which produce broad, red-shifted photoluminescence (PL). Here, we combine optical spectroscopy and ab initio calculations to study lead-free, Sb-doped Cs2BInCl6 nanocrystals with B-site alloying from Na to K. Alloying tunes the PL energy from about 2.48 to 2.70 eV, while absorption features near 3.8 eV remain nearly unchanged. Calculations reveal that Sb doping enables bright radiative recombination channels and delocalizes the excitonic wavefunction. We identify STEs responsible for the large PL red shift, associated with an upward shift of dopant-hybridized in-gap states. Na-to-K substitution selectively changes the STE lattice distortion, explaining why PL varies strongly with composition while absorption does not. Theory further predicts pronounced PL polarization anisotropy that reflects the STE wavefunction's spatial structure and depends on B-site composition. Time-resolved anisotropy measurements closely agree with these predictions, providing experimental fingerprints of the excitonic wavefunction and its evolution with alloying. Together, these results disentangle the roles of doping and alloying in lead-free DPs and establish polarization-resolved spectroscopy as a probe of STE wavefunctions.

Bo Ni

How can language models learn to optimize physical designs through experience? We introduce FracLearn, an experience-abstraction-generalization framework evaluated through a deliberately unfamiliar, Tetris-inspired fracture game. The model first proposes architectures and examines simulated fracture responses to accumulate experience. Abstraction organizes these observations into conditional principles linking reinforcement placement, crack evolution and load transfer, with supporting evidence and failure conditions. Generalization challenges the model to combine these principles in new architectures and surpass the earlier design record. Campaigns demonstrate progressive refinement and recovery through hypothesis revision, yielding a best composite score 11.0% above the experience-stage record. Fracture analyses connect improved performance to crack deflection and temporary trapping as toughening mechanisms, alongside greater residual load capacity. Learning occurs through external knowledge and decisions, without model-weight updates. This approach offers a route to scientific agents that turn computational experiments into reusable physical knowledge for materials discovery and engineering design.

Learning When to Transfer: Reinforcement Learning for Carbon-Footprint Modeling in New Manufacturing Domains

Muhammad Umar Farooq, Daniel R. Cooper

Manufacturers often need to characterize a new material, process, or product before enough operating data are available to train reliable predictive models for this new, target domain. Existing manufacturing operations contain potentially useful knowledge to accelerate this characterization, but indiscriminate reuse of data from existing source domains can cause negative transfer in learning of the new, target domain when the manufacturing domains differ sufficiently. This work proposes a reinforcement-learning method for selective knowledge transfer, where its reward scores each opportunity to transfer against supervised alternatives, so negative transfer is avoided and controlled during forward prediction instead of being diagnosed afterwards. Given limited data from a new target domain, the learned policy chooses among predictors based on individual source operations, subsets of the sources, or the supervised alternatives: fully pooled sources, or target data alone. A validation gate deploys the transferred prediction only when it improves upon the supervised alternatives. The method is designed for learning variables supporting carbon accounting, as well as performance and quality outcomes. We demonstrate the approach by quantifying the embodied greenhouse-gas emissions of parts produced via material-extrusion additive manufacturing, using data from 729 experiments spanning five materials: ABS, PETG, TPU, PLA, and recycled PLA. The response combines measured fabrication electricity with polymer-specific cradle-to-gate production factors, while 22 material, process, and toolpath features characterize each experiment. In leave-one-material-out evaluation, this method achieves a mean test RMSE of 5.2 g CO2e per part, 20.00% lower than target-only training (32.5% lower when only 5-10% of the target training data are available) and 10.30% lower than indiscriminate source-target pooling.

Ionic gradients shape the spatial organization and dynamics of supramolecular polymers

Claudio Perego, Annalisa Cardellini, Andrea Tagliabue, Giovanni M. Pavan

Living supramolecular materials such as microtubules and actin filaments assemble and function in dynamic, heterogeneous environments, subject to chemical concentration gradients. Their rapid and precise response to such gradients---via aggregation, disassembly, or autonomous motion---underlies the fine spatiotemporal control typical of living systems. Reproducing such responsiveness in synthetic materials is a central challenge in materials science and requires a molecular-level understanding of how gradients couple to supramolecular structure and dynamics, which remains difficult to access experimentally. To this end, we combine implicit-solvent coarse-grained molecular dynamics (MD) with the constant chemical potential MD (C$μ$MD) technique to investigate, at molecular resolution, the coupling of two prototypical supramolecular polymer models with externally imposed ionic concentration gradients. Despite their contrasting responses to homogeneous ionic conditions, both systems undergo pronounced spatial reorganization under the gradient, generating regions of locally enhanced supramolecular activity that are inaccessible to homogeneous systems with the same overall content of self-assembling species. Crucially, the local supramolecular dynamics is largely determined by the local concentrations, consistent with the formation of local quasi-equilibria: this direct connection between spatial composition and supramolecular activity emerges as a common governing principle across the two models. These findings shed light on the spatially complex supramolecular response that can be triggered by chemical gradients, and show how a single gradient simulation can encode this response across a continuous range of local chemical environments, thus providing a general strategy to investigate and design materials operating under spatially heterogeneous conditions.

Ashish Kumar Singh, Irina V. Lebedeva

Nudged elastic band (NEB) calculations of small-polaron hopping are demanding because nuclear motion is coupled to a self-consistent localized electronic state. Near the charge-transfer region, slow self-consistent-field convergence, localization switching, and competing electronic solutions complicate surrogate-assisted optimization. We develop a Gaussian-process-assisted NEB workflow for this regime and demonstrate electron small-polaron hopping in rutile TiO2 using spin-polarized density functional theory with a Hubbard U correction (DFT+U ) with SIESTA. A joint energy-force Gaussian process (GP) with a Matérn covariance model advances the elastic band, while active-atom root-mean-square and maximum-atom displacement limits constrain surrogate motion relative to evaluated configurations. Reaching these limits triggers first-principles sampling without requiring full surrogate convergence. Three independently DFT-verified 96-atom [001] calculations using 11, 13, and 15 images and different surrogate settings and GP parameterizations yield barriers of 0.1645, 0.1637, and 0.1640 eV, respectively, spanning 0.793 meV. In the 15-image calculation, repeated central-image sampling activates a separately calibrated local GP during surrogate optimization. Final full-band DFT verification gives a maximum projected NEB force of 0.0346 eV/Å. A 324-atom calculation yields a lower barrier of 0.1302 eV, indicating supercell sensitivity and demonstrating feasibility at larger GP observation dimension. The workflow combines controlled surrogate extrapolation, targeted charge-transfer-region interpolation, and independent DFT verification to obtain polaron migration pathways, emphasizing reproducible barriers and full-band force convergence.

Here, we further develop the graph-based machine learning method Wasserstein Weisfeiler-Lehman Gaussian Process Regression (WWL-GPR): we introduce additional electronic-structure and local-environment features relevant for oxide surfaces and use the resulting model to predict adsorption and TS energies on ZrO$_2$(101) surfaces doped with Al$^{3+}$, Cd$^{2+}$, Ga$^{3+}$, In$^{3+}$, and Zn$^{2+}$. These results support WWL-GPR as a data-efficient surrogate for connecting atomistic energetics with catalyst-screening observables on doped oxide surfaces.

Together, our benchmark, systematic studies, and Bragglite clarify the capabilities and limitations of current PXRD-to-structure models.

Role of defect trapping in the enhanced corrosion resistance of Ni-Mo alloys with yttrium and yttrium oxide additions

Jia-Hong Ke, Xinchang Zhang, Jingfan Yang, Qiufeng Yang, Ruchi Gakhar, Rongjie Song

Corrosion of structural alloys remains a critical challenge for advanced molten salt reactors, where aggressive fluoride salts promote continuous dissolution at the salt-metal interface. While most mitigation strategies emphasize thermodynamic stability, kinetic control of atomic transport has received comparatively less attention. Here, we propose a corrosion mitigation strategy based on diffusion control and vacancy trapping by dilute yttrium (Y) solutes and yttrium oxide (Y$_2$O$_3$) dispersions in Ni alloys. A combined computational and experimental approach integrates density functional theory (DFT), self-consistent mean-field (SCMF) diffusion modeling, and an impurity trapping model to quantify the impact of defect trapping on effective vacancy mobility. Strong first-nearest-neighbor vacancy-Y binding, off-lattice vacancy configurations, and elevated rotational and dissociation barriers result in pronounced suppression of vacancy diffusivity, while Y$_2$O$_3$-associated sites treated as fixed traps in the model further reduce the mobile vacancy population. Ni-Mo (NM), Y-modified (NMY), and Y$_2$O$_3$-containing (NMYO) alloys were tested in molten fluoride salt at 750°C. Corrosion penetration decreases systematically from NM to NMY to NMYO, consistent with the predicted reduction in vacancy-mediated transport. The same trend is observed in mass change, with net weight loss decreasing from 0.52% for NM to 0.23% for NMY and 0.09% for NMYO. These results are consistent with an alloy-side transport contribution from defect trapping and reduced atomic transport. This study therefore suggests point defect engineering as a promising alloy design strategy for extreme nuclear environments.

We find that the marked improvement in the accuracy of the calculated creation energy, when using the GW/BSE approach, is primarily attributable to the slight decrease in the magnitude of the electron energy-loss function when using the GW/BSE approach for energies below ~10-15 eV.
