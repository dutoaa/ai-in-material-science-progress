Crystoria: A Large-Scale Crystal Dataset Linking Thermodynamic and Dynamical Stability for Materials AI

Ke-Xin Pang, Ze-Feng Gao, Yan Gao, Zhong-Yi Lu

Large-scale crystal databases have transformed materials discovery by making millions of structures and thermodynamic properties accessible to materials informatics. Yet dynamical stability remains sparsely characterized at a comparable scale because its evaluation is substantially more computationally demanding than conventional structure and energy calculations. This mismatch creates a critical gap between the rapidly expanding crystal-structure space and the physical information available for materials research and machine learning. Here we introduce Crystoria, a large-scale crystal dataset designed to bring dynamical stability to the scale of modern materials data. We construct Crystoria by systematically curating low-energy and metastable inorganic crystals, standardizing their structural identities, and combining machine-learning-accelerated structural relaxation with phonon-based dynamical screening. The resulting dataset contains 2,649,640 crystal structures, spanning 89 elements, 219 of the 230 space groups, and primitive cells containing 1-48 atoms. Among them, 1,793,353 structures (67.68%) are classified as dynamically stable, revealing substantial variation in dynamical stability across chemical and crystallographic space. By bringing dynamical stability to the scale of millions of crystal structures, Crystoria provides a physically grounded foundation for materials artificial intelligence (AI) and enables systematic exploration of the dynamical crystal landscape.

Generative discovery of chemical reaction networks

Yunhong Han, Gaopeng Ren, Yan Liu, Ronghua Zhang, Huangjiang Huang, Ruiqi Zhang

Chemical kinetics determines molecular stability, reaction selectivity and the evolution of reacting mixtures, but discovering the underlying reaction networks remains challenging. Here we introduce ReactNexus, a generative framework for kinetic-guided network exploration starting solely from reactant graphs. Generative models propose transition states and predict the full reaction trajectories connecting reactants and products, while calculations using a machine-learned interatomic potential verify connectivity and evaluate electronic barriers. On an in-distribution benchmark of 10,000 generated candidates, 80.8% connect to the input reactant after density functional theory (DFT) optimization and intrinsic reaction coordinate verification. The resulting networks uncover lower-barrier cooperative pathways and competition between catalyst release and catalyst-bound branches. In the examined carbocation cascades, coverage of known product precursors saturates after limited expansion and distinct entries converge onto shared intermediates, indicating a compact accessible reaction space. Network-derived candidate structures further assist mass-spectral interpretation of a complex nucleophilic aromatic substitution reaction. Together, these results establish a generative approach to reaction-network discovery and mechanistic analysis without predefined products or reaction templates.

Framework-Conditional Open Materials Generation

Maya M. Martirossyan, Hillary Pan, Philipp Höllmer, Stefano Martiniani

Theoretical predictions of novel materials rely on a variety of techniques, from data-mining-based ion substitution methods to fully de novo generative models. Each approach imposes specific constraints and encounters unique challenges when extrapolating beyond the distribution of known materials to discover new ones. The foremost goal of materials generation is to produce stable, unique, and novel (S.U.N.) structures. Predicted structures that meet these criteria, however, typically result from novel chemical compositions mapped onto known structural prototypes or frameworks. We leverage this heuristic insight to present a new generative paradigm for the de novo prediction of inorganic crystalline materials constrained by their structural framework---i.e., the unit cell shapes and respective particle positions. Our method, termed OMatG-FC (Open Materials Framework-Conditional Generation), learns via a combination of discrete flow matching on the atomic species and stochastic interpolants for the volume scaling of the unit cell lattice, while fixing in-place the fractional coordinates and the unit-volume lattice vectors, dramatically reducing the dimensionality of the problem compared to unconstrained (framework-free) generation. Our method is more flexible than existing prototype-constrained methods, demonstrating capability for discovery of unseen, stable prototypes derived from known structural frameworks. We also investigate the number of unique frameworks in the MP-20 dataset, demonstrating the propensity of duplicate frameworks within the dataset and providing a framework-split version. We benchmark the performance of our model using the LeMat-GenBench suite using three ML interatomic potentials and achieve state-of-the-art S.U.N. rate below the convex hull, eschewing the need to learn atomic packings for materials discovery.

PAPERBENCHX: Can AI Reproduce Science Across Disciplines?

Ruoyu Wu, Zengji Tu, Aolong Sun, Tianyi Ma, Junren Li, Xinbo Xu

Before AI can be trusted to discover new science, it should be able to reproduce what science already knows. Reproduction provides a rigorous checkpoint for an AI scientist: the target claim is known, yet recovering it still requires translating an incomplete scientific description into an executable experiment and producing evidence that can be independently verified. Existing research-reproduction benchmarks largely focus on AI and machine learning, where experiments share relatively homogeneous software stacks. Extending this capability across scientific disciplines introduces a different challenge: agents must operate domain-specific scientific software, make physical and numerical choices, diagnose long-running computations, and distinguish valid evidence from merely plausible outputs. We introduce PAPERBENCHX, a benchmark of 93 expert-constructed paper-reproduction tasks spanning 12 research areas across electromagnetics, photonics, chemistry, materials science, biology, and robotics. Each task provides the source paper, a public task specification, and a domain-native scientific environment involving specialized scientific software. Agents must reconstruct a key experiment, execute and diagnose it, and deliver a runnable workflow that supports the target scientific claim. To evaluate scientific validity rather than surface-level agreement, we replay every submission from scratch and score only regenerated evidence against expert-validated criteria. Across 10 frontier agent configurations, the best configuration fully reproduces only 14% tasks under the 90% threshold. Meanwhile, mean rubric scores range from 27.53% to 59.08%, indicating that substantial partial progress rarely translates into complete scientific reproduction. These results reveal a large gap between paper understanding and reliably producing reproducible scientific evidence.

ML-COHP: predicting bond descriptors using latent space of a universal interatomic potentials

Daniil S. Alekseev, Ilya V. Chepkasov, Alexander G. Kvashnin

The integrated crystal orbital Hamilton population (ICOHP) is the standard measure of bond strength in a DFT calculation, but every structure needs its own DFT single point and LOBSTER projection, which takes days for a cell of a hundred atoms, such as a grain boundary or a catalytic nanoparticle. We predict ICOHP from the frozen descriptors of a MACE foundation model. A symmetric pair head reads the scalar and bond-projected vector channels of the two atoms and corrects a pair baseline built from the element embeddings of the same model. We tested the model on the high-entropy diboride (Hf,Zr,Ti,Nb,Ta)B$_2$, Pt clusters on defective graphene and Pt/Pd nanoparticles with adsorbed O$_2$. The error on bonds stronger than 0.5 eV is 1.6-4.1%, about half that of the Gaussian-process model of Ferreira et al. trained on the same data. Trained only on high-temperature HEB$_2$ snapshots, the model reproduces the LOBSTER ICOHP of relaxed grain boundary cells to 0.06 eV in about a second, against 5-51 hours of LOBSTER. The vector channels halve the error compared to the scalar ones, and the baseline keeps the model usable with two training structures. With geometries from a fine-tuned MACE potential, the model gives the bonding of Pt/Pd particles over a continuous O$_2$ coverage, and DFT on these particles shows that O$_2$ costs a Pt-rich shell several times more metal--metal bonding than a Pd shell.

Resisting Drying Stress in Laponite Bentonite Clay Composites, Two Step Yielding and Crack Peel Selection

Tapati Dutta, Agnishikha Chakrabarti, Soham Mitra, Deepshikha Das, Indranil Chakraborty

Clay films that dry on a rigid substrate crack, peel or stay intact, and which of these happens matters for coatings, barrier layers and ceramic processing. Composites of clay with polymers have been studied widely. Clay composites, whose components differ in size and charge, are much less explored. We show that mixing the nano scale clay Laponite with the micron scale clay Bentonite reduces desiccation damage on both a hydrophobic and a hydrophilic substrate, Laponite films stay essentially intact, Bentonite films fracture extensively, and on glass most intermediate compositions are less damaged than a simple average of the two would suggest. Rheology of the gelled suspensions reveals a two step yielding in every composition, and the cohesive energy derived from it depends by orders of magnitude on which step is used, a point that any rheology cracking correlation must specify. A dimensionless cohesion adhesion index, whose sign we derive from an energy balance, is consistent with compositions that peel on polypropylene and crack on glass. Replicate experiments were performed for robustness of results. The results identify composition as a simple handle on the crack resistance of clay films and open the search for the critical crack-free thickness as a function of clay composition.

AtomSteer: Test-Time Constraint Steering for Atomistic Generative Models

Pengfei Cai, Katharina S. C. Jäger, Utkarsh Utkarsh, Nofit Segal, Bowen Deng, Akshay Subramanian

Diffusion and flow matching models offer scalable approaches to generating crystalline materials, molecular conformers, and protein-ligand complexes. However, satisfying crystallographic symmetry, structural geometry, and accurate stereochemistry remains challenging. Satisfying these requirements calls for corrections that preserve the geometry already captured by the pretrained model. In this work, we propose AtomSteer, a test-time inference framework for steering pretrained atomistic generative models with structural constraints. During sampling, AtomSteer corrects intermediate structure predictions with bounded local projections, graph-rigid stereochemical transformations, and projections over periodic symmetries, accepting updates that improve constraint satisfaction while limiting changes to the rest of the structure. We demonstrate AtomSteer across three pretrained backbones, FlowMM, ET-Flow, and Boltz-2, spanning de novo crystal generation and crystal structure prediction, molecular conformer generation, and protein-ligand complex structure prediction. AtomSteer improves space-group distribution coverage in de novo crystal generation and improves target space-group recovery in crystal structure prediction. AtomSteer improves molecular validity and E/Z stereochemical accuracy while retaining conformer ensemble fidelity. For protein-ligand complexes, AtomSteer reduces ligand E/Z errors relative to experimental reference structures while achieving competitive docking success and PoseBusters validity. Our method provides a unified framework for adding chemical and crystallographic control to existing atomistic generative models without retraining or architectural changes.

First-Principles Identification of Higher-Order Configurational Energy Terms and Their Magnetic-State Dependence in Lithium-Rich Oxyfluorides: From QUBO to HUBO

Joonho Bae

In this work, we use spin-polarized density functional theory (DFT) to identify substantial third-order, nonadditive configurational energy contributions in lithium-rich oxyfluorides that quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) models cannot represent. We test the extension from QUBO to higher-order unconstrained binary optimization (HUBO) in two disordered-rocksalt cathode materials, Li$_2$MnO$_2$F (LMOF) and Li$_2$Mn$_{0.5}$Ti$_{0.5}$O$_2$F (LMTiOF), using composition-preserving DFT energy maps. Using fixed-geometry energy maps, we show that cubic HUBO captures nonadditive contributions absent from QUBO models and substantially reduces fitting errors in both materials. Direct DFT energy differences confirm third-order nonadditivity, while comparisons of magnetic initializations reveal a strong dependence of the LMTiOF contribution on the converged electronic state. These results support the inclusion of higher-order terms when modeling configurational energetics and highlight the need to account for magnetic-state dependence in constructing DFT energy maps. The extracted terms describe effective couplings among composition-preserving exchange variables, rather than uniquely identified microscopic three-atom interactions.

Excitons in solid molecular hydrogen from quantum Monte Carlo

Edgar Josué Landinez Borda, Ravindra Shinde, Claudia Filippi, Markus Holzmann, Saverio Moroni

We use quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) methods to investigate the lowest neutral excitation of solid molecular hydrogen in phase-I under increasing compression, with emphasis on structural and response properties. We employ QMC wave functions for the excited state that feature only a single orbital substitution, or a multideterminant expansion including every single particle-hole transition, and compare the results with the Bethe-Salpeter equation (BSE) and time-dependent density-functional theory (TDDFT). We examine a number of nuclear configurations representing thermal and quantum fluctuations. The effect of the multideterminant expansion is marginal on the QMC excitation energies but crucial on the exciton localization and related quantities, irrespective of full optimization and imaginary time projection. In the strong insulating regime at low density, multideterminant QMC results match and in some cases surpass the degree of localization afforded by BSE. A similar trend is observed for transition dipole moments and excitation-induced forces, with generally good agreement between QMC and BSE but marked discrepancies for nuclear configurations where localization differs significantly. TDDFT generally gives a more extended exciton with correspondingly weaker transition dipole moments. These differences decrease upon compression, as the system approaches a semiconducting regime where multiple particle-hole transitions are less important.

Development and application of a machine learning potential for diamond surfaces and their sliding interfaces with silicon nitride and silica: effect of passivation, orientation and load

Enrico Pedretti, Maria Clelia Righi

Friction and wear of diamond are controlled by its surface chemistry, which determines whether covalent bonds form across the sliding interface. First-principles simulations have explored the role of surface orientation and passivation, but are limited to a few hundred atoms and tens of picoseconds. To bridge this gap, we develop a reactive machine learning interatomic potential for the C/H/N/O/Si chemical space, covering diamond, silicon nitride, silicon oxide, their surfaces, interfaces, and environmental molecules. The potential is trained on a large dataset of diverse structures and is tested on multiple physical properties, including ab initio reaction barriers on diamond surfaces, and structural/mechanical properties of amorphous Si$_3$N$_4$ and SiO$_2$. We then simulate sliding of diamond-Si$_3$N$_4$ interfaces with four surface orientations, clean and H/OH-passivated terminations, and normal loads from 0.1 to 23 GPa. We identify two different regimes: one where sliding is localized at the interface, and shear stress is proportional to the interfacial bond density, and another where shear is distributed inside the softer material, and adhesion due to full interfacial bonding dominates. These results show how passivation, surface orientation and load together determine interfacial bonding and friction, with implications for the environmental sensitivity of diamond wear. More broadly, the potential enables reactive simulations of diamond, silicon nitride and silica with near-ab initio accuracy at length and time scales beyond first-principles methods, providing a useful tool for future studies of wear at plowing asperities, chemical-mechanical polishing, and properties of amorphous ceramic films.

First-Principles Investigation of Irida-Graphane: A Wide-Band-Gap Semiconductor Derived from Irida-Graphene

Djardiel S. Gomes, Isaac M. Félix, Marcelo L. Pereira Junior

Opening a band gap in metallic two-dimensional carbon allotropes is an important step toward their use in electronic and optoelectronic devices, and full hydrogenation has proven to be an effective route to achieve it. Here, we propose irida-graphane, the fully hydrogenated derivative of the irida-graphene, a monolayer composed of three-, six-, and eight-membered rings, and investigate its structural, mechanical, electronic, and optical properties using density functional theory (DFT) at the PBE and HSE06 levels combined with phonon and \textit{ab initio} molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations. All carbon atoms adopt $sp^3$ hybridization, with the hydrogen atoms of the three- and six-membered rings bonded to opposite sides of a buckled carbon framework, and the lattice parameter contracts by 4.3% with respect to irida-graphene. Irida-graphane is transparent in the visible range, with reflectivity below 0.3%, and absorbs strongly in the deep ultraviolet, with absorption coefficients of the order of $10^{5} - 10^{6}$ cm$^{-1}$. These results suggest irida-graphane as a candidate wide-gap material for ultraviolet optoelectronics and transparent coatings.

Odd Elasticity in Strain-Gradient Continua: Anisotropy Classification and Non-Hermitian Elastodynamics

Payam Poorsolhjouy

Odd elasticity extends continuum mechanics to active nonconservative systems containing internal energy sources, such as robotic active metamaterials with nonreciprocal feedback. Modeling these systems as effective continua requires relaxing Maxwell-Betti reciprocity, which results in stiffness tensors lacking major symmetry. In this contribution, we extend this framework to 3D strain-gradient odd elasticity to incorporate the length scales inherent in these heterogeneous systems. By applying the full hierarchy of 3D point-group symmetries along chiral and achiral branches, we derive the exhaustive set of fourth-, fifth-, and sixth-order odd elastic stiffness tensors. Further, utilizing the dynamic principle of virtual work, we formulate the equilibrium equations, higher-order boundary conditions, and the generalized acoustic tensor for elastodynamic analysis. The results demonstrate that nonreciprocal moduli survive even under full isotropy in the sixth-order stiffness, under hemitropy in the fifth-order stiffness, and under cubic symmetry in fourth-order stiffness. In contrast, the elastodynamic analysis reveals that under isotropic and hemitropic limits, despite the underlying odd stiffness moduli, the acoustic tensor remains unconditionally Hermitian. Non-Hermitian elastodynamics is shown to require anisotropy. By numerically analyzing a transversely hemitropic odd elastic continuum, we demonstrate that this symmetry reduction results in non-Hermitian elastodynamic phenomena including exceptional points in the dispersion spectra and active energy exchange through simultaneous amplification and attenuation of the elastic wave components. These results provide the necessary theoretical foundation for future multiscale homogenization frameworks for anisotropic strain-gradient active microstructures, and for the design of nonreciprocal strain-gradient metamaterials.

Predicting unstable planar fault energies in multicomponent alloys from pure elemental components

C Tang, J Liu, J Yi, M R Barnett

The unstable planar fault energies (UPFEs) govern slip and twinning but are not experimentally measurable. Using EAM potentials complemented by DFT, we investigate their temperature and compositional dependence and their relationship with the shear modulus. For pure \textit{fcc} elements with low stacking fault energies (SFEs), UPFEs are substantially less temperature-sensitive than SFEs.
