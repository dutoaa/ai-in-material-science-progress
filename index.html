Automated Materials Data and Knowledge Extraction using Machine Learning and Large Language Models

Aravindan Kamatchi Sundaram, B. Pabitramohan Prusty, Rohit Batra

Over the past decade, substantial efforts have been devoted to developing machine learning (ML) methods for extracting materials data and, where possible, materials knowledge directly from the scientific literature. This review traces the evolution of these methods from heuristic, rule-based pipelines through natural language processing (NLP) models and transformer-based encoders to, lastly, large language models (LLMs) and agentic systems capable of extracting materials information across multiple modalities, including text, tables, and figures. Beside construction of large, queryable materials databases and knowledge graphs that support downstream materials discovery, we discuss how these advances have enabled new capabilities, such as, generic question answering across materials science, AI co-scientists and chatbots for research assistance, and closed-loop optimization and discovery when integrated with autonomous laboratories. We critically assess the datasets generated using these approaches, their extraction accuracy and computational costs, and the respective strengths and limitations of different methodologies, and even provide practical guidelines for developing more reliable and scalable materials data extraction pipelines. Finally, we identify key open challenges, including the extraction of full materials structure-property-processing-performance tetrahedron at the sample level, the scarcity of reliable benchmark datasets for systematic evaluation, and the resolution of non-standard materials terminology across different document modalities.

Retention-constrained fine-tuning of a pre-trained aluminium MACE potential for non-equilibrium defect dynamics and plasticity

Yue Wu, Yuan Tian, Deng Pan, Mingwei Chen

Adapting a pre-trained machine-learning interatomic potential for non-equilibrium defect dynamics and plasticity requires improving defect accuracy without degrading the near-equilibrium elastic and vibrational response. Here we achieve this balance for the aluminium MACE potential QP17 by integrating conditional readout uncertainty, iterative full-parameter updates and protocol-compatible relative-energy anchoring. Sensitivities over 144 linear readout weights guide acquisition, with within-batch covariance updates and evolving neural representations. After four active-learning rounds (32 acquired configurations and 400 gradient updates), force-uncertainty acquisition reduces mean development force error from 22.43 to 21.52 meV/Angstrom, outperforming random sampling (22.70), geometry-diversity selection (22.46) and uniform training across all 104 candidate labels (22.71) under the same update budget. All three force-uncertainty runs satisfy bulk-mechanical retention criteria: historical relative-energy error decreases from 0.375 to 0.207 meV/atom, while harmonic phonon root-mean-square drift remains below 0.05 THz. Matched objective ablations reveal that relative-energy anchoring suppresses harmonic drift. Out-of-sample evaluations across planar faults, twin boundaries and grain boundaries demonstrate transferable gains in defect energies and deviatoric stresses, even though virial stresses are never fitted. A closed-loop molecular dynamics and density functional theory (MD-DFT) pilot confirms that automated acceptance gating intercepts updates that violate historical elastic-group energy or vibrational tolerances. Together, these components establish a retention-constrained fine-tuning protocol for improving plastic-deformation observables while preserving the tested bulk mechanical response.

Phase field dislocation dynamics prediction of stacking fault widths in Cu-Al Alloys using generalised stacking fault energy from DFT and interatomic potentials

Arjun Varma R., Ankosh D. Deshmukh, Sushil Kumar, Vaishali Shah, M. P. Gururajan

The equilibrium width of intrinsic stacking faults is an important material parameter affecting key deformation phenomena. Here, we perform a comparative assessment of $γ$-surfaces generated using MD -- with a classical embedded-atom potential (ZW) and a machine-learned Gaussian Approximation Potential (GAP) -- and density functional theory (DFT), for Cu-Al alloys of varying Al content, and incorporate them as an energy term in PFDD simulations to obtain the equilibrium SFW. All three methods reproduce the lattice parameter and elastic moduli of Cu and Cu-Al alloys in reasonable agreement with each other and with experimental measurements from literature, but the MD-derived $γ$-surfaces, for both ZW and GAP, deviate substantially from DFT at the seven characteristic points, with the largest discrepancies at $T$ and $T_1$. When incorporated into PFDD, the DFT-derived $γ$-surfaces successfully predict an equilibrium SFW that increases with Al content and agrees well with standalone MD measurements and experimental values for pure Cu, whereas the MD-derived $γ$-surfaces do not yield an accurate SFW within PFDD, as the altered generalized stacking fault energy landscape causes the dissociating partials to become pinned soon after separation. These results show that DFT-derived $γ$-surfaces remain the more reliable route to alloy SFW prediction, and interatomic potentials must capture the shear path accurately to enable robust, efficient MD-PFDD modelling of stacking faults in alloys.

From Autonomous Knowledge Generation to Generalizable Property Prediction: A Precision-First Pipeline for Electro-Optic Materials Discovery

Dibakar Datta

Electro-optic materials, whose refractive index changes linearly with an applied electric field (the Pockels effect), set the speed and energy of optical modulators and microwave-to-optical links for quantum computing. Designing better materials requires optimizing composition alongside temperature, strain, and bias field. However, general repositories lack electro-optic tensor data. We present a pipeline that builds such a database from literature following the rule: "no value is better than a wrong value." The pipeline has five stages: physics-aware acquisition, layout-aware extraction via LLM and deterministic table parsers, a three-tier verification gate, cross-paper consensus using median and median absolute deviation, and a noise-robust surrogate model audited by leave-one-material-out (LOMO) testing. Physics enters directly: notation is mapped, point-group symmetry-forbidden Pockels components are rejected, equivalent labels are merged, and values carry provenance tags. In a run over 49 publications, the gate processed 816 candidates into 414 accepted, 215 held for review, and 187 rejected. Against a hand-certified gold standard of 303 values from 16 papers, the run achieved a precision of 0.51 and recall of 0.48 for exact record matches. Across four database snapshots, held-out materials where the surrogate model beat a predict-the-mean baseline grew from 1 of 4 to 9 of 13 as the database expanded from 10 to 22 materials. While not a controlled comparison, this trend supports the premise that a precision-oriented, physics-checked database can power forward models beyond its training set and clarifies requirements for inverse material design.

No Value Is Better Than a Wrong Value: Finding and Handling Erroneous Data in Literature-Mined Datasets for AI

Achyut Manoj, Advai Aiyer, Andrew Lee, Nayan Saini, Pragya Birla, Dibakar Datta

Machine learning across materials science, chemistry, energy, and medicine increasingly relies on literature-extracted datasets due to a lack of curated databases. However, automated extraction introduces errors: text recognition misreads digits, units and notations are confused, values are misattributed, and LLMs hallucinate numbers. Drawing on our work building an electro-optic property database, we present a taxonomy of errors organized by their origin and signatures, alongside a layered framework for error detection. The framework combines schema and provenance checks, physical admissibility (bounds, symmetry, and governing equations), cross-source statistical consensus, model auditing, and suspicion-ranked review. In a production run, our verification gate halted 402 of 816 candidate values with typed reasons. Yet, an audit against curated references showed that 30 of 112 accepted values were defective; standard symmetry rules alone caught 21 of these. On measured BaTiO3 and KNbO3 tensors, constitutive identities caught 8 of 10 single-value errors and isolated the corrupted entries. In a controlled benchmark, the combined framework caught 81% of errors at 85% precision, including errors in single-report materials, bringing model accuracy on held-out data to within 3% of a model trained on clean data. Uncleaned data severely impairs discovery: 89% of the twenty largest values were errors, causing AI searches to prioritize false positives. Once bad numbers are controlled, missing measurement conditions emerge as the main limit, as distinct records become indistinguishable without full context. We outline directions for the field, including shared physics rule libraries and self-reporting databases.

A thermodynamic correction layer for machine learning interatomic potentials

Sarath Menon, Braulio Sanchez Ortega, Ralf Drautz

Machine learning interatomic potentials enable atomistic simulations with near \textit{ab initio} accuracy over large length and time scales. Foundation models extend this capability across the periodic table but inherit the thermodynamic description of the electronic structure methods used to generate their training data. Small errors in relative phase stability can therefore produce large errors in transformation temperatures. We introduce an additive thermodynamic correction layer that shifts phase free energies towards an assessed reference without retraining or modifying the base potential. A first order thermodynamic construction converts the required phase free energy shifts directly into configuration energy targets, which are fitted using a linear atomic cluster expansion and refined iteratively from measured free energies. We apply the method to melting and solid to solid transformations across elements with different crystal structures using a foundation GRACE model. After the correction, all transformation temperatures lie within the prescribed tolerance of the assessed values, including Ca, Ti, and Zr, where a solid to solid transition and melting are corrected together. Static energy curves, bulk moduli, and liquid structure remain close to those of the base model. Vacancy formation energies, thermal expansion, and liquid density depend on the corrected phase balance and change where the correction is large. The method requires neither retraining, differentiable dynamics, nor access to the parameters of the base potential. It provides a modular route for combining transferable atomistic models with assessed phase thermodynamics.

Exploring Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures for Self-Supervised Representation Learning of Powder X-ray Diffraction Patterns

Ming-Chiang Chang, Brian DeCost

Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) is the primary high-throughput method for resolving crystal structures, a cornerstone of materials science. As high-throughput synthesis and self-driving laboratories advance, automated XRD pattern analysis has become vital for accelerating materials discovery. However, traditional matrix factorization methods fail when lattice variations cause non-linear peak shifts. At the same time, supervised learning approaches face a combinatorial explosion when handling multi-phase mixtures. To overcome these challenges, we adopt a self-supervised Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) to learn representation embeddings from 1D diffraction patterns. By optimizing a masked self-prediction loss directly within the latent space, the framework captures high-level crystallographic semantics. Downstream evaluations show that lightweight attentive probes trained on frozen JEPA representations achieve space group classification and lattice parameter estimation accuracy close to much larger supervised vision transformers. Furthermore, latent probing reveals that the learned representation space naturally organizes physical transformations, isolating smooth trajectories for peak broadening. Interestingly, decoding along latent paths between single-phase crystal representations yields realistic multi-phase mixture patterns, demonstrating an emergent continuous structure learned without explicit multi-phase supervision.

These findings highlight the potential of transition-metal-functionalized aza-triphenylene as a promising platform for developing efficient HER electrocatalysts.

TCSP3.0: Template-Based Crystal Structure Prediction with Coefficient-Preserving Element Substitution and Structural Diversity

Ying Feng, Jeffrey Hu, Lai Wei, Liang Tao, Madeline Miklos, Dipannoy Das Gupta

Template-based crystal structure prediction (CSP) transfers a known crystal structure to a new composition by element substitution, so its accuracy depends on retrieving suitable templates, mapping elements and ranking the resulting candidates correctly. Auditing the original TCSP2.0 implementation on the T180 benchmark, we found that 469 of its 2,347 selected structures (20%) have the wrong composition, caused by element mappings that ignore stoichiometric coefficients and by sequential text substitution that cascades or corrupts element symbols. We propose TCSP3.0, which enforces coefficient-preserving mappings, substitutes species simultaneously on parsed structures and validates every candidate's composition and cell. It further adds periodic-number retrieval, soft oxidation-state evidence, radius-compatibility scoring, structural deduplication, and ORB-v3 machine-learning-potential relaxation and energy ranking of its candidates, as used by deep generative CSP baselines. TCSP3.0 achieves Top-1 and Top-20 match rates of 72.2% and 90.6% on T180, compared with 28.3% and 45.6% for TCSP2.0, and a Top-20 match rate of 74.0% on a new 800-target benchmark (T800). On T180 it also outperforms PNcsp+ and deep generative models CrystalFlow and MaskGXT (Top-1 72.2% versus at most 63.9%). Even without relaxation, TCSP3.0 reaches 67.8% at Top-1 and 90.6% at Top-20 in a median of 28 s per target on two CPU cores, about ten times faster than CrystalFlow. Paired ten-arm ablations on T180 and T800 show that most of the improvement comes from the correctness fixes; structural deduplication significantly improves the Top-5 match rate, embedding-based retrieval is required for coverage, and the remaining scoring features have small or null effects.

High-efficiency solid-state electrochemical thermal switching through phonon engineering of 2D materials

Harry Mclean, Steven Paul Hepplestone

Reversible solid-state thermal switches with large thermal contrasts remain rare, yet are essential for advanced thermal management. Using universal machine-learned interatomic potentials (uMLIPs) and density functional theory (DFT), we screen 57 transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDCs), spanning the 1T and 2H phases with Li and Mg intercalation. Among the candidates, Li$_a$ZrS$_2$ combines exceptional thermal conductivity switching with low heat capacity. Lithiation to Li$_{0.125}$ZrS$_2$ reduces the cross-plane thermal conductivity from approximately 3.7 to 0.05 W m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$. The switching behaviour exceeds that of previously reported solid-state electrochemical thermal switches. Device-level modelling further demonstrates the potential for waste-heat recovery, with a pulsed system capturing 86\% of the energy recovered by an ideal switch. We attribute the switching behaviour to substantial softening of the Zr--Zr framework, with the mean force constant decreasing from approximately 4.2 to 1.2 eV $\mathrm{\mathring{A}}^{-2}$, alongside enhanced scattering of heat-carrying acoustic phonons. These findings establish electrochemical intercalation as a promising route to engineering thermal transport in layered materials.

Autonomous atomistic-continuum coupling through uncertainty-aware active learning

Daniel Abdulah, Hariswaran Sitaraman, Ross E. Larsen, Kevin Patrick Griffin, Grace Wei, Janelle Domantay

We develop an uncertainty-aware surrogate modeling approach for coupling an atomistic kinetic Monte Carlo (KMC) surface-chemistry solver with a continuum multi-species fluid dynamics solver across disparate physical length and time scales. The surrogate model predicts boundary mass fluxes for six species and their uncertainties, which the continuum solver monitors to automatically trigger additional KMC simulations when new training data are needed. This enables efficient multiscale coupling and maintains a user-specified level of accuracy. We demonstrate the approach for reactive halide-perovskite vapor deposition, which involves a multi-species vapor jet impinging on a reactive substrate. The KMC solver uses a DFT-calibrated energetics model that we construct as part of this work and provides net deposition and desorption rates for six species. With a conservative uncertainty threshold, the surrogate-coupled approach reproduces cumulative mass fluxes of the directly coupled approach with errors typically within 10-20%, while capturing the non-monotonic dependence of net deposition on species partial pressure. Importantly, the results show that detailed crystal structure need not be explicitly represented to predict quantities exchanged between atomistic and continuum solvers; species-resolved mass fluxes provide a sufficiently low-dimensional representation of the atomistic response. For this accuracy target, the autonomous online-learning approach reduces the number of required atomistic simulations by two to three orders of magnitude relative to direct multiscale coupling.
