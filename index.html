AdsNet: A Graph Neural Network for Direct Adsorption Energy Prediction

Changzhi Ai, Frank Abild-Pedersen, Johannes Voss

Fast and accurate prediction of adsorption energies is critical for high-throughput computational catalyst screening, existing methods compromise either on evaluation speed (full-graph machine learning potentials) or geometric sensitivity (coordination-based models like the alpha-scheme). Here, we present AdsNet, an equivariant graph neural network that directly predicts adsorption energies in a single forward pass, which operates on a local graph restricted only to adsorbate atoms and its immediate surface neighbors within a cutoff radius. This local graph formulation decouples computational complexity from total system size while encoding continuous geometric features (interatomic distances and directional vectors) enabling sensitivity to subtle variations in local bonding environments. AdsNet adopts a message-passing architecture with scalar and vector node features over multiple interaction layers. Trained on AdsTraj, a dataset of 33,920 density functional theory (DFT) calculated structures spanning 26 transition metals and 6 adsorbates (H*, C*, O*, CO*, OH*, HOCO*), the model achieves an adsorption energy root-mean-square error (RMSE) of 0.032 eV and a force RMSE of 0.047 eV/A. Analytic forces obtained via automatic differentiation enable structure relaxation through a projected gradient descent algorithm with adaptive line search, eliminating entire geometry optimizations. Additionally, AdsNet uniquely decomposes predicted adsorption energies into contributions from individual atoms within the local adsorbate graph - an interpretability feature unattainable with conventional machine-learning potentials. AdsNet thus establishes an accurate, highly efficient, and interpretable framework for large-scale adsorption energy prediction and local interface relaxation across various material surfaces.

Synthetic Crystal Data Generation for Diffusion Pretraining in Material Property Prediction

Aamod Varma, Shuyi Jia, Victor Fung

Diffusion-based pretraining has emerged as a powerful strategy for learning transferable representations in graph neural networks for materials property prediction without requiring quantum-mechanical labels such as energies, forces, and stresses for every pretraining structure. However, the method retains a critical dependency: it requires structures residing near local minima of a potential energy surface, which have traditionally been obtained through costly DFT relaxations of known or physically synthesized compounds. This reliance constrains both the scale and the structural diversity of available pretraining data, leaving large regions of crystal structure space unexplored. In this work, we connect diffusion-based pretraining with synthetic structure generation to reduce this bottleneck. We introduce a synthetic data generation pipeline combining three complementary strategies: perturb-and-relax, generative sampling via MatterGen, and protostructure enumeration. Where structural relaxation is required, we use fast machine-learned interatomic potentials instead of performing a new DFT relaxation for each generated structure. We further compare a coordinate-denoising control with continuous-time variance-preserving (VP) and sub-VP diffusion objectives using a randomly initialized Orb-v2 backbone. The source comparison covers seven MatBench property-prediction tasks, and an eighth task, \texttt{mp\_gap}, is included in the protostructure scaling study. Every synthetic-pretraining condition numerically outperforms random initialization on all seven source-comparison tasks. Scaling the protostructure corpus from 20k to 165k under a fixed 400-epoch schedule numerically matches or exceeds MP-20k pretraining on most tasks; because this protocol increases both data diversity and optimization updates, the result reflects joint data-and-compute scaling.

This work solves both issues for the pentanary (MoWNbTaV)S\textsubscript{2} monolayer with a Gaussian approximation potential trained on DFT data by iterative learning. The potential and its development workflow enable further atomistic simulations and offer a transferable approach to screening related two-dimensional high-entropy materials.

Physics-Guided Crystal Structure Elucidation from Powder X-Ray Diffraction with Universal Machine-Learning Interatomic Potentials

Yunsung Lim, Seungwoo Hwang, Seungwu Han

Determining unique atomic coordinates from powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) remains challenging because peak overlap, weak scattering from light elements, limited scattering contrast between elements with similar atomic numbers, and experimental noise render the inverse problem underdetermined. Here, we present XCAPE (XRD-Constrained Accelerated Prediction Engine), a crystal structure prediction (CSP) framework that integrates experimentally derived composition, unit cell parameters, space group, and PXRD data into a symmetry-aware evolutionary search. XCAPE explores Wyckoff-site configurations using tailored mutation operations, evaluates candidates by both energetic stability and diffraction agreement, and accelerates geometry optimization using a universal machine-learning interatomic potential (MLIP). The framework recovered 71 of 77 database-derived benchmark structures (92.2%) from simulated PXRD patterns and all 12 reference structures from experimental PXRD patterns. It further identified 12 candidate structure assignments that are consistent with experimental PXRD patterns, lack matching entries in the Inorganic Crystal Structure Database (ICSD), and are thermodynamically competitive at the MLIP level. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations showed that 10 of these 12 candidates lie within 10 meV/atom of the convex hull. By bridging experimental diffraction data and physically plausible atomistic models, XCAPE can provide structural insight into previously unresolved materials and support accelerated materials discovery in automated research workflows.

Weight-Space Programming for Materials Science

Sk Md Ahnaf Akif Alvi, Raymundo Arróyave

A materials discovery campaign often begins with almost no measurements of the property it is searching for, and the models that rank candidates or screen the output of a generator then have to be built from those first few labels. Following recent work on weight-space programming, in which a language model writes the weights of a small network directly as text, we show that a general-purpose model, given the task in words, the names of the inputs and a network of 16 hidden units with every weight set to zero, can provide this starting point by filling in all of the weights from what it has learned about materials. With no labels, the networks of the strongest model rank experimental alloys at Spearman co-efficient = 0.64 to 0.79, where the best of five simple descriptors reaches 0.47 to 0.62. They rank the bulk modulus of crystals added during generative discovery loops at 0.95, where the inverse volume per atom reaches 0.89, and 35 phase-equilibrium targets computed with Thermo-Calc at a mean of 0.67, close to the 0.70 of an extrapolation from calculated binary and ternary data. As the prior mean of a Gaussian process they raise the ranking at five labels from 0.35 to 0.69 on alloys and from 0.15 to 0.95 on crystals, and they lower the regret of Bayesian optimization significantly on 4 of 5 experimental tasks and on 13 to 16 of 33 computed targets, depending on how multiple tests are corrected, without raising it significantly on any. The same networks also serve as the mean of a Bayesian neural network and as a screen for the samples of two crystal generators, where they do as well as standard models trained on 400 labeled structures. The gain is confined to the first tens of labels and to targets that no rule or sub-system extrapolation already ranks.

Alloying effects on the interface cohesion of grain boundaries in the Ni--Fe--Cr system

Aljoscha Felix Baumann, Reyhaneh Ghassemizadeh, Daniel F. Urban

Grain boundaries (GBs) in polycrystalline Ni-based alloys strongly influence mechanical strength and fracture behavior, yet the effect of alloying elements such as Fe and Cr on GB cohesion remains difficult to quantify systematically. Using density functional theory (DFT), we compute rigid-body separation (RBS) curves for six symmetric-tilt Ni GBs, extracting the work of separation $W_{\mathrm{sep}}$ and maximum traction $σ_{\max}$ via the universal binding energy relation (UBER). We find that substitution of Cr or Fe into the GB plane increases both quantities relative to pure Ni GBs. To overcome the size limitations of DFT, we compare several machine-learning interatomic potential (MLIP) foundation models against the DFT dataset and identify a model achieving quantitative agreement with DFT for $W_{\mathrm{sep}}$ and $σ_{\max}$. This MLIP enables systematic sampling of alloying configurations at finer concentration steps. We show that alloying-induced changes in GB cohesion depend on both, element type and local structures, revealing trends that are inaccessible with DFT alone. Extending the analysis to a ternary $\mathrm{Ni}_{56}\mathrm{Fe}_{20}\mathrm{Cr}_{24}$ composition, inspired by the principal constituents of the widely used commercial Alloy 718, shows that the binary trends provide a useful basis for interpreting the combined effect of both substitutes, while explicit ternary calculations are required to capture their interplay quantitatively. Together, these results provide a validated workflow for quantifying the effect of alloying elements on GB cohesion in multicomponent alloys. The resulting values may serve as input for continuum-scale fracture models, paving the way toward atomistically informed multiscale modeling.

We anticipate this Review will motivate future efforts in combining AI with experimental techniques to achieve unprecedented capabilities for thermal characterization of nanoscale materials and device characterization, facilitating materials discovery and electronics revolution.

Machine learning interatomic potentials and orientational defects in polymers

Arnold Kiv, Volodymyr Soloviev, Andrii Tuzhykov, Andrii Bielinskyi, Taras Kavetskyy, Nataliia Hoivanovych

Photopolymerization of acrylated epoxidized soybean oil (AESO) is a convenient model of a self-organizing process in a soft, bio-based material: ultraviolet light starts a free-radical chain reaction, the growing network releases heat, and the mobility of molecular groups changes on several time scales at once. We follow this process from two sides. Experimentally, the real and imaginary parts of the dielectric permittivity of an AESO:VDM (1:0.5) mixture with 3 mol% of the photoinitiator DMPA were recorded at 500-5000 Hz under an intermittent on/off illumination protocol, and the resulting time series were analysed with a sliding-window Shannon entropy and the Grassberger-Procaccia correlation integral. Both measures show that short light pulses at the start of curing trigger strongly correlated, collective relaxation, which fades as the network forms, leaving only localized reorientations active. We describe these reorientations as orientational defects and quantify their energetics with the MACE-OFF23 machine learning interatomic potential, using relaxed dihedral scans of five rotational modes ($α$, $α/β$, $β$, $γ$, $δ$) of an AESO fragment. Compared with density functional theory (DFT) reference profiles, MACE reproduces the shape of all five profiles (Pearson $r=0.95$-$0.98$) and the hierarchy of barriers - from 2.9 eV for the constrained main-chain rotation, through 0.54-0.81 eV for the acrylate rotations, to 0.16-0.18 eV for the methyl and 0.06 eV for the hydroxyl rotation - while underestimating the barrier heights by 3-20%. A simple Arrhenius estimate shows that the acrylate rotations are the modes closest to the frequency window of the dielectric measurement.

FamilySpace: From Emergent Chemical Semantics to Controllable Crystal Generation

Haowei Hua, Irwin King, Wanyu Lin

Latent crystal generative models typically combine a variational autoencoder (VAE) for learning atom-wise representations with a flow model that transports a simple source distribution to the learned latent distribution. However, previous studies have mainly focused on the quality of generated crystals, leaving the structure and generative utility of the atom-wise latent space largely unexplored. In this work, we show that even a vanilla crystal VAE implicitly organizes atom-wise latent representations according to elemental families, with different families occupying largely separated regions of the latent space. We term this phenomenon emergent chemical semantics. This structure suggests that sampling from a family-specific latent region can bias the decoder toward atoms from the corresponding elemental family. Building on this insight, we propose FamilySpace, a framework for controlling the elemental-family composition of generated crystals. FamilySpace assigns each elemental family a distinct source component, forming a family-indexed multi-source distribution, and trains a flow model using source-target pairs matched by family. In inference, a user-specified family template selects the source component for each atom-wise representation, enabling direct control over the family composition of generated crystals. Experiments on MP-20 and MPTS-52 show that FamilySpace achieves competitive crystal-generation performance while matching prescribed elemental-family compositions. Moreover, selected family templates increase the fraction of generated crystals predicted to be stable or metastable, demonstrating that latent-space composition control can support targeted exploration of promising chemical regions without additional optimization techniques such as reinforcement learning. Code is available at https://github.com/hhw11/FamilySpace

Shirin Sabokdast, Pedram Mirchi, Ali Rajabpour

Simon Fernbach, Lisa Mitterhuber, Egbert Zojer, Natalia Bedoya-Martínez

In this work, we investigate thermal transport in a set of thin-film layers used in modern HEMT designs. Our theoretical analysis employs three phonon-transport models of increasing sophistication, ranging from a relatively simple Debye-approximation model to a first-principles framework that incorporates Density Functional Theory (DFT)-derived harmonic and anharmonic phonon properties. To describe ternary semiconductors, alloy scattering is also considered, and to account for the properties of non-ideal, polycrystalline thin films, scattering at grain and film boundaries is taken into account. For modeling the specific samples at hand, the latter are fitted to thermal conductivity data measured with Time-Domain Thermoreflectance (TDTR). Overall, we find that the first-principles model performs best and produces the most physically consistent results. Special emphasis is placed on establishing a reliable procedure for determining intrinsic transport properties dominated by Umklapp scattering. Building on these, it is then possible to systematically add additional scattering terms to the models and assess how model-inherent factors, such as the treatment of scattering rates or the description of the phonon dispersion, affect model accuracy and overall predictive capabilities.

Edge-dislocation interactions with sub 3 nm vacancy and interstitial clusters in FeCr alloys: Mechanisms, Obstacle strength and Fate

Layatmika Sahoo, Suchandrima Das

Fe-Cr alloys harden at irradiation doses where transmission electron microscopy (TEM) shows no defects, implicating defects too small to be resolved. Their obstacle strength is unknown, so hardening models ignore them and fit measured hardening to visible defects alone. Here, molecular dynamics drives an edge dislocation through relaxed 1-3 nm vacancy and mixed Fe-Cr interstitial clusters in Fe-3Cr, Fe-5Cr and Fe-10Cr at 300 K. Vacancy clusters relax into platelets whose strength rises with size to about 350 MPa at 2.9 nm, following strong-obstacle scaling independently of Cr content, and always survive dislocation passage. Interstitial clusters relax into $\frac{1}{2}\langle 111 \rangle$, mixed or $\langle 100 \rangle$ loops. They are mostly weaker than platelets of the same size, their strength varies up to fivefold with the local Cr arrangement, and most are absorbed. Only $\langle 100 \rangle$ loops in Fe-5Cr and Fe-10Cr often survive. During pinning, Cr collects at the junction between these loops and the dislocation; atom tracking for one loop shows that more than half comes from the loop itself. Surviving loops are not stronger than absorbed ones: strength and survival are thus distinct. Computed dispersed-barrier strengths ($α\approx$ 0.02-0.25) are upper bounds that lie below the $α$ values obtained in earlier studies by fitting hardening to TEM-visible loops. These earlier values are therefore inflated by TEM-invisible defects. Wherever small vacancy clusters form, they are more effective obstacles than interstitial clusters of the same size. These defect-specific strengths and fates provide a physical basis for models of low-dose hardening.

Benchmarking the model against 109 experimentally reported quaternary-to-senary compositions produced through diverse processing routes yields an overall classification accuracy of 80%. Analysis of ternary, quaternary, and quinary RCCAs reveals that higher-order miscibility is strongly inherited from constituent binary subsystems, indicating the importance of choosing suitable binary interactions when designing RCCAs. Element-specific trends further show that Ti, Ta, Nb, and Mo generally stabilize solid solutions, whereas Cr and Zr promote ordered precipitation and phase segregation. Hf, W, and V, meanwhile, enable tuning between these regimes. These results establish a composition-space framework for the targeted design of RCCAs with robust solid-solution stability or tunable multiphase microstructures.

Twenty diverse candidates (2.22-2.84 mmol H$_2$ g$^{-1}$) were then selected by a multi-objective ranking that also accounts for raw-material price, supply resilience and potential environmental and health impacts. The approach links materials discovery with practical deployment constraints, and the methodology is transferable to other chemical looping processes.

Generating grain boundary structures at a specified energy with a symmetry-constrained diffusion model

Kazuya Miyamoto, Ibuki Okuda, Izumi Takahara, Teruyasu Mizoguchi

Grain boundaries (GBs) govern many properties of polycrystalline materials, and inverse design would make the GB structure itself a design variable. Crystal generative models such as MatterGen operate on periodic cells, which contain two GBs. The cell energy defines a single GB only when the two are equivalent, but existing models do not enforce this equivalence. Building on MatterGen, we developed a symmetry-constrained diffusion model in which the two GBs are related by a symmetry operation: atoms near one GB are generated, and those near the other are reconstructed through a symmetry projection applied at every denoising step. We applied it to the $Σ5(210)[001]$ symmetric tilt GB in Al, conditioning generation on the GB energy from a machine-learning interatomic potential (MLIP). All 350 generated structures kept a geometrically equivalent GB pair after relaxation. Given a target GB energy, the model generated structures at that energy: for the three in-range targets with many nearby training structures, 44% to 70% fell within $\pm 0.1$ J/m$^2$ of the target. Tracking weakened at higher energies and failed outside the training range. Of the 91 near-target structures, 90 matched no training structure under the adopted geometric criterion, indicating that the model can propose configurations near the target energy that are distinct from the training structures, a capability that could help identify unknown metastable GB structures observed in experiments. Building GB-pair equivalence into generation thus makes GB properties well-defined design targets, enabling inverse design of GB structures.

Technical Note on Matlantis PFP v9: Extending the Element and Domain Coverage of the r2SCAN Training Data

Daiki Shintani, Shigeru Iwase, Jun Oshiki, Kohei Shinohara, Nontawat Charoenphakdee, Makoto Hiramatsu

Universal machine learning interatomic potentials (uMLIPs) make atomistic simulations possible at scales beyond the reach of density functional theory (DFT). Matlantis PFP is a uMLIP that treats a variety of domains, including crystals, molecules, and surfaces, with a single model. PFP v8 incorporated a training dataset computed with the r2SCAN functional, chosen for its good agreement with experiment, but the elements and domains covered by that dataset were limited. Here we present Matlantis PFP v9, in which the training data computed with the r2SCAN functional are extended to the lanthanides from Pr to Lu and to the elements from Po to Cm, for a total of 96 elements, and to additional domains such as clusters and adsorption structures. The formation energies of crystals containing the newly supported elements reproduce experimental standard enthalpies of formation with the same level of accuracy as crystals composed only of previously supported elements, and the adsorption energies of molecules on transition-metal surfaces have an overall error against experiment comparable to that of DFT-PBE, although chemisorbed species are overbound. On the same type of GPU (A100), the inference speed and the maximum system size exceed those of SevenNet-Omni, a public uMLIP trained on r2SCAN data: forces are evaluated three to four times faster, and five to ten times as many atoms can be handled. As a result, PFP-R2SCAN is now applicable to systems containing rare-earth and actinide elements and to adsorption on surfaces.
