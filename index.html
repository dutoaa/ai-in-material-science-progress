Challenging Atomistic Foundation Models on Disordered Carbons

Joseph Hart, Samuel G. H. Brookes, Fabian L. Thiemann, Christoph Schran

Foundation machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) promise broad transferability, yet their out-of-domain reliability is hard to establish. Here, we use pure and O/H-containing disordered carbon spanning multiple phases, competing hybridisations and porous frameworks to stress-test six popular foundation MLIPs. We first screen all six on density functional theory (DFT) energy and force errors and binding curves, then subject four to melt-quench-anneal molecular dynamics and evaluate the resulting structural observables. Binding curves and O/H-containing trajectories reveal unphysical extrapolation and dynamical failures that low DFT energy and force errors fail to reveal. Of the models tested, MACE-MH-1 is the most consistently robust, with low DFT errors and stable MD. Despite differing architectures, OMAT-based models share structural biases, highlighting the importance of training coverage. All four models generate structures of nanoporous carbon that reproduce the principal experimental and specialised carbon potential signatures. Incorporating O/H leaves short-range order largely unchanged but shifts pore-size distributions towards larger pores. These results emphasise the need for application-specific validation and extend molecular dynamics modelling of nanoporous carbon towards experimentally realistic structures.

Stabilization of Ferroelectric Domains via Interfacial Strain in HoFeO3- BaTiO3 Nanofiber Heterostructures for Enhanced Hybrid Energy Harvesting

Smita Chaturvedi, Swathi Krishna, Shivshankar Jokare, Supriya Sahoo, Vikash Kushwaha, Priyank Shyam

Piezoelectric nanogenerators (PENGs) are an attractive platform for self-powered mechanical-energy harvesting, but their output is often limited by depolarization fields, charge leakage, and inefficient stress transfer to the active dipoles, particularly in polymer-dominated composites. Here we report HoFeO3-BaTiO3 (HFO-BTO) nanofiber heterostructures prepared by alternating sol-gel electrospinning, in which orthorhombic HoFeO3 (Pbnm) and tetragonal BaTiO3 (P4mm) coexist in intimate contact. Rietveld-refined X-ray diffraction, high-resolution transmission electron microscopy, and Raman spectroscopy indicate interfacial lattice distortion. Polarization-electric field loops show that the heterostructure retains a polarization comparable to that of BaTiO3 (16.39 vs 15.80 microcoulombs per square centimeter at zero field) while its switching-current maximum moves to a higher field (about 7 vs about 3.5 kilovolts per centimeter), consistent with strain-stabilized ferroelectric domains. When the nanofibers are encapsulated in polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) and excited under cyclic impact, the device produces a hybrid output: the piezoelectric response of the strained BaTiO3 is supplemented by a triboelectric contribution from contact electrification. The HFO-BTO device delivers open-circuit voltage peaks of about +6 volts and -8 volts and a power density of 24.31 microwatts per cubic centimeter at a 4.7 megaohm load. This is 2.4 times the value for an identically fabricated BaTiO3 device (10.00 microwatts per cubic centimeter) and 8.7 times that of the HoFeO3 device (2.78 microwatts per cubic centimeter). These results link interface-driven lattice distortion to improved electromechanical output and identify all-oxide nanofiber heterostructures as a promising route to lead-free energy harvesting.

Concurrent two-scale topology optimization of printable bone scaffolds via rectified-flow generation and exact latent-space refinement

Reza T Batley, Sourav Saha

Bone scaffolds must reconcile a macroscale mechanical response with microscale geometric feasibility, yet conventional approaches to their design restrict attainable constitutive properties. This work presents a concurrent two-scale topology optimization framework; a conditional rectified flow generates periodic lattice microstructures, which are then refined in latent space by exact finite-element homogenization sensitivities. The resulting unit-cell geometries and their spatial assignments are jointly optimized under mechanical and feature-size constraints. In a distal rabbit femur benchmark, all eight flow-started refinements produce printable cells with maximum relative errors under 1\% across six prescribed engineering moduli. Constructing the implant from a single tiled cell reproduces intact whole-femur compliance within 0.1\% and regional strain energy within 0.35\% under four loading conditions. In human femoral reconstruction, multiscale concurrent optimization produces a cortical mid-shaft scaffold with porosity 63\% while satisfying the compliance constraint within 2\% under walking and stair-climbing loads. It achieves an 84\% porosity on a proximal cancellous scaffold with strain-energy deviations below 0.25\% under the same loads. Analysis of the learned representations reveals distinct architectural families in latent space. Further, the attention heads of the rectified-flow transformer specialize for spatial shifts -- learning the analogue of a finite-difference stencil -- and coordinate symmetries, with ablations showing their influence on generated topologies. These results establish a route to printable, mechanically constrained scaffold designs. The framework is general enough for extending to many other multiscale structural design applications including topology-microstructure co-design in metal additive manufacturing.

Autonomous adaptive spectral analysis with an LLM agent for self-driving laboratories

Yuki K. Wakabayashi, Yui Ogawa

Self-driving laboratories accelerate materials exploration through autonomous closed-loop integration of synthesis, characterization, and next-experiment selection. However, characterization does not always directly provide the target property; measured spectra or images often require automated analysis to extract quantitative descriptors based on physically motivated assumptions and predefined criteria. Such procedures can fail for measurements outside these criteria, requiring a decision on whether a reliable value can be recovered or no numerical value should be reported. Here, we implemented an autonomous adaptive spectral-analysis agent using a large language model (LLM) to supervise a deterministic numerical-analysis routine. For each spectrum, the agent executes the predefined analysis script and assesses the result using the measured spectrum and fitting diagnostics. Valid results are accepted unchanged, recoverable failures are reanalyzed through bounded adjustments of predefined fitting parameters, and NaN (not a number) is returned when no scientifically defensible value can be obtained. We evaluated the workflow on optical transmittance spectra from 203 epitaxial beta-Ga2O3 films grown during a previously reported self-driving thin-film growth campaign. The predefined optical analysis script returned Urbach energy, an optical-quality metric, for 183 spectra and failed for 20. The agent retained those 183 values and evaluated 20 exception cases, recovering 16 through bounded parameter adjustments and returning NaN for 4 when the spectral evidence did not support a reliable Urbach tail. The audit records captured spectral features, parameter changes, and rationale for each decision. These results demonstrate autonomous scientific exception handling by an LLM agent while leaving quantitative property extraction to deterministic numerical analysis.

Ilya V. Chepkasov, Alexander V. Shapeev, Alexander G. Kvashnin

Active learning (AL) addresses this challenge by closing the loop between candidate generation, data selection, reference calculation, and model updating. Here, we survey more than seventy studies, with an emphasis on work from 2020 to 2026, to understand how active learning has evolved from a niche, on-the-fly training technique for query-by-committee and Gaussian process force fields into a diverse family of data-efficient strategies. he reviewed examples include on-the-fly molecular dynamics, active data selection for fine-tuning pretrained models, kernel- and representation-based acquisition using frozen pretrained models, and reaction-aware sampling for chemical systems far from equilibrium. We explore the use of active learning in elemental and alloy mechanics, hydrogen and ion diffusion, ferroelectrics and effective Hamiltonians of functional materials, reactive and photochemical dynamics, high-entropy and compositionally disordered materials, as well as generative and autonomous material discovery pipelines. Drawing on a growing body of critical, methodologically self-aware literature, we also show that this achievement carries systematic caveats. Uncertainty estimates are not interchangeable across model architectures and are frequently the least reliable exactly where they matter most. We close by discussing the outlook for standardized, uncertainty-calibrated, and observable-targeted active-learning workflows in the foundation-model era.

Atomic-level study of dose-dependent lattice thermal conductivity degradation in irradiated tungsten

Mary K Chessey, José Julio Gutiérrez Moreno, Sajjan Singh Mehta, Paolo Settembri, Arda Erbasan, Dominik Freinberger

Plasma-facing components (PFCs) in future fusion reactors must withstand extreme environments, making tungsten (W) a prime candidate material due to its high thermal conductivity, high melting temperature, low sputtering yield, low tritium absorption. However, neutron irradiation introduces complex nanostructural defects that severely degrade these critical properties. In this work, we use Non-Equilibrium Molecular Dynamics (NEMD) simulations coupled with the Creation Relaxation Algorithm (CRA) to investigate the lattice thermal conductivity (kappa) of W across a wide range of irradiation doses. Systems treated with different doses up to 1.8 canonical displacements per atom (cdpa) were simulated to generate and study diverse defect structures, including vacancies and self-interstitial atom (SIA) clusters. By correlating spatially-resolved temperature gradients with local defect concentrations, we show that even small changes in defect type and distribution significantly affect thermal transport. We employ Machine Learning (ML) models to develop a quantitative relationship between the defect features and the resulting thermal conductivity degradation. The ML analysis identifies vacancy density as the dominant structural factor controlling thermal transport. We found a significant, nonlinear suppression of kappa with increasing defect density. Predictive multi-defect mapping built upon our ML model resolved a step-like degradation transition at a critical vacancy threshold of 0.05 at.%, beyond which phonon scattering completely saturated and capped the maximum relative lattice thermal conductivity degradation at approximately 30 at.%. This integrated simulation and ML approach provides a powerful, predictive framework for quantifying the complex relationship between structure and thermal conductivity in irradiated fusion materials.

SIESTA-QCOMP: Bridging Classical and Quantum Computing for Strongly Correlated Systems

Yann Pouillon, Federico Marchesin, Ivano Tavernelli, Emilio Artacho

Density Functional Theory (DFT) is the established workhorse of computational materials science, yet it faces fundamental challenges with strongly correlated electrons, such as in transition metal complexes and bond-breaking processes. While quantum computing offers a theoretically exact path to resolve these problems, current hardware limitations prohibit fully quantum simulations of realistic material systems. To bridge this gap, we present SIESTA-QCOMP, a modular suite embedding quantum methodologies within DFT. Harnessing SIESTA's localised basis set, we implement a projection-based embedding scheme that treats the bulk environment classically while mapping the strongly correlated "impurity" subspace to a quantum processor. The architecture uses a socket-based driver to orchestrate workflows between the Fortran-based SIESTA engine and Python-based frameworks like Qiskit, ensuring high interoperability. We detail the hybrid workflow, currently utilising a Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), and discuss the road map toward methods such as Sample-based Quantum Diagonalisation (SQD). We illustrate the framework on an hypothetical iron porphyrin calculation and argue that such hybrid architectures are not merely interim solutions but essential foundations for integrating quantum utility into materials design, as envisioned by our broader QCOMP4DFT initiative.

Beyond the training data, the MLIP also identified the formation pathway for an additional di-interstitial configuration and a stepwise divacancy dissociation mechanism. These results reveal migration-assisted defect complex formation mechanisms and demonstrate how targeted MLIP training can identify reaction pathways.
