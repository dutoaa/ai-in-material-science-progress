UniHam: A Large-Scale SOC-Complete Dataset and Benchmark for Hamiltonian Learning in Materials

Yuewen Huang, Pin Chen, Yutong Lu

Accurate prediction of electronic Hamiltonians would enable broad property inference while avoiding the high computational cost of Density Functional Theory (DFT). However, progress toward general-purpose materials foundation models is limited by a data bottleneck: existing Hamiltonian datasets are typically small, lack structural diversity, and often omit essential relativistic physics such as spin--orbit coupling (SOC). We therefore construct UniHam, a large-scale Hamiltonian dataset and benchmark suite comprising 100,000+ DFT-computed complex-valued Hermitian Hamiltonians with full SOC, covering 72 elements and a wide range of crystal geometries and symmetries (spanning diverse lattice types and space-group families). Building on UniHam, we benchmark two representative state-of-the-art models under a standardized protocol and introduce complementary evaluation metrics that jointly assess three dimensions: (i) Hamiltonian reconstruction accuracy, (ii) out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization across composition/symmetry shifts, and (iii) the ability to support downstream property prediction from the predicted Hamiltonians. Experiments on UniHam demonstrate that the proposed benchmark and metrics effectively differentiate model capabilities, revealing intrinsic SOC- and element-dependent failure modes, large variations in compositional OOD robustness, and the necessity of spectral-level evaluation to assess whether Hamiltonian predictions reliably support downstream electronic-structure properties. Overall, UniHam provides a reproducible, SOC-complete benchmark that can sharpen model comparisons and accelerate the development of next-generation foundation models for quantum materials.

Strain-dependent Dynamics of Interactions of Iodine-vacancy pairs in CsPbI3: A DFT and Machine-Learning Molecular Dynamics Study

Donggeun Kang, Seongju Lee, Yonggoon Jeon

Iodine-vacancy migration and association are important factors in the structural stability of halide perovskites. Although neighboring vacancies have been suggested to form energetically favorable pairs, their stability and reconfiguration under finite-temperature lattice deformation remain unclear. Here, we combine machine-learning molecular dynamics (MLMD) and density functional theory (DFT) to investigate neighboring iodine vacancies in tilted CsPbI3 under isotropic and biaxial strain. The MLMD trajectories reveal a nonmonotonic dependence of short-time hopping fractions on strain, with strong isotropic compression favoring point-like separation and tension promoting pair-preserving relay hopping. Notably, biaxial compression produces distinct orientation-dependent responses: vacancy pairs initially aligned with the compressed plane preferentially undergo separation, whereas those perpendicular to the plane predominantly exhibit relay hopping. Complementary static calculations using DFT and the SevenNet-0 potential yield repulsive interactions for all configurations examined. These findings highlight the importance of finite-temperature dynamics and strain-dependent local geometry in understanding vacancy-pair stability and reconfiguration in halide perovskites.

Progressive observations calibrate some downstream responses and destabilize earlier accurate predictions. MatScope establishes measurable targets for developing reliable scientific reasoning in future self-evolving materials laboratories and materials-aware world models.

MOTIF: LLM-Defined Material Taxonomies for OOD Property Prediction via Invariant Feature Selection

Gwangseon Jang, Sieun Yun, Dong Ho Lee, Kyong-Ha Lee, Chanuk Lim, Jin Woo Choi

Out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization remains a central challenge in materials property prediction, where models must extrapolate to novel chemistries beyond the training distribution. While large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong scientific reasoning, their direct use for quantitative prediction remains limited. To address this, we systematically evaluate multiple roles for LLM assistance, finding that only taxonomy-based grouping consistently improves OOD generalization over a strong kNN+GBT baseline. Building on this, we here propose MOTIF, a framework that leverages LLMs not as predictors, but as generators of candidate taxonomies that partition materials into chemically meaningful environments. These taxonomies are evaluated via training-only pseudo-OOD validation, and only those that improve generalization are retained. Invariant feature selection is then applied within each accepted taxonomy, followed by gated aggregation across taxonomies. We evaluate on 2D halide perovskites, where bandgap prediction must generalize across distinct organic spacer families. On this benchmark, MOTIF reduces mean OOD MAE from 0.122 to 0.111 eV. Under a frozen dev/test protocol, the deployable configuration further improves errors from 0.123 to 0.117 eV (electronic bandgap) and from 0.181 to 0.173 eV (optical bandgap). Beyond this primary task, we validate transferability across additional materials datasets, including 3D perovskite solar cell efficiencies and Matbench benchmarks with structure-based splits. Across domains, MOTIF consistently improves robustness under distribution shift, outperforming expert-defined groupings, rule-based heuristics, and clustering baselines, while remaining competitive with strong classical selection methods. These results demonstrate that LLMs are most effective not as predictors, but as tools for discovering structure in data.

Neural network scaling and generalization for 3D strain fields in heterogeneous materials

Lucas Fourel, Paul Devianne, Marine Debouvry, Tristan Destruhaut, Russell Gunn, Ashwini Gupta

Computational homogenization and coupled multiscale simulations of heterogeneous materials require repeated solution of mechanical fields on Representative Volume Elements, which becomes prohibitively expensive for high-resolution three-dimensional microstructures. Machine-learning surrogates can accelerate these computations, yet most existing approaches predict fields for fixed macroscopic loadings and provide limited guidance on how to scale network architectures for high-resolution 3D tensor-field prediction. This work presents a U-Net surrogate trained to predict the full 36-component strain localization tensor field in voxelized concrete mesostructures at resolutions up to $256^3$. Training localization tensors are generated with an FFT-based linear elastic solver, and an extensive hyperparameter optimization is performed to derive scaling rules and an accuracy-speed trade-off analysis. The results show that only network width and depth need to be scaled with problem complexity, while several conventional U-Net design choices, such as doubling channels after downsampling, are suboptimal for this problem. The final neural network achieves a Mean Absolute Error of 1.118% on both the validation and the held-out test sets, and an inference speedup of approximately 8.7x over the FFT-based solver on an Nvidia H100 GPU. Generalization experiments reveal transfer across aggregate volume fractions and highlight the importance of training on diverse morphologies. To safeguard reliability, a Maximum Mean Discrepancy metric computed on internal feature maps is proposed for detecting out-of-distribution microstructures without costly reference computations.

To overcome this challenge, we present Composable Catalytic Intelligence (CatAI), a physics-grounded modular AI framework that learns 1.8 million structure-property relations and organizes them into 30 physicochemical encoders. This reusable intermediate layer enables task-specific models to guide catalyst discovery for targeted reactions and objectives. Across benchmark tasks, CatAI achieves a 7.8% lower task-averaged prediction error than the next-best models. In parallel, it guided autonomous closed-loop optimization of a highly active Cu-based geminal-atom photocatalyst for C-O coupling reactions surpassing the traditional Ni-based systems. This approach connects property-level data to catalytic objectives, supporting physicochemical knowledge reuse across mechanistically distinct systems through computational screening and iterative model-experiment feedback.

Universal Multichannel Coherence-Mediated Thermal Transport in Carbon Allotropes: Experimental Evidence and Scaling Laws

A. Jeżowski, A. I. Krivchikov, O. A. Korolyuk, V. V. Sagan, Y. V. Horbatenko, S. V. Cherednychenko

Understanding thermal transport in structurally disordered carbon materials remains a fundamental challenge because conventional models based on a single coherence contribution cannot accurately describe heat conduction over a broad temperature range. In this work, the thermal conductivity of graphene oxide (GO), thermally reduced graphene oxide (trGO), GO-hydrated fullerene composites (GO-HyFn), expanded graphite (EG), and EG-multiwalled carbon nanotube (MWCNT) composites was measured between 4.5 and 300 K. Despite substantial differences in density, porosity, graphitic ordering, and microstructure, all materials exhibit glass-like temperature dependence of thermal conductivity. The experimental results demonstrate that the conventional two-channel model, comprising a Peierls contribution and a single thermally activated coherence term, systematically fails to reproduce the observed temperature dependence. To resolve this discrepancy, we propose a generalized multichannel phenomenological model in which the coherence contribution consists of up to three Arrhenius-type channels with distinct activation energies. Depending on the structural state, two or three coherence channels are required to accurately describe the thermal conductivity. A key finding is the reproducible hierarchy of activation energies across diverse carbon allotropes, following approximately (E2 ~ 4E1 and E3 ~ (12-25)E1). While the channel amplitudes increase with material density, the activation energies remain nearly invariant, indicating universal vibrational energy scales. The proposed framework also consistently describes published data for other disordered carbon materials. These results provide experimental evidence for multichannel coherence-mediated heat transport and establish a unified phenomenological bridge between structural hierarchy with thermal transport in disordered carbon solids.

To address this, we propose a self-folding methodology based on thermally induced stresses in thin-film bimetallic composites. Pairing the shape memory alloy Nitinol (NiTi) with molybdenum or stainless steel (SS316L) exploits their differing thermal expansion coefficients to generate localized hinge bending, enabling deterministic, autonomous folding into prescribed mountain and valley configurations. These results demonstrate the feasibility of stress-induced, self-folding thin-film origami architectures built from clinically established biomaterials, providing a scalable design framework for next-generation, active neurovascular implants with integrated sensing and monitoring functionality.

Elastic Landau states as martensite nuclei in austenite

Mohd Tahseen, Vivekanand Dabade

In this article, we investigate the geometric closure of austenite--martensite interfaces. Classical compatibility conditions permit stress-free interfaces between the two phases: the condition $λ_2 = 1$ admits planar interfaces, whereas the cofactor conditions additionally admit curved interfaces. However, neither, guarantees the geometric closure of these interfaces. We identify two-parameter families of martensitic deformations that overcome this limitation and enable the formation of closed, stress-free martensite nuclei embedded in an austenite matrix. A main result is the interpretation of the classical deformation modes of simple shear and pure shear as martensitic phase transformations. These correspond to cubic to monoclinic-II and cubic to orthorhombic transformations, respectively. The former naturally satisfies the \emph{extreme compatibility conditions}, which are stronger than the cofactor conditions, and gives rise to \emph{elastic Landau states}, a self-accommodating martensitic nuclei embedded in austenite. We show that these states can be assembled to form arbitrarily shaped, stress-free martensitic islands surrounded by austenite. Such states can arise even in conventional shape memory alloys that do not satisfy supercompatibility, provided hierarchical twinning produces an average deformation that is a simple shear. The theory provides a geometric mechanism for self-accommodation. Its predicted deformation gradients agree with those reconstructed from experimentally observed microstructures. This agreement suggests that these microstructures originate from simple shear.

How anion composition governs structural frameworks and Li-ion transport in oxychloride solid-state electrolytes

Mixed-anion lithium oxychlorides exhibit high Li conductivity, but the relationship between oxygen content, structural framework, and Li-ion transport remains unclear. Here, crystal structure prediction and molecular dynamics simulations with machine-learning interatomic potentials are used to examine $\mathrm{Li}_yM\mathrm{O}_x\mathrm{Cl}_{6-2x}$ ($M$ = Nb and Ta [Group 5] and $M$ = Ti, Zr, and Hf [Group 4]). These findings establish how oxygen content controls framework and Li-ion transport, guiding the design of lithium oxyhalide electrolytes.

Quantum Embedding from Molecular to Crystalline Environments at the Cost of a Gas-Phase Calculation

Manas Sharma, Marek Sierka

We present a direct-space formulation and implementation of quantum density functional theory (DFT) embedding for molecular active subsystems embedded in frozen environments (hosts) of arbitrary dimensionality, from finite molecular hosts to extended systems. The host is computed once without the embedded molecule (the guest) and is represented only by its electron density fitted in an atom-centered auxiliary Gaussian basis together with its nuclear charges with the periodic electrostatics separated into an explicit near-field and a continuous fast-multipole far-field operator. The method allows the removal of host atoms from this representation to create vacancies or other voids without requiring a supercell creation. Analytic nuclear gradients are also implemented, enabling structural relaxation of the active subsystem. We demonstrate the versatility of the approach through applications to adsorbates on periodic surfaces and molecules embedded in bulk solvent environments. For CO on MgO(001), we show that DFT-in-DFT converges rapidly and reproduces the periodic interaction energy within 0.44 kJ/mol while reducing the cost by a factor of 27. Real-time time-dependent embedded DFT (RT-TDDFET) of methylenecyclopropene in periodic water is showcased to produce a ~0.6 eV solvatochromic blue shift, while strong-field propagation of CO2 on hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) yields substrate-induced effects at the cost of a gas-phase propagation. Coupling the embedding scheme to excited-state correlated wave function theory methods such as CC2 yields a solvent shift within 0.01 eV of supermolecular CC2 references, while reducing the computational cost by over 500 times.
