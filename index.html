Enabling large language model agents for high-fidelity many-body perturbation theory calculations

Benran Zhang, Byeongchan Lee, Zien Zhu, Chih-En Hsu, Zhenfa Zheng, Hung-Chung Hsueh

Many-body perturbation theory (MBPT) methods, including the GW approximation, the GW plus Bethe-Salpeter equation (GW-BSE) method, and GW perturbation theory (GWPT), among others, provide accurate first-principles descriptions of excited-state properties and multiparticle interactions of materials. However, their use remains limited due to complex multi-software workflows, interdependent physical parameters, and substantial theoretical expertise. Large language model (LLM) agents, which couple a language model to software tools, show potential in automating computational tasks, but MBPT remains an unaddressed challenge. We show that LLM agents tested in our work, including frontier models such as Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5, do not reliably construct MBPT workflows in one attempt, suggesting that correct setup requires expert knowledge and consistency that current models lack. Across tested model-harness combinations, out-of-the-box agents average 54%, 36%, and 19% on GW, GW-BSE, and GWPT workflow benchmarks, with strong model dependence and large run-to-run variation. Errors typically originate from cross-stage inconsistencies that surface only several stages after they are introduced. We therefore develop AI-Donkey, an agentic framework that combines expert-curated knowledge with deterministic execution to enable high-fidelity GW, GW-BSE, and GWPT calculations and analyses. With AI-Donkey, all tested agents, from frontier proprietary models to open-weight models, produce complete, runnable MBPT workflows with no observed run-to-run variation. We further demonstrate autonomous calculations and post-processing on 1D, 2D, and bulk materials, including the many-body enhancement of Fröhlich electron-phonon coupling in polar semiconductors. Our approach provides a general framework for knowledge engineering to enable reliable agentic workflows in computational materials science.

Sorption Molecular Dynamics of Point Defects in 2D Materials with a Machine Learning Potential

Nils-Ruben Skogemyr, Albin Sjöstedt, Isak Wernroth, Isak Häggqvist, Isac Ljungwald, Hans-Ove Åkesson

Defect engineering is a crucial tool for tuning and tailoring the properties of two-dimensional (2D) materials. A key challenge is identifying which defects are kinetically stable at room temperature and sufficiently promising to warrant further investigation. However, identifying stable defects with suitable properties using first-principles calculations, such as density functional theory (DFT) is computationally demanding, effectively limiting the scope of high-throughput studies. In this work, we investigate whether the machine learning interatomic potential MACE can reproduce sorption trends (adsorption vs. absorption) previously obtained from high-throughput DFT calculations and extend the analysis to elevated temperatures. A total of 17,624 defect structures from the ADAQ database are simulated using MACE-accelerated molecular dynamics (MD) at both 0 K and 300 K. The results show that MACE successfully reproduces the overall sorption trends observed in DFT calculations at 0 K at a fraction of the computational cost. Simulations performed at 300 K further indicate that the qualitative sorption trends remain largely the same when including finite-temperature effects. These findings demonstrate that MACE-driven MD can be used as an effective high-throughput prescreening tool for defect-related studies in 2D materials, specially related to sorption. While conventional first-principles methods, such as DFT, remain necessary for accurate and quantitative validation, the use of MLIPs enables MD simulations of finite-temperature defect behavior at a fraction of the computational cost of DFT-based MD.

Theoretical Investigation of Oxygen-Vacancy Diffusion in SrTiO$_3$ using Neural Network Potentials

Kazutaka Nishiguchi, Ryota Yamamoto, Seiichiro Ten-no

Oxygen-vacancy formation and diffusion play important roles in determining the electronic and atomic transport properties of SrTiO$_3$ (STO). Accurate analysis of such point defects requires sufficiently large-scale and long-time molecular dynamics (MD) simulations; however, direct investigations of oxygen-vacancy dynamics using {\it ab initio} MD (AIMD) based on density functional theory (DFT) are limited to relatively small systems and short time scales because of their high computational cost. In this study, we develop a neural network potential (NNP) for pristine and oxygen-deficient STO using a reference dataset generated from AIMD simulations. The trained NNP model accurately reproduces the DFT reference energies, atomic forces, and virials for both pristine STO and STO containing an oxygen vacancy. The NNP also reproduces the supercell-size dependence of the total energies and oxygen-vacancy formation energies, including the formation energy extrapolated to the dilute limit. Large-scale and long-time MD simulations using the NNP (NNP-MD) are then performed to investigate oxygen-vacancy diffusion. The resulting oxygen-vacancy diffusion coefficients show substantially better agreement with experimental data than those obtained from conventional classical MD simulations. An Arrhenius analysis yields an oxygen-vacancy migration enthalpy of 0.64 eV, in close agreement with the experimental value of 0.67 eV. These results demonstrate that NNPs trained on first-principles data provide an accurate and computationally efficient framework for investigating defect formation and long-time vacancy transport in perovskite oxides.

What Do Foundation Models for Market Microstructure Learn? A Controlled Study with Synthetic Limit Order Books

Fernando Acero

Autoregressive transformers trained on limit order book (LOB) message streams are increasingly proposed as foundation models for market microstructure. Because the generative process of real markets is unknown, these models can be judged only by the realism of their output, leaving open what they actually learn. We make the question answerable by inverting the setup: we train GPT-style models on order flow from simulators whose latent parameters are known, so that the Bayes-optimal predictor, the generating state, and the exact data entropy serve as references. Measured against them, next-token pretraining on order flow implements amortized Bayesian inference over market regimes. The learned predictive matches the exact Bayes optimum to within a millinat per token and approaches the oracle floor even on a nonstationary family whose latent state drifts within an episode, a regime that requires filtering. The latent regime is linearly decodable from frozen activations, well above baselines, so a frozen-model read-out calibrates simulator parameters more accurately than windowed maximum likelihood at a fraction of the cost. In our experiments these capabilities are not produced by scale: capacity and token volume saturate early, and it is the diversity of training regimes, not the token count, that determines generalization to unseen markets. On real NASDAQ data, zero-shot transfer is set by how well the simulator prior covers real flow: an enriched mixture nearly closes the gap to an in-domain baseline, and pretraining beats from-scratch training at every budget across held-out tickers. Known ground truth turns average next-token loss, which is uninformative near the entropy floor, into an exact comparison against the Bayes-optimal and oracle predictors.

Material dependence of direct light-induced charge injection from metallic nanoparticles into adsorbates

Jordan Alan Edwards, Johannes Lischner

Direct injection of charge from metallic nanoparticles into adsorbed molecules via the chemical interface damping of localized surface plasmons has emerged as a promising avenue to avoid the rapid thermalization of hot carriers. In this paper, we develop an approach for predicting hot-electron and hot-hole injection rates from large spherical nanoparticles into adsorbed carbon monoxide (CO) molecules. The approach combines a detailed description of the adsorbate-metal interactions via ab initio density functional theory with a macroscopic electrodynamics treatment of the localized surface plasmon. We apply this approach to nine different metals including both traditional (Ag, Au, Cu) and emerging (Al) plasmonic metals as well as non-plasmonic metals (Ni, Rh, Pd, Ir, Pt). For all metals, hot-electron injection is larger than hot-hole injection. This is a consequence of the alignment of the CO frontier orbitals, with the CO lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) being closer to the metal Fermi energy than the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO). The largest electron injection rates in the optical photon ranges are obtained for Al, Ag, Cu and Au, with Cu and Au showing significant plasmonic enhancement. The non-plasmonic metals show weak electron injection as a consequence of their high LUMO energies relative to the metal Fermi level. The results of our study pave the way towards a rational design of metallic nanoscale photocatalysts for specific chemical processes.

Bond-informed search for ordered configurations in multi-principal element alloys

Gaurav Arora, Alex Tai, Rodrigo Freitas, David Cereceda

Ordered configurations in multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) control their local structure and influence a broad range of mechanical and thermodynamic properties. Yet they are notoriously difficult to identify. Locating low-energy ordered candidates requires exploring a configurational space that grows combinatorially with the number of elements, and Monte Carlo searches driven by density-functional theory (DFT) need a new electronic-structure calculation at every trial step. Here we introduce MECx (Minimum Energy Configuration eXplorer), a framework that guides Monte Carlo searches with a surrogate energy model trained on configurations stratified in bond-count space. Applied to ternary, quaternary, and quinary BCC alloys using compact training sets of 100 DFT-labeled configurations, MECx identifies ordered configurations with lower energies than those found by direct DFT-based Monte Carlo within practical computational limits. These configurations exhibit distinct pair-specific ordering, including B2- and B32-like motifs, and undergo progressive disordering as temperature increases. The MPEA-trained models also transfer to selected binary subsystems without retraining. The resulting ordered configurations agree with those obtained by direct DFT-based Monte Carlo and produce experimentally distinguishable superlattice reflections in simulated neutron diffraction patterns.

We demonstrate, that the negative twin boundary energy along the entire transformations path predicted by DFT for $T=0$, supports the formation of ordered modulated martensites at finite temperatures. Our results pave the way for large-scale simulations of magnetic shape memory alloys with near-DFT accuracy.

Risk-Controlled DFT Selection for Machine-Learned Interatomic Potentials

Kun Ryu, Yeo Jin Jung, Shyue Ping Ong, Claire Donnat, Ying Shirley Meng

Automated materials discovery requires evaluating large pools of candidate configurations, with density functional theory (DFT) as a major throughput bottleneck. Pretrained machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) approximate DFT cheaply, but their errors vary by orders of magnitude across configurations, making it difficult to decide when to trust the MLIP predictions and when to fall back to DFT. We frame this decision as a risk-controlled selection problem and solve it within a conformal risk control (CRC) framework. Each configuration is mapped from atomic numbers and Cartesian coordinates to a fixed geometric descriptor, which a lightweight predictor uses to estimate an MLIP-DFT disagreement score in energy, average force, and maximum force. CRC then calibrates this score into a selection threshold for DFT labeling such that the MLIP error on the unselected configurations, which keep their MLIP labels, stays below a user-chosen fraction $α$ of the expected error. Under an identical guarantee across eight dataset-MLIP pairs, our predictor routes 7% and 14% fewer configurations to DFT at $α=0.3$ than committee baselines. Because committee disagreement overlooks errors the MLIPs share, our gains are largest when the DFT labels and MLIP training data differ in DFT functional and basis set, and narrow when the two agree. Without model access, the structure-only predictor also ranks errors as well as the target MLIP's latent representation and outperforms widely used descriptors such as Smooth Overlap of Atomic Positions (SOAP) and Atom-Centered Symmetry Functions (ACSF).

Into the Unknown: Benchmarking AI agents on open scientific research problems

Seungone Kim, Jiayi Geng, Young-Jun Lee, Bo Liu, Juyoung Suk, Nicholas Lubbers

Can AI agents carry out research on open scientific problems? We introduce Into the Unknown, a benchmark that provides an agent with a science paper along with a research direction to pursue and tasks it to write a follow-up paper. We work with scientists at US Department of Energy (DOE) national laboratories, who each contribute an unpublished follow-up direction from their own work together with the necessary conditions a submission must meet. While assessing the quality of a follow-up paper is challenging in general, using these conditions to form a rubric makes evaluation more tractable for research problems that have no reference answer. We have nine frontier agents write 15 follow-ups each, spanning quantum computing, plasma physics, computational chemistry, materials science, and scientific machine learning domains. None of the 135 submissions satisfies all the conditions, and 114 fail our integrity check, most often because the paper claims work that was never done or reports numbers that contradict the recorded evidence. Overall, agents readily produce papers that look like follow-ups, but none meets the standard of the domain scientist, leaving substantial headroom. Our code and data are publicly available.

Comprehensive Workflow for Uncertainty-Aware Ionic Liquid Solvent Discovery

Alexander Lobo, Urvi Awasthi, Leonid Zhukov

Designing synthesizable materials with targeted properties remains one of the central objectives in materials sciences. Ionic liquids (ILs) are well suited to this goal due to their "tunability", where physical and chemical properties can be precisely customized by modifying their molecular structure, specifically by varying the combination of cations and anions. At the same time, current data-driven property models for ionic liquids often degrade in scarce, noisy, and uneven data regimes, where measurements cover only a small fraction of chemical space and a limited range of thermodynamic conditions. This makes out-of-distribution screening brittle: even when point predictions look plausible, it is difficult to compare candidates consistently, quantify downstream implications, or trust rankings enough to guide molecular discovery. We present a property-prediction and optimal candidate selection workflow that treats data processing, deep learning modeling, and physics-based calculations as a coupled system, linking property prediction with uncertainty estimation and physics-based filtering. By tightening the link between predicted properties and the criteria used for down-selection, the workflow supports reliable out-of-distribution ranking and enables prioritization of previously untested ionic liquid candidates with favorable predicted trade-offs. We demonstrate the application of our workflow by designing novel ionic liquids for CO2 capture from flue gas, predicting their properties and selecting Pareto-optimal candidates based on CO2 solubility, IL viscosity and working capacity.

Trust-Limited Machine-Learning-Accelerated Nudged Elastic Band Calculations for Small-Polaron Hopping in Rutile TiO2

Ashish Kumar Singh, Irina V. Lebedeva

Nudged elastic band (NEB) calculations of small-polaron hopping are demanding because nuclear motion is coupled to a self-consistent localized electronic state. Near the charge-transfer region, slow self-consistent-field convergence, localization switching, and competing electronic solutions complicate surrogate-assisted optimization. We develop a Gaussian-process-assisted NEB workflow for this regime and demonstrate electron small-polaron hopping in rutile TiO2 using spin-polarized density functional theory with a Hubbard U correction (DFT+U ) with SIESTA. A joint energy-force Gaussian process (GP) with a Matérn covariance model advances the elastic band, while active-atom root-mean-square and maximum-atom displacement limits constrain surrogate motion relative to evaluated configurations. Reaching these limits triggers first-principles sampling without requiring full surrogate convergence. Three independently DFT-verified 96-atom [001] calculations using 11, 13, and 15 images and different surrogate settings and GP parameterizations yield barriers of 0.1645, 0.1637, and 0.1640 eV, respectively, spanning 0.793 meV. In the 15-image calculation, repeated central-image sampling activates a separately calibrated local GP during surrogate optimization. Final full-band DFT verification gives a maximum projected NEB force of 0.0346 eV/Å. A 324-atom calculation yields a lower barrier of 0.1302 eV, indicating supercell sensitivity and demonstrating feasibility at larger GP observation dimension. The workflow combines controlled surrogate extrapolation, targeted charge-transfer-region interpolation, and independent DFT verification to obtain polaron migration pathways, emphasizing reproducible barriers and full-band force convergence.

VoroTypes: automated enumeration of Voronoi cell topologies for structural identification of complex crystals in atomistic simulations

Emanuel A. Lazar, Chris H. Rycroft

VoroTypes enumerates, for an arbitrary crystal structure, the complete family of Voronoi cell topologies that can arise under small generic perturbations of atomic positions, and exports it as a topological filter for the VoroTop framework. This extends per-atom structural identification in molecular dynamics simulations, previously limited to a handful of simple reference structures, to complex crystals such as Frank--Kasper and Laves phases, with per-sublattice resolution. The package takes a standard crystallographic description (a CIF or POSCAR file) as input, applies exact symmetry reduction and validity pruning to accelerate the enumeration, and produces filters that load directly into the standalone VoroTop program or into OVITO. A library of pre-computed filters covers over a thousand structures from the Materials Project.

Whether sub-yield aging and over-yield rejuvenation in cyclically deformed amorphous solids share a common physical origin or arise from distinct microscopic mechanisms remains a fundamental yet unresolved debate. Using molecular dynamics simulations of a metallic glass, we resolve this duality by uncovering a unified dynamic clock that dictates energy landscape navigation across both regimes. Intense high-rate agitation increases the escape probability from deeper potential energy minima, thereby suppressing the retention efficiency of structural relaxation. By identifying these localized fluctuations as the primary origin of rate-attenuated sub-yield relaxation, we establish an exact scaling identity relating the relaxation attenuation exponent to the stress fluctuation exponent. This work directly bridges non-linear rheology with stress fluctuation dynamics, providing a unified framework to predict and manipulate structural evolution in amorphous solids.

Together, these results disentangle the roles of doping and alloying in lead-free DPs and establish polarization-resolved spectroscopy as a probe of STE wavefunctions.
