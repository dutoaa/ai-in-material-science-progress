GBClaw: An agentic AI framework for atomistic grain boundary research and mechanistic discovery

Yazhuo Liu, Kunqing Ding, Xiaoguo Gong, Zifeng Wang, Yichen Yang, Yifeng Che

Agentic artificial intelligence (AI) systems hold immense potential for automating complex workflows in materials discovery and design, but extending them to domain-intensive mechanistic investigations remains challenging. Here, we present GBClaw, an agentic AI framework for investigating the structure and deformation of general grain boundaries (GBs). Although general GBs constitute the vast majority of real-world interfaces in polycrystalline materials, they remain understudied because their structural complexity and interfacial mismatch complicate experimental and modeling research. GBClaw integrates large language models (LLMs) into a modular architecture comprising an orchestrator, specialized agents, domain-specific tools, simulation interfaces, and hierarchical memory. Through dynamic replanning and iterative workflow refinement, the framework effectively connects systematic simulations with targeted mechanistic analysis. Applied to 14 general asymmetric tilt GBs in nickel, GBClaw characterized interfacial defect structures and identified diverse deformation modes across these incommensurate boundaries. Comparing boundary sliding with and without secondary GB misfit dislocations revealed substantial differences in shear strength, underscoring the critical role of defect hierarchies in sliding resistance. Furthermore, researcher-guided follow-up calculations traced an anomalously high shear resistance to metastable boundary structures. This work demonstrates that combining agentic AI with domain expertise bridges high-throughput computational screening with deep physical understanding of complex material interfaces.

Multiscale Modeling of Zr Corrosion: Predicting Microstructures of Oxide Films

K. Starkey, L. Kahle, M. Christensen, V. Eyert, A. El-Azab, C. B. Geller

A first-principles-informed multiscale modeling approach is reported for oxide formation during corrosion of zirconium. The present simulations reproduce the columnar corrosion film microstructure and grain size distribution seen in micrographs of oxidized Zr surfaces. This structural feature emerges from a hexagonal ZrO layer adjacent to the $α$-Zr/ZrO$_{2-x}$ interface, which is dynamically stabilized by continuous heterogeneous grain nucleation and grain boundary mass diffusion. This ZrO layer is identified as a significant oxygen diffusion barrier and a potential contributor to sub-parabolic pretransition corrosion kinetics. The simulations also reproduce the undulatory nature of the metal/oxide interface, leading to regions with tensile stress components capable of nucleating cracks, despite the overall state of compressive hydrostatic stress within the oxide film. First principles density functional theory (DFT) computations of fundamental ZrO$_x$ properties were combined with molecular dynamics simulations using a machine-learned potential based on a DFT training set to provide extensive input to an advanced phase field model for oxide formation and its microstructure, as implemented in the new MedeA PhaseField module. The phase field simulations reported herein incorporated computed stoichiometry-dependent thermochemical data, anisotropic elastic properties, interface energies, bulk and grain boundary mass transport processes, multiphase heterogeneous grain nucleation, and strain energy-dependent phase stabilities.

Challenging Atomistic Foundation Models on Disordered Carbons

Joseph Hart, Samuel G. H. Brookes, Fabian L. Thiemann, Christoph Schran

Foundation machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) promise broad transferability, yet their out-of-domain reliability is hard to establish. Here, we use pure and O/H-containing disordered carbon spanning multiple phases, competing hybridisations and porous frameworks to stress-test six popular foundation MLIPs. We first screen all six on density functional theory (DFT) energy and force errors and binding curves, then subject four to melt-quench-anneal molecular dynamics and evaluate the resulting structural observables. Binding curves and O/H-containing trajectories reveal unphysical extrapolation and dynamical failures that low DFT energy and force errors fail to reveal. Of the models tested, MACE-MH-1 is the most consistently robust, with low DFT errors and stable MD. Despite differing architectures, OMAT-based models share structural biases, highlighting the importance of training coverage. All four models generate structures of nanoporous carbon that reproduce the principal experimental and specialised carbon potential signatures. Incorporating O/H leaves short-range order largely unchanged but shifts pore-size distributions towards larger pores. These results emphasise the need for application-specific validation and extend molecular dynamics modelling of nanoporous carbon towards experimentally realistic structures.

CrystalAR: Autoregressive Modeling for Symmetric Crystal Generation

Ziqiao Meng, Rongsheng Chen, Zhi Zheng, Nikita Kazeev, Zhenkun Wang, Andrey Ustyuzhanin

Deep generative modeling has achieved remarkable progress in crystal generation. Given the rich symmetries inherent in crystalline structures, recent work has sought to explicitly incorporate space-group symmetry into generative models, enabling more effective exploration of novel symmetry templates. Yet introducing symmetry templates increases the combinatorial complexity of stoichiometric relationships between atomic species and crystallographic sites, while creating a fundamental tension between flexible exploration of discrete crystallographic templates and faithful realization of these templates as continuous three-dimensional structures. To address these challenges, we propose CrystalAR, a unified autoregressive framework that models Wyckoff symmetry templates and complete crystal structures within a single generative sequence. Specifically, CrystalAR reformulates continuous coordinate generation as a symmetry-conditioned hierarchy of discrete spatial localization decisions, autoregressively refining the positions of all symmetry-inequivalent sites across resolution levels while jointly modeling their spatial configuration within each level. This discrete hierarchical formulation further enables constraint-aware search over both crystallographic templates and geometric realizations. As a result, CrystalAR retains strong symmetry-template generation capability while achieving competitive de novo generation performance and template-free crystal structure prediction (CSP) accuracy. These results suggest that symmetry-template generation can be retained alongside broadly competitive de novo generation performance and competitive CSP without reliance on symmetry templates retrieved from prior data, providing a promising alternative paradigm for symmetry-preserving crystal generation.

Concurrent two-scale topology optimization of printable bone scaffolds via rectified-flow generation and exact latent-space refinement

Reza T Batley, Sourav Saha

Bone scaffolds must reconcile a macroscale mechanical response with microscale geometric feasibility, yet conventional approaches to their design restrict attainable constitutive properties. This work presents a concurrent two-scale topology optimization framework; a conditional rectified flow generates periodic lattice microstructures, which are then refined in latent space by exact finite-element homogenization sensitivities. The resulting unit-cell geometries and their spatial assignments are jointly optimized under mechanical and feature-size constraints. In a distal rabbit femur benchmark, all eight flow-started refinements produce printable cells with maximum relative errors under 1\% across six prescribed engineering moduli. Constructing the implant from a single tiled cell reproduces intact whole-femur compliance within 0.1\% and regional strain energy within 0.35\% under four loading conditions. In human femoral reconstruction, multiscale concurrent optimization produces a cortical mid-shaft scaffold with porosity 63\% while satisfying the compliance constraint within 2\% under walking and stair-climbing loads. It achieves an 84\% porosity on a proximal cancellous scaffold with strain-energy deviations below 0.25\% under the same loads. Analysis of the learned representations reveals distinct architectural families in latent space. Further, the attention heads of the rectified-flow transformer specialize for spatial shifts -- learning the analogue of a finite-difference stencil -- and coordinate symmetries, with ablations showing their influence on generated topologies. These results establish a route to printable, mechanically constrained scaffold designs. The framework is general enough for extending to many other multiscale structural design applications including topology-microstructure co-design in metal additive manufacturing.

MOFFieldBench: A Machine Learning Benchmark for Continuous Electronic Fields in Metal-Organic Frameworks

Zhaolin Hu, Hehe Fan, Chenhao Rao, Wangyihan Guo, Meng Xu, Qiwei Yang

Machine-learning models for metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) predominantly rely on atomic structures, whereas electron-density (ED) and electrostatic-potential (ESP) fields remain underexplored as scalable learning representations. These continuous fields encode complementary spatial and electronic information relevant to structure-property relationships, particularly pore geometry and guest-framework interactions, but costly first-principles calculations, storage requirements and the scarcity of systematically aligned datasets limit their broader use. Here we introduce MOFFieldBench, a multimodal benchmark comprising approximately 46,000 MOFs with one-to-one aligned crystallographic structures, ED fields and ESP fields. A unified workflow integrates field generation, error-controlled compression and sparse point-cloud representation, supporting field reconstruction, prediction of 13 material properties, multimodal property prediction and pairwise cross-modal retrieval. ED and ESP provide competitive representations for pore geometry, adsorption and separation properties. Multimodal fusion improves several prediction tasks relative to the best unimodal reference, supporting complementary predictive roles for atomic structures and electronic fields. Learned ED reconstruction closely approximates first-principles reference densities, while predicted-density initialization reduces self-consistent-field calculation time by 13.59\% on average in paired tests. These results support electronic fields as practical, informative learning modalities and establish MOFFieldBench as a resource for studying representation choice, multimodal learning and electronic-field-driven MOF modelling.

Autonomous adaptive spectral analysis with an LLM agent for self-driving laboratories

Yuki K. Wakabayashi, Yui Ogawa

Self-driving laboratories accelerate materials exploration through autonomous closed-loop integration of synthesis, characterization, and next-experiment selection. However, characterization does not always directly provide the target property; measured spectra or images often require automated analysis to extract quantitative descriptors based on physically motivated assumptions and predefined criteria. Such procedures can fail for measurements outside these criteria, requiring a decision on whether a reliable value can be recovered or no numerical value should be reported. Here, we implemented an autonomous adaptive spectral-analysis agent using a large language model (LLM) to supervise a deterministic numerical-analysis routine. For each spectrum, the agent executes the predefined analysis script and assesses the result using the measured spectrum and fitting diagnostics. Valid results are accepted unchanged, recoverable failures are reanalyzed through bounded adjustments of predefined fitting parameters, and NaN (not a number) is returned when no scientifically defensible value can be obtained. We evaluated the workflow on optical transmittance spectra from 203 epitaxial beta-Ga2O3 films grown during a previously reported self-driving thin-film growth campaign. The predefined optical analysis script returned Urbach energy, an optical-quality metric, for 183 spectra and failed for 20. The agent retained those 183 values and evaluated 20 exception cases, recovering 16 through bounded parameter adjustments and returning NaN for 4 when the spectral evidence did not support a reliable Urbach tail. The audit records captured spectral features, parameter changes, and rationale for each decision. These results demonstrate autonomous scientific exception handling by an LLM agent while leaving quantitative property extraction to deterministic numerical analysis.

Active Learning for Interatomic Potentials: A Review of Uncertainty-Driven Data Efficiency

Ilya V. Chepkasov, Alexander V. Shapeev, Alexander G. Kvashnin

Developing a machine learning interatomic potential (MLIP) that is accurate for the specific conditions under which it will be applied, rather than simply for the predefined configurations chosen by the model developer, is a significant challenge in atomistic simulation. Active learning (AL) addresses this challenge by closing the loop between candidate generation, data selection, reference calculation, and model updating. Here, we survey more than seventy studies, with an emphasis on work from 2020 to 2026, to understand how active learning has evolved from a niche, on-the-fly training technique for query-by-committee and Gaussian process force fields into a diverse family of data-efficient strategies. he reviewed examples include on-the-fly molecular dynamics, active data selection for fine-tuning pretrained models, kernel- and representation-based acquisition using frozen pretrained models, and reaction-aware sampling for chemical systems far from equilibrium. We explore the use of active learning in elemental and alloy mechanics, hydrogen and ion diffusion, ferroelectrics and effective Hamiltonians of functional materials, reactive and photochemical dynamics, high-entropy and compositionally disordered materials, as well as generative and autonomous material discovery pipelines. Drawing on a growing body of critical, methodologically self-aware literature, we also show that this achievement carries systematic caveats. Uncertainty estimates are not interchangeable across model architectures and are frequently the least reliable exactly where they matter most. We close by discussing the outlook for standardized, uncertainty-calibrated, and observable-targeted active-learning workflows in the foundation-model era.

LOB-Impact Benchmark: Emergent Market Impact in Generative Limit Order Book Models

George Nigmatulin, Valentin Mohl, Sascha Frey, Kang Li, Aramis Marti-Shahandeh, Mihai Cucuringu

Realistic market impact is essential for generative limit order book (LOB) models to serve as world models for trading agents. If a simulator misprices this mechanism, agents learn policies that are optimal in the simulator but wrong in the real market. Existing metrics assess generated streams purely through distributional comparison with historical data, leaving counterfactual response to an agent's own orders largely unexplored. Here, we show that market impact emerges naturally in neural LOB generators trained solely for next-token prediction. We inject execution-style metaorders into the generated message flow of five autoregressive models across five NASDAQ stocks and measure the response using estimators from the empirical impact literature, validated on baselines whose physics is known in advance. Our benchmark evaluates any message-level generator in three phases: free generation (reading order-flow persistence against price diffusivity); metaorder injection (yielding the impact law's exponent and amplitude); and a post-execution window (measuring relaxation). In addition, comparing each visible injection to a blind, simulator-only control isolates the model's learned response from standard book mechanics. For every model on every stock, we measure how the impact responds as a function of cumulative metaorder participation. Interestingly, we observe emergent impact in generative models of market data, with exponents in the range of 0.63 to 1.21, slightly above the traditional 0.5 of the square-root law of impact. In the family of models tested, we are so far unable to observe impact decay during relaxation. We tentatively attribute this to the still-short context windows of the models. We release the benchmark as open source to validate future generative models for market data.

Reaction-induced Localized Annealing for On-chip Materials Discovery

Nensi Toncich, Nerea Abando, Henning Galinski, Frans Spaepen, Ralph Spolenak

On-chip thermal processing is a key technology for materials discovery across all disciplines of the natural sciences. Here, we present a microfabricated heat-delivery platform that enables reaction-induced localized on-chip annealing up to 1400°C. A buried reactive multilayer combined with a nanometric diffusion barrier provide ultra-fast heating and quenching rates on the order of 10$^6$°C s$^{-1}$ as illustrated by the generation of ultra-high aspect ratio microstructures during rapid-solidification and the stabilization of dopant-free tetragonal zirconia at room temperature. Rapid kinetics also enable the observation of transient states of capillary-driven hole collapse in melts. Our work expands localized high-temperature processing routes and offers a material-independent approach for rapid screening of multidimensional nanomaterials on and off-equilibrium.

Stabilization of Ferroelectric Domains via Interfacial Strain in HoFeO3- BaTiO3 Nanofiber Heterostructures for Enhanced Hybrid Energy Harvesting

Smita Chaturvedi, Swathi Krishna, Shivshankar Jokare, Supriya Sahoo, Vikash Kushwaha, Priyank Shyam

Piezoelectric nanogenerators (PENGs) are an attractive platform for self-powered mechanical-energy harvesting, but their output is often limited by depolarization fields, charge leakage, and inefficient stress transfer to the active dipoles, particularly in polymer-dominated composites. Here we report HoFeO3-BaTiO3 (HFO-BTO) nanofiber heterostructures prepared by alternating sol-gel electrospinning, in which orthorhombic HoFeO3 (Pbnm) and tetragonal BaTiO3 (P4mm) coexist in intimate contact. Rietveld-refined X-ray diffraction, high-resolution transmission electron microscopy, and Raman spectroscopy indicate interfacial lattice distortion. Polarization-electric field loops show that the heterostructure retains a polarization comparable to that of BaTiO3 (16.39 vs 15.80 microcoulombs per square centimeter at zero field) while its switching-current maximum moves to a higher field (about 7 vs about 3.5 kilovolts per centimeter), consistent with strain-stabilized ferroelectric domains. When the nanofibers are encapsulated in polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) and excited under cyclic impact, the device produces a hybrid output: the piezoelectric response of the strained BaTiO3 is supplemented by a triboelectric contribution from contact electrification. The HFO-BTO device delivers open-circuit voltage peaks of about +6 volts and -8 volts and a power density of 24.31 microwatts per cubic centimeter at a 4.7 megaohm load. This is 2.4 times the value for an identically fabricated BaTiO3 device (10.00 microwatts per cubic centimeter) and 8.7 times that of the HoFeO3 device (2.78 microwatts per cubic centimeter). These results link interface-driven lattice distortion to improved electromechanical output and identify all-oxide nanofiber heterostructures as a promising route to lead-free energy harvesting.

Learning the Geometry of Materials Space with Trained Neural Tangent Kernels

Daniel Kaplan, Gabriel Kotliar, Anirvan M Sengupta

Neural networks trained to predict materials properties learn representations whose physical content can be difficult to extract. We use the neural tangent kernel (NTK), evaluated at trained parameters, to recover the geometry learned by a network that predicts superconducting transition temperatures from elemental composition alone. Kernel ridge regression with the trained NTK closely reproduces the network's predictive performance and substantially outperforms regression with the initial kernel. Spectral analysis and multidimensional scaling reveal a geometry dominated by a small number of directions. Clustering and local neighborhood analysis organize compounds into recognizable superconducting families and connect materials with related chemical and correlated-electron properties despite substantial differences in $T_c$. These results establish trained tangent kernels as practical probes of learned materials representations, connecting property prediction with the exploration of materials space.

Ammonia-Hydrogen Phase Equilibria in Planetary Envelopes from First-Principles-Based Machine-Learning Molecular Dynamics

Akash Gupta, Austin Guo, Jie Deng

Ammonia and hydrogen are major constituents of planets within and beyond the solar system, yet how they interact in planetary envelopes is poorly known. We perform large-scale two-phase coexistence simulations of the ammonia-hydrogen system from 500 to 4000 K and 0.1 to 100 GPa, driven by machine-learning interatomic potentials trained on density functional theory calculations. From these we map the compositions of coexisting hydrogen-rich and ammonia-rich phases, and the critical curve that separates conditions of complete miscibility from those of phase separation. The critical temperature increases with pressure, from 500 K at 0.1 GPa to 2000 K near 30 GPa, and we find no phase separation at 3000 and 4000 K up to 100 GPa. The slope of the critical curve decreases substantially between 0.3 and 30 GPa, and the curve nearly flattens above about 30 GPa and 2000 K, where molecules begin to give way to atomic species. The coexistence curves are asymmetric with an ammonia-rich critical composition. For the expected envelope compositions, the phase boundary lies close to, and intersects, published thermal profiles of Uranus and Neptune, implying ammonia may be raining out in these planets today. In contrast, in warm sub-Neptunes such as TOI-270 d, ammonia and hydrogen are likely miscible in all proportions. Together with previous studies, our results suggest that silicates, water and ammonia separate from hydrogen in sequence as planets cool, with implications for their compositional, thermal and structural evolution.

Atomic-level study of dose-dependent lattice thermal conductivity degradation in irradiated tungsten

Mary K Chessey, José Julio Gutiérrez Moreno, Sajjan Singh Mehta, Paolo Settembri, Arda Erbasan, Dominik Freinberger

Plasma-facing components (PFCs) in future fusion reactors must withstand extreme environments, making tungsten (W) a prime candidate material due to its high thermal conductivity, high melting temperature, low sputtering yield, low tritium absorption. However, neutron irradiation introduces complex nanostructural defects that severely degrade these critical properties. In this work, we use Non-Equilibrium Molecular Dynamics (NEMD) simulations coupled with the Creation Relaxation Algorithm (CRA) to investigate the lattice thermal conductivity (kappa) of W across a wide range of irradiation doses. Systems treated with different doses up to 1.8 canonical displacements per atom (cdpa) were simulated to generate and study diverse defect structures, including vacancies and self-interstitial atom (SIA) clusters. By correlating spatially-resolved temperature gradients with local defect concentrations, we show that even small changes in defect type and distribution significantly affect thermal transport. We employ Machine Learning (ML) models to develop a quantitative relationship between the defect features and the resulting thermal conductivity degradation. The ML analysis identifies vacancy density as the dominant structural factor controlling thermal transport. We found a significant, nonlinear suppression of kappa with increasing defect density. Predictive multi-defect mapping built upon our ML model resolved a step-like degradation transition at a critical vacancy threshold of 0.05 at.%, beyond which phonon scattering completely saturated and capped the maximum relative lattice thermal conductivity degradation at approximately 30 at.%. This integrated simulation and ML approach provides a powerful, predictive framework for quantifying the complex relationship between structure and thermal conductivity in irradiated fusion materials.

Early-stage local melting during solid-state sintering of a Cu-Ni-Ti-Zr-Hf alloy: A combined thermodynamic and kinetic analysis

Andrea Skolakova, Hana Lesakova, Frantisek Lukac, Marketa Jarosova, Jan Pinc

Complex concentrated alloys (CCAs) combining elements from 3d transition and refractory groups, specifically Cu-Ni-Ti-Zr-Hf-based shape memory systems, exhibit excellent functional properties but present significant manufacturing challenges via powder metallurgy. In this study, a non-equimolar Cu15Ni35Ti25Zr12.5Hf12.5 (at. %) alloy was successfully produced by induction melting, followed by atomization and subsequent consolidation via Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS) at temperatures ranging from 850 to 1100 C. Although global equilibrium phase diagrams predict solid-state stability up to 1260 C, localized melting unexpectedly occurred during SPS at 1100 C. By combining retrospective Scheil-Gulliver solidification simulations with 2D local equilibrium heat maps, we demonstrate that this early-stage melting is not driven by macroscopic thermal gradients, but by microsegregation inherited from atomization. During solid-state heating, thermally activated short-range diffusion, accelerated by high defect densities at particle interfaces, partitions Ti towards the interparticle network, leaving Cu-enriched regions (up to ~41 at. %) within the particle interiors. Owing to the strongly negative mixing enthalpy of Cu with Hf (-19.2 kJ/mol for $ΔH_{mix}^{l-l}$) and, to a lesser extent, with Ti, the local solidus of these Cu-rich regions is dramatically depressed. Upon heating to 1100 C, these regions cross their local solidus and form a liquid phase, whose solidification produces the Cu-Hf co-segregation. This framework accounts for localized melting phenomena in rapidly solidified CCA powders containing low-melting constituents with strong negative mixing enthalpies.

SIESTA-QCOMP: Bridging Classical and Quantum Computing for Strongly Correlated Systems

Yann Pouillon, Federico Marchesin, Ivano Tavernelli, Emilio Artacho

Density Functional Theory (DFT) is the established workhorse of computational materials science, yet it faces fundamental challenges with strongly correlated electrons, such as in transition metal complexes and bond-breaking processes. While quantum computing offers a theoretically exact path to resolve these problems, current hardware limitations prohibit fully quantum simulations of realistic material systems. To bridge this gap, we present SIESTA-QCOMP, a modular suite embedding quantum methodologies within DFT. Harnessing SIESTA's localised basis set, we implement a projection-based embedding scheme that treats the bulk environment classically while mapping the strongly correlated "impurity" subspace to a quantum processor. The architecture uses a socket-based driver to orchestrate workflows between the Fortran-based SIESTA engine and Python-based frameworks like Qiskit, ensuring high interoperability. We detail the hybrid workflow, currently utilising a Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), and discuss the road map toward methods such as Sample-based Quantum Diagonalisation (SQD). We illustrate the framework on an hypothetical iron porphyrin calculation and argue that such hybrid architectures are not merely interim solutions but essential foundations for integrating quantum utility into materials design, as envisioned by our broader QCOMP4DFT initiative.
