Synthetic Crystal Data Generation for Diffusion Pretraining in Material Property Prediction

Aamod Varma, Shuyi Jia, Victor Fung

Diffusion-based pretraining has emerged as a powerful strategy for learning transferable representations in graph neural networks for materials property prediction without requiring quantum-mechanical labels such as energies, forces, and stresses for every pretraining structure. However, the method retains a critical dependency: it requires structures residing near local minima of a potential energy surface, which have traditionally been obtained through costly DFT relaxations of known or physically synthesized compounds. This reliance constrains both the scale and the structural diversity of available pretraining data, leaving large regions of crystal structure space unexplored. In this work, we connect diffusion-based pretraining with synthetic structure generation to reduce this bottleneck. We introduce a synthetic data generation pipeline combining three complementary strategies: perturb-and-relax, generative sampling via MatterGen, and protostructure enumeration. Where structural relaxation is required, we use fast machine-learned interatomic potentials instead of performing a new DFT relaxation for each generated structure. We further compare a coordinate-denoising control with continuous-time variance-preserving (VP) and sub-VP diffusion objectives using a randomly initialized Orb-v2 backbone. The source comparison covers seven MatBench property-prediction tasks, and an eighth task, \texttt{mp\_gap}, is included in the protostructure scaling study. Every synthetic-pretraining condition numerically outperforms random initialization on all seven source-comparison tasks. Scaling the protostructure corpus from 20k to 165k under a fixed 400-epoch schedule numerically matches or exceeds MP-20k pretraining on most tasks; because this protocol increases both data diversity and optimization updates, the result reflects joint data-and-compute scaling.

Physics-Guided Crystal Structure Elucidation from Powder X-Ray Diffraction with Universal Machine-Learning Interatomic Potentials

Yunsung Lim, Seungwoo Hwang, Seungwu Han

Determining unique atomic coordinates from powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) remains challenging because peak overlap, weak scattering from light elements, limited scattering contrast between elements with similar atomic numbers, and experimental noise render the inverse problem underdetermined. Here, we present XCAPE (XRD-Constrained Accelerated Prediction Engine), a crystal structure prediction (CSP) framework that integrates experimentally derived composition, unit cell parameters, space group, and PXRD data into a symmetry-aware evolutionary search. XCAPE explores Wyckoff-site configurations using tailored mutation operations, evaluates candidates by both energetic stability and diffraction agreement, and accelerates geometry optimization using a universal machine-learning interatomic potential (MLIP). The framework recovered 71 of 77 database-derived benchmark structures (92.2%) from simulated PXRD patterns and all 12 reference structures from experimental PXRD patterns. It further identified 12 candidate structure assignments that are consistent with experimental PXRD patterns, lack matching entries in the Inorganic Crystal Structure Database (ICSD), and are thermodynamically competitive at the MLIP level. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations showed that 10 of these 12 candidates lie within 10 meV/atom of the convex hull. By bridging experimental diffraction data and physically plausible atomistic models, XCAPE can provide structural insight into previously unresolved materials and support accelerated materials discovery in automated research workflows.

Weight-Space Programming for Materials Science

Sk Md Ahnaf Akif Alvi, Raymundo Arróyave

A materials discovery campaign often begins with almost no measurements of the property it is searching for, and the models that rank candidates or screen the output of a generator then have to be built from those first few labels. Following recent work on weight-space programming, in which a language model writes the weights of a small network directly as text, we show that a general-purpose model, given the task in words, the names of the inputs and a network of 16 hidden units with every weight set to zero, can provide this starting point by filling in all of the weights from what it has learned about materials. With no labels, the networks of the strongest model rank experimental alloys at Spearman co-efficient = 0.64 to 0.79, where the best of five simple descriptors reaches 0.47 to 0.62. They rank the bulk modulus of crystals added during generative discovery loops at 0.95, where the inverse volume per atom reaches 0.89, and 35 phase-equilibrium targets computed with Thermo-Calc at a mean of 0.67, close to the 0.70 of an extrapolation from calculated binary and ternary data. As the prior mean of a Gaussian process they raise the ranking at five labels from 0.35 to 0.69 on alloys and from 0.15 to 0.95 on crystals, and they lower the regret of Bayesian optimization significantly on 4 of 5 experimental tasks and on 13 to 16 of 33 computed targets, depending on how multiple tests are corrected, without raising it significantly on any. The same networks also serve as the mean of a Bayesian neural network and as a screen for the samples of two crystal generators, where they do as well as standard models trained on 400 labeled structures. The gain is confined to the first tens of labels and to targets that no rule or sub-system extrapolation already ranks.

Machine learning interatomic potentials and orientational defects in polymers

Arnold Kiv, Volodymyr Soloviev, Andrii Tuzhykov, Andrii Bielinskyi, Taras Kavetskyy, Nataliia Hoivanovych

Photopolymerization of acrylated epoxidized soybean oil (AESO) is a convenient model of a self-organizing process in a soft, bio-based material: ultraviolet light starts a free-radical chain reaction, the growing network releases heat, and the mobility of molecular groups changes on several time scales at once. We follow this process from two sides. Experimentally, the real and imaginary parts of the dielectric permittivity of an AESO:VDM (1:0.5) mixture with 3 mol% of the photoinitiator DMPA were recorded at 500-5000 Hz under an intermittent on/off illumination protocol, and the resulting time series were analysed with a sliding-window Shannon entropy and the Grassberger-Procaccia correlation integral. Both measures show that short light pulses at the start of curing trigger strongly correlated, collective relaxation, which fades as the network forms, leaving only localized reorientations active. We describe these reorientations as orientational defects and quantify their energetics with the MACE-OFF23 machine learning interatomic potential, using relaxed dihedral scans of five rotational modes ($α$, $α/β$, $β$, $γ$, $δ$) of an AESO fragment. Compared with density functional theory (DFT) reference profiles, MACE reproduces the shape of all five profiles (Pearson $r=0.95$-$0.98$) and the hierarchy of barriers - from 2.9 eV for the constrained main-chain rotation, through 0.54-0.81 eV for the acrylate rotations, to 0.16-0.18 eV for the methyl and 0.06 eV for the hydroxyl rotation - while underestimating the barrier heights by 3-20%. A simple Arrhenius estimate shows that the acrylate rotations are the modes closest to the frequency window of the dielectric measurement.

Orientation-Tunable Interfacial Thermal Conductance in Lateral MoSe2/MoS2 Heterostructures: A Deep Potential Molecular Dynamics Study

Shirin Sabokdast, Pedram Mirchi, Ali Rajabpour

Managing heat flow across interfaces in lateral 2D transition-metal dichalcogenide (TMD) heterostructures is a key challenge for next-generation nanoelectronic and optoelectronic devices. To tackle this, we trained a deep learning interatomic potential (DLP) using DeePMD-kit on density functional theory (DFT) data, then paired it with non-equilibrium molecular dynamics (NEMD) simulations to study interfacial thermal conductance (ITC) in lateral MoSe$_2$/MoS$_2$ heterostructures, considering both armchair and zigzag interface orientations. The potential we trained closely matches DFT energies, forces, and phonon dispersion relations, which confirms its reliability for thermal transport simulations. Examining cell lengths from 10 to 40 nm and extrapolating to infinite length, the armchair interface shows a higher ITC (473 MW/m$^2$K) than the zigzag interface (387 MW/m$^2$K), and the armchair interface remains higher at every length considered. Both increase further as temperature rises. At 300 K, the vibrational density of states shows greater phonon spectral overlap at the armchair interface than at the zigzag interface. Together, these results offer a solid machine-learning framework for studying heat transport in lateral TMD heterostructures, and identify interface orientation as an effective means of controlling heat dissipation at these junctions.

Choosing the Right Representations: Task-Aware Fusion for Multimodal Materials Language Models

Huu Binh Ta, Alex Morehead, N. Benjamin Erichson, Ferdinando Fioretto, Sandeep Madireddy

Multimodal language models for materials science rely on effective mechanisms for incorporating three-dimensional structural representations. MatterChat addresses this challenge using a large bridge module between a pretrained structural encoder and the LLM, together with a separate contrastive alignment stage. We instead ask whether this bridge can adaptively select and combine representations from multiple structural encoders according to the target property. We find that supervised and generative material encoders provide complementary information for property prediction. We found that, for Zatom-1, this information is not concentrated in the final layer. Instead, the most informative representations occur at different depths depending on the target property. This motivates Task-Conditioned Attention Fusion (TCAF), a lightweight attention-based bridge that uses task-conditioned latent queries to combine CHGNet features with intermediate Zatom-1 representations. The bridge is trained directly on downstream prediction tasks, allowing representation selection and material-language alignment to be learned jointly without separate contrastive pretraining. Across classification and regression tasks, TCAF outperforms MatterChat, with up to 47.8% lower RMSE, while using substantially fewer trainable parameters and halving the overall training time. Further experiments incorporating UMA-S demonstrate TCAF's flexibility across encoder combinations, with the three-encoder configuration achieving the highest macro-average classification accuracy of 89.61%.

IANN: A Unified Framework for Equivariant InterAtomic Neural Networks with User-Friendly Implementation

Changzhi Ai, Xueping Qin, Dimosthenis Sokaras, Frank Abild-Pedersen, Johannes Voss

Machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) now approach density functional theory (DFT) accuracy at a fraction of its cost, but the practical benefit is limited by software fragmentation: each state-of-the-art architecture is released as a separate codebase, with its own data format, configuration system, training loop and molecular-dynamics interface. Comparing two architectures on the same dataset, or moving a trained model into a production simulation, therefore requires substantial re-engineering. We introduce IANN (InterAtomic Neural Network), an open-source PyTorch framework that reimplements equivariant graph neural networks - PaiNN, NequIP, MACE, Allegro, EquiformerV2, EquiformerV3 and UMA - behind a unified data object, a unified trainer and a single deployment route, so that the architecture is a one-word change in a configuration dictionary. Forces, stresses and virials are obtained for every architecture from one analytic differentiation of the energy with respect to the edge vectors of the atomic graph, exact under periodic boundary conditions. Distributed training is configured automatically from the SLURM, PBS or MPI environment, so the same script runs unchanged from a workstation to a multi-node GPU allocation. Self-describing checkpoints serve directly as ASE calculators, as ensembles reporting uncertainties, or as TorchScript models run inside LAMMPS through bundled C++ pair styles with single- and multi-GPU inference. Trained on a common dataset of millions of structures, the architectures can be compared under identical conditions. We further release PaiNN foundation models trained on 41.9 million curated DFT structures from MPtrj, Alexandria, OMat24, MatPES, OC20, OC22, OC25, MPr2SCAN and MatPESr2SCAN, spanning the PBE, RPBE and r2SCAN functionals. Additionally, IANN provides an agent interface to facilitate integration with autonomous agents.

Daiki Shintani, Shigeru Iwase, Jun Oshiki, Kohei Shinohara, Nontawat Charoenphakdee, Makoto Hiramatsu

Universal machine learning interatomic potentials (uMLIPs) make atomistic simulations possible at scales beyond the reach of density functional theory (DFT). Matlantis PFP is a uMLIP that treats a variety of domains, including crystals, molecules, and surfaces, with a single model. PFP v8 incorporated a training dataset computed with the r2SCAN functional, chosen for its good agreement with experiment, but the elements and domains covered by that dataset were limited. Here we present Matlantis PFP v9, in which the training data computed with the r2SCAN functional are extended to the lanthanides from Pr to Lu and to the elements from Po to Cm, for a total of 96 elements, and to additional domains such as clusters and adsorption structures. The formation energies of crystals containing the newly supported elements reproduce experimental standard enthalpies of formation with the same level of accuracy as crystals composed only of previously supported elements, and the adsorption energies of molecules on transition-metal surfaces have an overall error against experiment comparable to that of DFT-PBE, although chemisorbed species are overbound. On the same type of GPU (A100), the inference speed and the maximum system size exceed those of SevenNet-Omni, a public uMLIP trained on r2SCAN data: forces are evaluated three to four times faster, and five to ten times as many atoms can be handled. As a result, PFP-R2SCAN is now applicable to systems containing rare-earth and actinide elements and to adsorption on surfaces.

Application of Pretrained Universal Machine Learning Force-Fields for Phase Transitions Prediction in ZrO2

Rajan Khadka, Axel van de Walle, Patrick Tepesch

Pretrained universal machine-learning force fields can reduce the cost of finite-temperature molecular dynamics relative to density functional theory. However, most benchmarks test only static properties and do not assess whether these models reproduce the correct phase-transition sequence. Here, we benchmark ten open-source universal machine-learning force fields for zirconia (ZrO$_2$) using phonon dispersion calculations and variable-cell isothermal-isobaric molecular dynamics. Zirconia is a useful test because it has three thermodynamically stable crystalline phases prior to melting, following the sequence monoclinic-tetragonal-cubic. Four models reproduce this sequence: 7net-l3i5, MatterSim-v1.0.0-1M, MACE-mpa, and MACE-D3. Models with orthorhombic-tetragonal energy errors above about 10 meV/atom (7net-0, CHGNet, and MACE) instead produce an orthorhombic intermediate and predict monoclinic-orthorhombic-tetragonal-cubic-liquid upon heating. ORB-based models fail to sustain stable variable-cell dynamics because their phonon frequencies are compressed by 30-60% relative to the density functional theory reference. Across all ten models, two ground-state diagnostics predict finite-temperature behavior. Relative polymorph energetics should agree with the matched density functional theory reference within about 6 meV/atom, and the phonon spectrum should not show systematic softening. These diagnostics are practical screening metrics for assessing pretrained force fields for phase-transition predictions.

DSADiff: Data Structure-Aware Diffusion for Crystal Structure Prediction via Mitigating Complexity- and Imbalance-Induced Biases

Jiang Xiaobo, Dinghong Lai

Crystal Structure Prediction (CSP) is an important step in accelerating the generation and discovery of new materials. In recent years, diffusion-based CSP methods have made significant progress. However, existing models still suffer from generation bias when facing diverse materials spaces, while systematic approaches for tracing the sources of such performance variations remain limited. To address this issue, we propose LAS (Layered Analysis Strategy), a mechanism analysis framework that identifies crystal complexity and elemental composition imbalance as two key factors affecting generation difficulty and further reveals their relationships with model generation performance. Building on these findings, we further propose DSADiff (Data Structure-Aware Diffusion), a data structure-aware diffusion model that transforms the key factors identified by LAS into a unified generation difficulty representation and uses it to jointly drive feature modeling and training strategy optimization. This enables the model to adapt its representation and generation capability to samples with different data structures and difficulty levels, thereby mitigating learning bias across samples. Experiments on three CSP benchmark datasets, including Carbon-24, demonstrate that DSADiff consistently improves both Match Rate and RMSE, with a maximum Match Rate improvement of 9%, surpassing existing state-of-the-art methods and achieving new state-of-the-art performance. This work deepens the understanding of diffusion-based CSP models from the perspective of generation bias and provides a new direction for designing less biased generative models for complex materials spaces.

Adsorbate-Free Screening of Dissociative Water Adsorption on Nickel Nanoparticles via Graph Neural Networks

Marta Monti, Alec Noppe, Olivier Lugier, Maria B. Brands, Bernd Ensing

Dissociative water adsorption is a key step in heterogeneous and electrochemical catalysis, but on finite Ni nanosystems the OH$^*$ and H$^*$ fragments can occupy many combinations of surface sites, making exhaustive density functional theory (DFT) screening impractical. We introduce a graph neural network (GNN) framework with an adsorbate-free representation: candidate O- and H-binding sites are defined by sets of Ni atoms (\textit{anchors}), and each site pair is encoded as a graph constructed solely from these anchors and their local Ni environment. Using only MACE-relaxed bare Ni geometries as input, separate GNNs predict the DFT-reference adsorption energies of OH$^*$+H$^*$ configurations and whether the proposed binding sites would be retained upon DFT relaxation, eliminating the need to construct or relax adsorbates during screening. Trained across Ni nanoclusters and Ni(111)/Ni(100) surfaces, the energy model achieves a mean absolute error of 0.031 eV on the internal validation set and demonstrates transferability to unseen Ni$_{55}$, Ni$_{91}$, and Ni$_{130}$ clusters and larger surface cells, particularly for relative-energy ranking, although quantitative accuracy varies across systems. These independent tests also support the retention classifier's use as a conservative filter for selecting configurations likely to preserve their proposed binding sites. Application to Ni nanoparticles containing up to $\sim$3000 atoms identifies favorable co-adsorption motifs and shows that lower O-site coordination generally stabilizes the dissociated state, whereas H-site coordination has a weaker influence. The framework thus offers an efficient route to screening adsorption energetics, with potential extensions to other metals and adsorbate combinations.

Nahual: A Sequence Model for Language and Atoms

Austin H. Cheng, Marcel Müller, Andreas Burger, Yeonghun Kang, Tsz Wai Ko, Marta Skreta

Generative models play a rapidly growing role in the design and simulation of molecules. The most widely used applications of generative models are in large language models (LLMs), owing to the natural flexibility of language for specifying and answering queries. However, generalist LLMs do not effectively handle large sets of 3D coordinates, whereas specialist generative models of 3D molecules cannot reach the controllability afforded by language. To bridge these capabilities, we propose Nahual, a decoder-only autoregressive diffusion model for any sequence of text and 3D molecules. To enable expressive autoregression on continuous coordinates, we propose next-token diffusion, whereby a causal transformer denoises each token while attending to the clean tokens before it, without increasing context size. We train Nahual on a curated set of twelve 3D chemistry tasks ranging from microsolvation to molecular conformer search to adsorption on metal surfaces, with associated evaluation metrics and baselines. The result is a single set of end-to-end-trained weights that simultaneously performs all tasks and surpasses state-of-the-art models specialized for crystal structure prediction and property-conditional molecule generation. Our results demonstrate the viability of scaling generalist decoder-only models for native multimodal understanding and generation across language and chemistry. Code is available at https://github.com/the-matter-lab/nahual.

Structure and Thermal Conductivity of Epoxy-Graphene Oxide Composites from 4.5 to 300 K

On the Matbench Discovery leaderboard, models with three to five layers approach the accuracy of deeper models, and one basis, truncated along the Pareto front of error and speed, spans more than two orders of magnitude in cost.
