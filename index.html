PerovAgent: Autonomous Ligand Design for Halide Perovskite Nanocrystals

Seungjun Cha, Yoo Jae Jeong, Amanda Conde Del Moral, Juan-Pablo Correa-Baena, Seth R. Marder, David S. Ginger

Designing surface-capping ligands for high-performance halide perovskite nanocrystals requires navigating a vast molecular space while discovering design rules that govern ligand-surface interactions. Here, we introduce PerovAgent, an autonomous multi-agent framework that combines large language model (LLM)-driven scientific reasoning with computational tools for atomistic model construction, machine-learned interatomic potential evaluation, and statistical validation. Across ten independent campaigns on CsPbBr3 surfaces, PerovAgent adaptively explored and refined diverse ligand chemistries while progressively improving the binding-energy objective. Beyond optimization, the framework converts recurring chemical observations into explicit design principles that can be statistically evaluated. Of 35 formulated principles, 19 were prospectively supported, of which seven also passed the confound audit, highlighting the need to systematically validate plausible LLM-generated hypotheses during autonomous discovery. A recurrent alkynyl-phosphonate principle was further examined using density functional theory, which reproduced the predicted energetic preference. These results demonstrate an autonomous discovery framework that not only searches molecular space and optimizes the scalar objective but also transforms its search trajectory into systematically testable design principles. The framework can also be extended to more complex ligands, including zwitterionic, multidentate, and polymerizable ligands, to explore a wider range of ligand-surface interactions and uncover new design principles for halide perovskite nanocrystals.

Graph Neural Networks for Accurate Quasiparticle Energy Levels in Large Supercells of Transition Metal Dichalcogenides

Rafael R. Del Grande, David A. Strubbe

The GW method is the one of the main workhorses of the materials science community to accurately calculate the electronic bandgap of materials, as it overcomes the gap underestimation problem from Density Functional Theory (DFT). Despite its accuracy, its computational cost becomes prohibitive when calculations have more than 10-100 atoms, which makes more difficult its use for defects, surfaces and moiré systems that demand large supercells. To overcome this problem, we developed a Graph Neural Network trained with smaller GW calculations which can make accurate predictions on large supercells at a small fraction of the computational cost. Our model uses as features the projection of DFT wavefunctions into localized atomic orbitals, while graph nodes represent the atomic positions and edges represent atom to atom distances, which captures the system geometry. We explore how to do "small AI", providing a workflow of how to create a personal and convenient dataset of GW calculations to train the model that can be applied to different materials so that the final user does not need to rely on massive datasets. We define what must be included in the training dataset to capture the physics of our objective simulation, and perform a Bayesian optimization of hyperparameters. In the case of monolayer MoS$_2$ with a sulfur vacancy our GNN models achieve high accuracy (MAE ranging 10 to 50 meV) with a simplified workflow applicable to other materials, accelerating accurate large electronic structure calculations.

Effects of Yttrium doping on Proton Conductivity in Ba(Fe, Co, Zr, Y)O$_{3-δ}$ Cathode Materials for Proton Ceramic Fuel Cells

Chiyoung Kim, Ryan Jacobs, Jun Meng, Jack H. Duffy, Kyle S. Brinkman, Yueh-Lin Lee

Protonic ceramic fuel cells (PCFCs) offer efficient energy conversion at intermediate temperatures, but their performance is often constrained by sluggish oxygen reduction kinetics at the cathode. Triple conducting perovskite materials such as BaCo$_x$Fe$_y$Zr$_z$Y$_{1-x-y-z}$O$_{3-δ}$ (BCFZY) have shown promise as air electrodes. In this study, we investigated the impact of Y doping on proton conductivity by decoupling the effects on proton diffusivity and proton concentration. Using ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations, we assessed proton tracer diffusivity across three compositions: BaCo$_{0.4}$Fe$_{0.4}$Zr$_{0.2}$O$_{3-δ}$ (BCFZ), BaCo$_{0.4}$Fe$_{0.4}$Zr$_{0.1}$Y$_{0.1}$O$_{3-δ}$ (BCFZY0.1) and BaCo$_{0.4}$Fe$_{0.4}$Y$_{0.2}$O$_{3-δ}$ (BCFY). Thermodynamic defect models incorporating hydration, oxidation, and hydride formation reactions are employed to estimate temperature-dependent proton concentrations. Our results suggest that increasing the Y content from BCFZ to BCFY increases proton diffusivity by 1.7% at 500 $^\circ$C. Under PCFC operating gas conditions, proton concentration and proton conductivity are predicted to increase by 3.8% and 5.3%, respectively, at 500 $^\circ$C. These increases are modest compared with the larger enhancements reported experimentally under reducing conditions, highlighting the importance of gas conditions in evaluating the effects of Y doping. Under low oxygen partial pressure conditions, we predict that the dominant proton transport mechanism shifts from hydroxyl groups (OH$_O^{\bullet}$) to hydride species (H$_O^{\bullet}$). This work provides mechanistic insight into proton transport in BCFZY cathodes and offers design principles for optimizing proton-conducting air electrodes in next-generation PCFCs.

DeepDOSReasoner: Physics-Grounded Reasoning for Density-of-States Prediction and Materials Discovery

Yingheng Wang, Francesco Ricci, Zhilong Wang, Tao Yu, Junwen Bai, Xiangyun Lei

Predicting a material's behavior from its atomic structure is a longstanding goal of the physical sciences. The electronic and phonon densities of states (eDOS and phDOS) govern key electronic, optical, vibrational, and thermodynamic properties, yet their calculation using density functional theory (DFT) is costly: about 60% of Materials Project entries lack eDOS and over 80% lack phDOS. Existing crystal-to-spectrum models rely on pooled structural representations and pointwise regression, which blur spectral features and violate total-state conservation. Here we introduce DeepDOSReasoner, a physics-grounded framework that decodes spectra through atom-to-energy source attention and refines the normalized DOS through learned continuity-equation transport, enforcing nonnegativity and total-state conservation by construction. DeepDOSReasoner achieves the highest prediction accuracy across all evaluated metrics for both eDOS and phDOS, recovers element-resolved contributions without supervision, and most reliably identifies rare valence-band (VB) gaps. In a prospective screen of 8,106 uncharted materials validated by new DFT calculations, its predictions remain the most accurate among the methods evaluated, with 25 of its top 50 candidates confirmed as VB-gap materials. Three case studies spanning substituted compounds, photovoltaic chalcogenides, and a high-entropy alloy demonstrate transfer across materials classes. Ablations confirm that both stages are necessary, while the model retains comparable accuracy when trained on only 25% of the data. Predicting spectra in milliseconds enables exhaustive screening; we release predictions filling the missing eDOS for 91,405 and phDOS for 127,768 Materials Project entries. DeepDOSReasoner provides a blueprint for integrating machine learning with physical reasoning toward high-throughput spectral screening and autonomous materials discovery.

Foundation interatomic potentials capture structural disorder in metallic glasses

Dario Massa, Luca Leoni, Antoni Wadowski, Kacper Cybinski, Anshul D. S. Parmar, Carmine Autieri

Foundation machine-learning interatomic potentials transfer well across chemical and structural space, yet their reliability for highly disordered metallic states is less established. Metallic glasses are a demanding benchmark: they have traditionally been modeled with Embedded Atom Method (EAM) potentials fitted to specific compositions, while ab initio molecular dynamics remains too expensive at the required sizes and timescales. Using the prototypical Cu-Zr-Al glass, we show that foundation potentials capture structural disorder, reproducing the structure, quenching dynamics, and elastic, mechanical and thermal properties of the amorphous state at near first-principles accuracy. We benchmark MACE foundation models against a classical EAM potential along molecular dynamics quenches, validating against density functional theory and experiment, first at Cu46Zr46Al8 and then across the good glass-forming region of the ternary. We further test two specialization routes: fine-tuning on glass structures generated with Lennard-Jones surrogate models, and on automatically generated small periodic cells (ASSYST). Models pre-trained on databases that include non-equilibrium liquid and amorphous configurations, such as OMAT, are accurate and transferable across composition out of the box, clearly outperforming the composition-specific EAM potential. Fine-tuning on target glass structures brings only modest gains, while specialization on small crystalline cells degrades the description of disorder. The readiness of foundation potentials for disordered metals is therefore set mainly by the diversity of their pre-training data rather than by system-specific fine-tuning, shifting the modeling effort from bespoke dataset generation to pre-training coverage and careful validation of the resulting dynamics.

Strain-Induced Tetragonal-to-Cubic Transition in MAPbI$_3$ from Machine-Learned Molecular Dynamics

Daan Holleman, Luuk Klein Nagelvoort, Monica Morales-Masis, Linn Leppert

Metal-halide perovskites are promising photovoltaic absorbers whose optoelectronic properties are highly tunable. Epitaxial strain offers a route to stabilize desirable phases, tune band gaps, and reduce energetic disorder, but rational strain engineering requires predictive models. Static, zero-temperature "strained-bulk" density functional theory (DFT) calculations, successful for oxide perovskites, neglect the dynamic disorder and anharmonicity that govern halide perovskites at ambient temperature. Here, we show that molecular dynamics (MD) based on machine-learned force fields trained on-the-fly on DFT reproduces the structural and optoelectronic response of methylammonium (MA) lead iodide (MAPbI$_3$) to epitaxial strain at 300 K. We simulate bulk and free-standing slab models across experimentally accessible biaxial strains by locking the in-plane lattice vectors in nanosecond-long simulations. We predict a tetragonal-to-cubic transition at $\sim$0.5% tensile strain, marked by $c/a = 1$ and the loss of the $a^0a^0c^-$ tilting pattern, in agreement with experiments on epitaxial MAPbI$_3$ films on KCl. MA cations retain preferential orientations in the strain-induced cubic phase, unlike in its temperature-induced counterpart. Bulk and slab models yield consistent trends, with surface effects decaying within a few octahedral layers. Band gaps computed as ensemble averages over MD snapshots blue-shift under tensile strain, consistent with experiment, whereas static calculations on averaged structures predict a red shift. Our study positions MD with constrained lattice vectors as a method for predicting finite-temperature strain phase diagrams and guiding the epitaxial design of perovskite absorbers.

Lattice Thermal Conductivity of MgO and the MgSiO$_3$ Polymorphs Bridgmanite and Post-Perovskite at Lower-Mantle Conditions Using Fine-Tuned MACE Potentials

Amrendra Kumar Tiwari, Sandro Jahn

Heat flow across the core-mantle boundary (CMB) depends on the lattice thermal conductivity ($κ_{\mathrm{latt}}$) of lowermost-mantle minerals, yet calculated CMB values for MgO differ severalfold, and high-temperature estimates of the conductivity increase across the bridgmanite-post-perovskite transition range from about 20 to 50%. Direct solutions of the linearized phonon Boltzmann transport equation (LBTE) require thousands of density functional theory (DFT) force evaluations per thermodynamic state. Here we replace these evaluations with phase-specific, fine-tuned MACE potentials for MgO, MgSiO3 bridgmanite (Pv), and MgSiO3 post-perovskite (PPv). The potentials reproduce DFT energies, forces, stresses, pressure-volume relations, and compressed-state phonons for all three phases. For MgO at 0 GPa, replacing MACE forces with DFT forces in otherwise identical transport calculations changes $κ_{\mathrm{latt}}$ by 0.2% at 300 K and 1.9% at 2000 K. PPv is 44-55% more conductive than Pv at the same pressure and temperature and strongly anisotropic, with $κ_{[100]}/κ_{[010]}$ ranging from 1.7 to 2.1. The quasi-harmonic phase boundary has a Clapeyron slope of $(7.4 \pm 0.1)$ MPa/K and intersects a model geotherm at 116.2 GPa and 2660 K, where the transition raises the silicate conductivity by 47%, to 8.6 W/(m K). Heating in the basal thermal boundary layer lowers the MgO and PPv conductivities to 37.3 and 6.7 W/(m K) at the CMB, where Hashin-Shtrikman bounds of 9.5-11.4 W/(m K) for an Fe-free aggregate of 80 vol% silicate and 20 vol% MgO encompass the 10 W/(m K) used in earlier heat-flow estimates. The density-temperature parameterizations provide an intrinsic end-member reference for lower-mantle thermal models.

Photocatalytic Water Splitting in GeC/ReS$_2$ van der Waals Heterostructures

Parisa Beyranvand, Ismaeil Abdolhosseini Sarsari, Noejung Park

Photocatalytic water splitting is considered as one of the most promising approaches for the green hydrogen production. But the rapid recombination of photogenerated electron-hole pairs severely inhibits its photocatalytic efficiency. Construction of van der Waals heterostructures is a good way to suppress this recombination. However, in staggered band heterostructures, the charge transfer could occur either through the type-II or the direct Z-scheme mechanism. Therefore it is very important to identify which mechanism is dominant. In the present work, we systematically study the GeC/ReS$_2$ heterostructure using first-principles density functional theory (DFT). The system exhibits good thermodynamic, dynamic and thermal stability and an indirect band gap of 1.38 eV which greatly enhances the visible-light absorption. We find that the direct Z-scheme mechanism is the dominant interfacial charge transfer by analysing band alignment and Gibbs free energy pathways. The oxygen evolution reaction (OER) can be driven spontaneously in the pH range $4 \le \text{pH} \le 14$ under light irradiation for both type-II and Z-scheme pathways but the energy barrier is much lower for the Z-scheme mechanism. Moreover, the type-II mechanism is not suitable for hydrogen evolution reaction (HER), but the Z-scheme mechanism can promote HER well in the entire pH range ($0 \le \text{pH} \le 14$). Remarkably, the strain-free (pristine) condition exhibits a solar-to-hydrogen (STH) efficiency of 41.40\%, well above the 10\% threshold for commercial viability. The STH efficiency is further enhanced to 60.68\% under a tensile strain of +6\%. To summarise, the Z-scheme strain-tunable GeC/ReS$_2$ heterostructure is a promising and efficient photocatalyst for overall water splitting.

Origin of the anomalous shape of the $γ$-loop in paramagnetic Fe1-xCrx alloys from coupled Atomistic Spin Dynamics and ab initio Molecular Dynamics

Joakim Stenborg, Davide Gambino, Björn Alling

The region of fcc stability in Fe-rich Fe-Cr alloys, the $γ$-loop, has an anomalous shape: at low temperatures, Cr addition initially stabilizes fcc, but above 8 at.% Cr it stabilizes bcc. At high temperatures, Cr always stabilizes bcc. This behavior reflects a delicate balance between magnetic, vibrational and configurational free-energy contributions that is challenging to capture from first principles. We investigate phase stability in Fe1-xCrx (x = 0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15) using coupled atomistic spin dynamics and ab initio molecular dynamics with thermodynamic integration. Gibbs free energy differences between fcc and bcc are calculated from 800 to 1800 K. Our calculations reproduce the experimental $γ$-loop and its compositional dependence, with near-quantitative accuracy for the $γ$-$δ$ transition. The $α$-$γ$ boundary, including its anomalous shape, is reproduced qualitatively, although transition temperatures are underestimated by approximately 200 K. Static calculations of magnetically ordered and disordered states provide an explanation: in ordered states, increasing Cr initially reduces the energetic preference for bcc over fcc, whereas in disordered states, Cr progressively stabilizes bcc at all compositions. Vibrational effects contribute to phase stability, while atomic short-range clustering at higher Cr content ($x \geq 0.15$) may explain the observed closing of the $γ$-loop from below. The simulations reveal finite Cr moments in the paramagnetic state, whose magnitudes depend on their local magnetic environment. The results demonstrate that the anomalous $γ$-loop arises from the interplay of magnetic energetics, magnetic disorder, lattice vibrations and chemical clustering, which must be treated on equal footing to capture phase stability from first principle

Solvent-Mediated Disintegration of a Vitrimer Network

Manoj K Patel, Tarak K Patra

Vitrimers are dynamic polymer networks characterized by associative bond exchange, which enables topological rearrangement while preserving the overall network connectivity. However, how this dynamic connectivity evolves in the presence of a solvent, particularly as a function of temperature, remains poorly understood. Here, we employ hybrid molecular dynamics Monte Carlo simulations to elucidate the temperature-dependent restructuring and dissociation of vitrimer networks in solution. By constructing temperature composition phase diagrams, we demonstrate that the critical temperature for network dissolution increases nonlinearly with crosslink concentration. We find that the network disintegration begins well below the critical dissolution temperature, indicating that the loss of network connectivity can occur even before the onset of macroscopic dissolution. However, the network undergoes extensive restructuring around the critical temperature for a highly crosslinked system, resulting in the formation of numerous smaller, disconnected networks. Concurrently, the number of intrachain dynamic bonds increases with temperature. Interestingly, complete disintegration into individual polymer chains is not observed; instead, the solution comprises a broad distribution of smaller network fragments. These findings reveal the complex interplay between solvent-induced network disintegration and dynamic bond redistribution, providing molecular level insights into the dissolution of vitrimer networks and their potential for solvent assisted recycling.

Computing hydration free energies and crystalline solubility of drug-like molecules with MACE

J. Harry Moore, Paul Mortenson, Davide Branduardi, Pavel Buslaev, Javier Antorán, Laurence Midgley

Accurate prediction of thermodynamic solubility is critical in drug discovery, where it influences oral bioavailability and drives decisions in formulation chemistry. First-principles calculations of intrinsic solubility offer the potential to achieve superior generalization to existing direct, data-driven predictors, which often suffer significant performance degradation outside of their training distribution. Whilst free energy perturbation calculations provide a rigorous framework to estimate these quantities, their accuracy is typically limited by the quality of the empirical force field used. In this work, we present a computational workflow driven by the MACE family of machine learning interatomic potentials to compute the solubility of neutral drug-like molecules. We introduce $λ$-MACE, a potential optimised for condensed-phase free energy calculations, which enables hydration free energy calculations at experimental accuracy on an evaluation set of 70 compounds from the FreeSolv database selected for their relevance to medicinal chemistry. We further combine these hydration free energy calculations with sublimation free energy calculations to predict crystalline solubility given a known experimental crystal structure, on an additional set of 10 compounds. We achieve state-of-the-art performance for each leg of the calculation, with experimental accuracy for the hydration leg, and accuracy competitive with industry-standard DFT for the sublimation leg. However, the overall errors after combining both legs lead to accuracy of 1.53 log units. This is significantly above the experimental accuracy (0.17-0.6 log units). Closing this gap will require substantially tighter accuracy in each leg, on the order of half of chemical accuracy (0.5 kcal/mol), with crystalline thermodynamics the hardest remaining challenge.
