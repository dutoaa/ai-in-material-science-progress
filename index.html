Generative Design and Graph Neural Network Screening of Novel Bimetallic Alloy Catalysts for the Hydrogen Evolution Reaction

Vipin Kurian Elavunkel, Sylvain Le Tonquesse, Hadrien Vroylandt

Identifying earth-abundant catalysts for the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) is a central challenge in scalable green hydrogen production. We present a computational pipeline that couples diffusion-based generative modelling, machine-learning force-field relaxation, and graph neural network (GNN) property prediction to discover bimetallic HER catalysts beyond the structures and stoichiometries covered by existing adsorption databases. A generative model (MatterGen) is fine-tuned on bulk alloy structures labelled with their minimum hydrogen adsorption energy and steered towards adsorption energies near zero. From the generated pool we retain the stable structures that are novel bimetallic alloys: binary compositions built from the training-set metals but at stoichiometries or structures absent from that database. Each candidate's low-index facets are ranked by a machine-learning-force-field (MatterSim) surface-energy screen, and only the most stable terminations are converted into explicit slab models with hydrogen adsorbed at all high-symmetry surface sites and relaxed with the same force field. Two GNN surrogates (MEGNet and ALIGNN), trained on a high-throughput density functional theory (DFT) dataset, then screen the resulting slab candidates. Because the two models encode the local environment through different inductive biases, we retain only the alloys on which both agree, reducing the influence of any single model. This consensus screening, combined with a MatterSim energy-difference triangulation, resulted in a shortlist of near-thermoneutral bimetallic candidates. Eight are validated with plane-wave DFT, and four of them, Pd5Au, Pt5Rh, Cu5Pt3, and Pd13Ru3, bind hydrogen within the thermoneutral window. All eight alloys are dynamically stable by phonon calculations. These four alloys are highlighted as DFT-validated bimetallic HER candidates for experimental follow-up.

Quantum 3xplorer: AI-Native Engine for Next-Generation Quantum Materials Modeling

Vsevolod Biryukov, Sergey V. Barabash, Pranab Kumar Das, Jack Deslippe, Mauro Del Ben, Felipe H. da Jornada

Quantum-mechanical approaches, such as density functional theory (DFT), underpin much of modern computational materials science and are the primary source of data for AI-driven discovery. Yet the established electronic-structure codes today represent decades-old software architectures, making new physical models slow and costly to implement, validate, and deploy on emerging hardware. We present Quantum 3xplorer (Q3, pronounced "Quantum Explorer"), an AI-native quantum simulation engine written from scratch by AI coding agents under the direction and supervision of domain scientists. As a first and demanding validation case, we implement plane-wave pseudopotential DFT end to end: norm-conserving, ultrasoft, and projector-augmented-wave formalisms, spin polarization, DFT+U, and dispersion corrections, together with forces, stresses, structural relaxation, and electronic-structure analysis. Q3 reproduces established reference implementations, runs faster on a single core in representative benchmarks, and scales across GPU-accelerated supercomputers. It was developed in weeks rather than years. Its layered, physics-first architecture separates abstract quantum-mechanical concepts from their numerical representations, making its framework extendable beyond plane waves to other basis sets and levels of theory. Its inputs and outputs follow structured, schema-validated data standards, making every result AI-ready. Q3 is open source under Apache 2.0 and built to be reused, extended, and improved by the research community. Q3 points to a paradigm shift in how computational quantum science is done. Scientists define the physics, formulate the models, and set the criteria for validation; AI agents write, test, and optimize the code. The decades-long path from a new theory to a trusted, high-performance implementation can thus collapse to days, significantly accelerating scientific discovery.

Crystoria: A Large-Scale Crystal Dataset Linking Thermodynamic and Dynamical Stability for Materials AI

Ke-Xin Pang, Ze-Feng Gao, Yan Gao, Zhong-Yi Lu

Large-scale crystal databases have transformed materials discovery by making millions of structures and thermodynamic properties accessible to materials informatics. Yet dynamical stability remains sparsely characterized at a comparable scale because its evaluation is substantially more computationally demanding than conventional structure and energy calculations. This mismatch creates a critical gap between the rapidly expanding crystal-structure space and the physical information available for materials research and machine learning. Here we introduce Crystoria, a large-scale crystal dataset designed to bring dynamical stability to the scale of modern materials data. We construct Crystoria by systematically curating low-energy and metastable inorganic crystals, standardizing their structural identities, and combining machine-learning-accelerated structural relaxation with phonon-based dynamical screening. The resulting dataset contains 2,649,640 crystal structures, spanning 89 elements, 219 of the 230 space groups, and primitive cells containing 1-48 atoms. Among them, 1,793,353 structures (67.68%) are classified as dynamically stable, revealing substantial variation in dynamical stability across chemical and crystallographic space. By bringing dynamical stability to the scale of millions of crystal structures, Crystoria provides a physically grounded foundation for materials artificial intelligence (AI) and enables systematic exploration of the dynamical crystal landscape.

Generative discovery of chemical reaction networks

Yunhong Han, Gaopeng Ren, Yan Liu, Ronghua Zhang, Huangjiang Huang, Ruiqi Zhang

Chemical kinetics determines molecular stability, reaction selectivity and the evolution of reacting mixtures, but discovering the underlying reaction networks remains challenging. Here we introduce ReactNexus, a generative framework for kinetic-guided network exploration starting solely from reactant graphs. Generative models propose transition states and predict the full reaction trajectories connecting reactants and products, while calculations using a machine-learned interatomic potential verify connectivity and evaluate electronic barriers. On an in-distribution benchmark of 10,000 generated candidates, 80.8% connect to the input reactant after density functional theory (DFT) optimization and intrinsic reaction coordinate verification. The resulting networks uncover lower-barrier cooperative pathways and competition between catalyst release and catalyst-bound branches. In the examined carbocation cascades, coverage of known product precursors saturates after limited expansion and distinct entries converge onto shared intermediates, indicating a compact accessible reaction space. Network-derived candidate structures further assist mass-spectral interpretation of a complex nucleophilic aromatic substitution reaction. Together, these results establish a generative approach to reaction-network discovery and mechanistic analysis without predefined products or reaction templates.

ORCHID: Connecting Finite-Temperature Thermodynamic Evidence to Computational Materials Discovery

Raymundo Arróyave, James E. Saal, Dongwon Shin

Finite-temperature phase stability is essential to materials discovery, but the evidence needed to describe it remains scattered across decades of literature. ORCHID organizes this evidence around the thermodynamic states that the observations describe, using tiered agentic extraction guided by thermodynamic rules, expert supervision and explicit validation. The collection contains 73,542 values from 1,321 papers, represented with their thermodynamic states, phase identities, provenance, evidence classes and uncertainty, together with assessed phase assemblages and calculated references. A qualified subset is released in ESPEI-compatible form for CALPHAD assessment, and machine-readable access supports both human and AI-assisted reuse. Blind expert evaluation of 48 diagram fields gives 93.8% agreement, increasing to 97.9% after accounting for notation conventions. On a common formation-enthalpy benchmark, eight MLIPs show residual spreads close to their DFT reference, motivating evaluation of finite-temperature phase stability through PhaseForge. One-shot MLIP-PhaseForge descriptions of Cu-Zr recover 0.35-0.56 of observed phase occurrences at assessed field points. ORCHID-assisted refinement through PhaseForge+ recovers 9 of 13 invariant temperatures within 2 sigma and reduces held-out activity error from 5.1 to 1.7 kJ/mol. By supplying interpreted literature evidence directly to gradient-based refinement through PhaseForge+, ORCHID connects evidence recovery to programmatic CALPHAD assessment. With the initial description and fitting datasets prepared, the final Cu-Zr refinement completes in approximately 27 minutes, providing a foundation for continuously updated thermodynamic models and their use in materials discovery.

Agentic Global Optimization of Site-Disordered Crystalline Materials

Martin Hoffmann Petersen, Zekun Ren, Kedar Hippalgaonkar

Site-disordered crystals, in which several elements partially occupy the same atomic site, cannot be simulated atomistically without first resolving an explicit ordering, and the number of possible orderings grows combinatorially with supercell size. Because each candidate requires an expensive structural relaxation, locating the global-minimum ordering is a costly combinatorial search. Here, we present an agentic search in which a surrogate model, fitted to previously evaluated configurations, selects each subsequent candidate by balancing exploitation, uncertainty, and novelty. Four surrogates are implemented within a shared framework: an ExtraTrees ensemble, Bayesian ridge regression, sparse ARD regression, and an ALiEGNN-inspired graph ensemble. These are benchmarked against three traditional methods: random walk, Markov chain Monte Carlo, and a genetic algorithm across 14 test cases spanning 12 crystal structures, with exhaustively enumerated global minima as ground truth. The agentic surrogates reach the global minimum in 94% of runs, compared with 55% for the traditional methods, with a median 4.3-fold reduction in evaluations relative to the best traditional method. Bayesian ridge regression succeeds in all runs with the fewest evaluations. Applied to Na2/3FePO4, with coupled Na/vacancy configurational disorder and Fe2+/Fe3+ charge disorder, the framework recovers the experimentally reported ordering when charge states are encoded in the surrogate representation, demonstrating its extension to multi-sublattice disorder.

Full-Potential Korringa Kohn Rostoker Study of Structural and Mechanical Properties in SiGeSn Alloys

Wasim Raja Mondal, Swarnava Ghosh, Ka-Ming Tam, Markus Eisenbach, Yang Wang, Hanna Terletska

Accurate first-principles descriptions of open, covalently bonded crystal structures remain challenging within conventional Korringa--Kohn--Rostoker (KKR) approaches owing to the spherical approximations commonly employed for the effective crystal potential. Here, we systematically assess the recently developed full-potential KKR (KKR-FP) implementation in the open-source MuST package and demonstrate its applicability to diamond-structured group-IV semiconductors and SiGeSn alloys. Benchmark calculations for diamond-structured Si reveal substantial limitations of the conventional muffin-tin and atomic sphere approximations, whereas KKR-FP accurately reproduces the experimental equilibrium lattice parameter and bulk modulus, yielding 5.433 and 94.9 GPa. The full-potential treatment also recovers the semiconducting character of Si. Further calculations for elemental Ge and Sn establish the applicability of the approach across group-IV materials. Extending this framework to SiGeSn alloys, we investigate their composition-dependent equilibrium lattice parameters, bulk moduli, and mixing enthalpies. The calculated lattice parameters exhibit positive deviations from Vegard's law, while the bulk modulus decreases substantially with changes in alloy composition. Moreover, positive mixing enthalpies of 180.92 and 346.32 meV/atom are obtained for Si0.5Ge0.375Sn0.125 and Si0.375Ge0.25Sn0.375, respectively, indicating an energetic penalty for homogeneous alloy formation relative to the selected elemental reference states. These findings highlight the importance of nonspherical potential contributions in accurately describing open-structured semiconductors and establish KKR-FP within MuST as a promising framework for investigating the structural properties and mixing energetics of compositionally complex group-IV alloys

Framework-Conditional Open Materials Generation

Maya M. Martirossyan, Hillary Pan, Philipp Höllmer, Stefano Martiniani

Theoretical predictions of novel materials rely on a variety of techniques, from data-mining-based ion substitution methods to fully de novo generative models. Each approach imposes specific constraints and encounters unique challenges when extrapolating beyond the distribution of known materials to discover new ones. The foremost goal of materials generation is to produce stable, unique, and novel (S.U.N.) structures. Predicted structures that meet these criteria, however, typically result from novel chemical compositions mapped onto known structural prototypes or frameworks. We leverage this heuristic insight to present a new generative paradigm for the de novo prediction of inorganic crystalline materials constrained by their structural framework---i.e., the unit cell shapes and respective particle positions. Our method, termed OMatG-FC (Open Materials Framework-Conditional Generation), learns via a combination of discrete flow matching on the atomic species and stochastic interpolants for the volume scaling of the unit cell lattice, while fixing in-place the fractional coordinates and the unit-volume lattice vectors, dramatically reducing the dimensionality of the problem compared to unconstrained (framework-free) generation. Our method is more flexible than existing prototype-constrained methods, demonstrating capability for discovery of unseen, stable prototypes derived from known structural frameworks. We also investigate the number of unique frameworks in the MP-20 dataset, demonstrating the propensity of duplicate frameworks within the dataset and providing a framework-split version. We benchmark the performance of our model using the LeMat-GenBench suite using three ML interatomic potentials and achieve state-of-the-art S.U.N. rate below the convex hull, eschewing the need to learn atomic packings for materials discovery.

PAPERBENCHX: Can AI Reproduce Science Across Disciplines?

Ruoyu Wu, Zengji Tu, Aolong Sun, Tianyi Ma, Junren Li, Xinbo Xu

Before AI can be trusted to discover new science, it should be able to reproduce what science already knows. Reproduction provides a rigorous checkpoint for an AI scientist: the target claim is known, yet recovering it still requires translating an incomplete scientific description into an executable experiment and producing evidence that can be independently verified. Existing research-reproduction benchmarks largely focus on AI and machine learning, where experiments share relatively homogeneous software stacks. Extending this capability across scientific disciplines introduces a different challenge: agents must operate domain-specific scientific software, make physical and numerical choices, diagnose long-running computations, and distinguish valid evidence from merely plausible outputs. We introduce PAPERBENCHX, a benchmark of 93 expert-constructed paper-reproduction tasks spanning 12 research areas across electromagnetics, photonics, chemistry, materials science, biology, and robotics. Each task provides the source paper, a public task specification, and a domain-native scientific environment involving specialized scientific software. Agents must reconstruct a key experiment, execute and diagnose it, and deliver a runnable workflow that supports the target scientific claim. To evaluate scientific validity rather than surface-level agreement, we replay every submission from scratch and score only regenerated evidence against expert-validated criteria. Across 10 frontier agent configurations, the best configuration fully reproduces only 14% tasks under the 90% threshold. Meanwhile, mean rubric scores range from 27.53% to 59.08%, indicating that substantial partial progress rarely translates into complete scientific reproduction. These results reveal a large gap between paper understanding and reliably producing reproducible scientific evidence.

Query-Efficient Quantum Multi-Objective Optimization for Materials Configuration Search via Bayesian Weight Selection

Onur Boyar, Seiji Takeda, Akihiro Kosugi, Indra Priyadarsini S, Lisa Hamada, Sina Klampt

Materials design is a multi-objective problem, and recent advances in quantum optimization make it possible to bring quantum solvers to bear on such problems. The realistic near-term win, though, is not raw quantum advantage but making an expensive, noisy quantum solver usable by spending its calls wisely. Quantum Approximate Multi-Objective Optimization (QAMOO) traces a Pareto front by solving a family of scalarized Ising problems, one per objective-weight vector, with QAOA --- but chooses those weights \emph{non-adaptively}, sampling them at random, blind to what it has already found. We recast weight selection as \emph{multi-objective Bayesian optimization}: our method, BW-QAMOO (Bayes-Weighted QAMOO), picks each next weight by expected hypervolume improvement (EHVI) under a Gaussian-process (GP) surrogate over the weight-to-objective map. On a frustrated alloy-configuration benchmark across $m\in\{3,4\}$ objectives and $n\in\{10,11,12\}$ sites, BW-QAMOO reaches the same Pareto hypervolume as QAMOO's random sampling with a median 33% fewer scalarized solves across the 90/95\% targets and 60--74\% fewer than a uniform-grid baseline), on a genuine (simulated) QAOA solver; a paired Wilcoxon test confirms the saving is significant, and the advantage grows with the objective count m as the weight simplex expands and blind sampling wastes more of the budget. This is a \emph{query-efficiency} result, and we close with the open challenges on the path to real alloy data and hardware.

Radial Kernel Attention for Long-Range Modeling in Polymer Machine Learning Force Fields

Wenbo Fu, Chenghui Mao, Chengwen Hu, Haifeng Lv, Zhou Lv

We introduce Radial Kernel Attention (RKA) for long-range modeling in polymer machine learning force fields. Local neighbor graphs constrain communication across polymer chains and their surrounding environments, while explicit all-pair attention incurs quadratic cost. RKA maps atomic distances from the cluster centroid to learned radial features and combines their overlap with content-dependent queries and keys. Factorized aggregation and readout enable global feature exchange at linear cost in atom count for fixed feature dimensions. We insert the invariant scalar update between EquiformerV3 local blocks and obtain conservative forces by differentiating the total energy, including its radial coordinate dependence. We evaluate energy and force prediction for finite polymer-derived clusters in OPoly26. Our models are trained on the official training split for 12 epochs and evaluated directly after training on the complete official validation split. A matched 5M comparison shows that RKA reduces per-atom energy and force MAEs by 67.4% and 36.3%, respectively. The 20M RKA model is compared with UMA-S-1.2 and UMA-S-1.2.1 on the same validation structures. The 20M model attains a total-energy MAE of 15.81 meV and a force MAE of 2.619 meV/angstrom; its per-atom energy MAE is 0.1182 meV/atom. RKA provides a compact global communication pathway that improves energy and force prediction on the evaluated polymer-derived clusters.

SKFitter: Symmetry resolved Slater Koster tight-binding models from first principles for s, p, d and f electrons, spin orbit coupling and magnetism

Sundeep Kapila, Bhaskaran Muralidharan, Satadeep Bhattacharjee

Constructing accurate and physically interpretable tight binding (TB) Hamiltonians from first principles calculations remains challenging, particularly for materials exhibiting complex orbital hybridization, spin orbit coupling (SOC), and magnetic order. SKFitter is an open-source Python framework for constructing and optimizing symmetry resolved Slater Koster TB models directly from density functional theory (DFT) calculations performed using Quantum ESPRESSO or VASP. The framework supports s to f orbitals, incorporates on site SOC and site resolved magnetic exchange interactions, and accommodates both orthogonal and nonorthogonal basis. A central feature is the symmetry resolved parameterization of orbital manifolds, which allows independent two centre hopping integrals between symmetry allowed orbital groups and across distinct neighbour shells. SKFitter also identifies missing orbital contributions and guide systematic basis set refinement and optimizes parameters through weights. The resulting Hamiltonian can be used to evaluate anomalous (AHC) and spin Hall conductivities (SHC), and topological invariants. We demonstrate the framework using eight representative materials spanning semimetals, semiconductors, transition metals, f-electron compounds, and magnetic systems. Symmetry resolved models achieve band energy errors of 9 to 41 meV within 1 eV of the Fermi level. The fitted Hamiltonians reproduce characteristic features of the SHC response of Pt, the SOC dependence of the AHC of Fe, and the momentum dependent altermagnetic spin splitting of MnTe without any altermagnet specific interaction. For EuMn2Bi2, projection guided orbital refinement reduces the near Fermi level fitting error from 120 to 32 meV. Together, these results establish SKFitter as a flexible and reproducible framework for developing symmetry consistent, physically interpretable TB models of complex materials.

Resisting Drying Stress in Laponite Bentonite Clay Composites, Two Step Yielding and Crack Peel Selection

Tapati Dutta, Agnishikha Chakrabarti, Soham Mitra, Deepshikha Das, Indranil Chakraborty

Clay films that dry on a rigid substrate crack, peel or stay intact, and which of these happens matters for coatings, barrier layers and ceramic processing. Composites of clay with polymers have been studied widely. Clay composites, whose components differ in size and charge, are much less explored. We show that mixing the nano scale clay Laponite with the micron scale clay Bentonite reduces desiccation damage on both a hydrophobic and a hydrophilic substrate, Laponite films stay essentially intact, Bentonite films fracture extensively, and on glass most intermediate compositions are less damaged than a simple average of the two would suggest. Rheology of the gelled suspensions reveals a two step yielding in every composition, and the cohesive energy derived from it depends by orders of magnitude on which step is used, a point that any rheology cracking correlation must specify.
