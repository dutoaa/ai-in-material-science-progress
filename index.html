Synthetic Crystal Data Generation for Diffusion Pretraining in Material Property Prediction

Aamod Varma, Shuyi Jia, Victor Fung

Diffusion-based pretraining has emerged as a powerful strategy for learning transferable representations in graph neural networks for materials property prediction without requiring quantum-mechanical labels such as energies, forces, and stresses for every pretraining structure. However, the method retains a critical dependency: it requires structures residing near local minima of a potential energy surface, which have traditionally been obtained through costly DFT relaxations of known or physically synthesized compounds. This reliance constrains both the scale and the structural diversity of available pretraining data, leaving large regions of crystal structure space unexplored. In this work, we connect diffusion-based pretraining with synthetic structure generation to reduce this bottleneck. We introduce a synthetic data generation pipeline combining three complementary strategies: perturb-and-relax, generative sampling via MatterGen, and protostructure enumeration. Where structural relaxation is required, we use fast machine-learned interatomic potentials instead of performing a new DFT relaxation for each generated structure. We further compare a coordinate-denoising control with continuous-time variance-preserving (VP) and sub-VP diffusion objectives using a randomly initialized Orb-v2 backbone. The source comparison covers seven MatBench property-prediction tasks, and an eighth task, \texttt{mp\_gap}, is included in the protostructure scaling study. Every synthetic-pretraining condition numerically outperforms random initialization on all seven source-comparison tasks. Scaling the protostructure corpus from 20k to 165k under a fixed 400-epoch schedule numerically matches or exceeds MP-20k pretraining on most tasks; because this protocol increases both data diversity and optimization updates, the result reflects joint data-and-compute scaling.

Weight-Space Programming for Materials Science

Sk Md Ahnaf Akif Alvi, Raymundo Arróyave

A materials discovery campaign often begins with almost no measurements of the property it is searching for, and the models that rank candidates or screen the output of a generator then have to be built from those first few labels. Following recent work on weight-space programming, in which a language model writes the weights of a small network directly as text, we show that a general-purpose model, given the task in words, the names of the inputs and a network of 16 hidden units with every weight set to zero, can provide this starting point by filling in all of the weights from what it has learned about materials. With no labels, the networks of the strongest model rank experimental alloys at Spearman co-efficient = 0.64 to 0.79, where the best of five simple descriptors reaches 0.47 to 0.62. They rank the bulk modulus of crystals added during generative discovery loops at 0.95, where the inverse volume per atom reaches 0.89, and 35 phase-equilibrium targets computed with Thermo-Calc at a mean of 0.67, close to the 0.70 of an extrapolation from calculated binary and ternary data. As the prior mean of a Gaussian process they raise the ranking at five labels from 0.35 to 0.69 on alloys and from 0.15 to 0.95 on crystals, and they lower the regret of Bayesian optimization significantly on 4 of 5 experimental tasks and on 13 to 16 of 33 computed targets, depending on how multiple tests are corrected, without raising it significantly on any. The same networks also serve as the mean of a Bayesian neural network and as a screen for the samples of two crystal generators, where they do as well as standard models trained on 400 labeled structures. The gain is confined to the first tens of labels and to targets that no rule or sub-system extrapolation already ranks.

A Practitioner's Guide to Materials Theory and Computation in the Age of Big Data and Artificial Intelligence

Jack Yang

Computational modelling has become a cornerstone of materials science, with advances in atomistic simulations, high-throughput computation, and artificial intelligence transforming how materials are understood, discovered, and designed. These developments build upon generations of theoretical innovations that have translated complex mathematical and physical principles into practical predictive tools. However, the growing diversity of computational methods presents challenges for practitioners seeking a coherent understanding of their theoretical foundations. Drawing upon the author's research experience, this monograph provides a practitioner-oriented, pedagogical overview of computational materials science, unified through the concepts of potential-energy landscapes and statistical sampling. It explores how these concepts underpin structural stability, phase behaviour, and electronic and magnetic properties, through a systematic treatment of empirical force fields, density-functional theory, many-body electronic-structure methods, statistical sampling, and property prediction. It further examines high-throughput workflows, materials databases, and the probabilistic foundations of artificial intelligence. Particular emphasis is placed on atomistic representations, graph neural networks, and generative models, highlighting the roles of symmetry, group theory, differential geometry, and stochastic processes in modern materials modelling. By connecting established physical theories with emerging data-driven approaches, this monograph offers a unified perspective on computational materials science and identifies opportunities and challenges toward physics-informed, efficient, and reliable materials discovery.

IANN: A Unified Framework for Equivariant InterAtomic Neural Networks with User-Friendly Implementation

Changzhi Ai, Xueping Qin, Dimosthenis Sokaras, Frank Abild-Pedersen, Johannes Voss

Machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) now approach density functional theory (DFT) accuracy at a fraction of its cost, but the practical benefit is limited by software fragmentation: each state-of-the-art architecture is released as a separate codebase, with its own data format, configuration system, training loop and molecular-dynamics interface. Comparing two architectures on the same dataset, or moving a trained model into a production simulation, therefore requires substantial re-engineering. We introduce IANN (InterAtomic Neural Network), an open-source PyTorch framework that reimplements equivariant graph neural networks - PaiNN, NequIP, MACE, Allegro, EquiformerV2, EquiformerV3 and UMA - behind a unified data object, a unified trainer and a single deployment route, so that the architecture is a one-word change in a configuration dictionary. Forces, stresses and virials are obtained for every architecture from one analytic differentiation of the energy with respect to the edge vectors of the atomic graph, exact under periodic boundary conditions. Distributed training is configured automatically from the SLURM, PBS or MPI environment, so the same script runs unchanged from a workstation to a multi-node GPU allocation. Self-describing checkpoints serve directly as ASE calculators, as ensembles reporting uncertainties, or as TorchScript models run inside LAMMPS through bundled C++ pair styles with single- and multi-GPU inference. Trained on a common dataset of millions of structures, the architectures can be compared under identical conditions. We further release PaiNN foundation models trained on 41.9 million curated DFT structures from MPtrj, Alexandria, OMat24, MatPES, OC20, OC22, OC25, MPr2SCAN and MatPESr2SCAN, spanning the PBE, RPBE and r2SCAN functionals. Additionally, IANN provides an agent interface to facilitate integration with autonomous agents.

DSADiff: Data Structure-Aware Diffusion for Crystal Structure Prediction via Mitigating Complexity- and Imbalance-Induced Biases

Jiang Xiaobo, Dinghong Lai

Crystal Structure Prediction (CSP) is an important step in accelerating the generation and discovery of new materials. In recent years, diffusion-based CSP methods have made significant progress. However, existing models still suffer from generation bias when facing diverse materials spaces, while systematic approaches for tracing the sources of such performance variations remain limited. To address this issue, we propose LAS (Layered Analysis Strategy), a mechanism analysis framework that identifies crystal complexity and elemental composition imbalance as two key factors affecting generation difficulty and further reveals their relationships with model generation performance. Building on these findings, we further propose DSADiff (Data Structure-Aware Diffusion), a data structure-aware diffusion model that transforms the key factors identified by LAS into a unified generation difficulty representation and uses it to jointly drive feature modeling and training strategy optimization. This enables the model to adapt its representation and generation capability to samples with different data structures and difficulty levels, thereby mitigating learning bias across samples. Experiments on three CSP benchmark datasets, including Carbon-24, demonstrate that DSADiff consistently improves both Match Rate and RMSE, with a maximum Match Rate improvement of 9%, surpassing existing state-of-the-art methods and achieving new state-of-the-art performance. This work deepens the understanding of diffusion-based CSP models from the perspective of generation bias and provides a new direction for designing less biased generative models for complex materials spaces.

Adsorbate-Free Screening of Dissociative Water Adsorption on Nickel Nanoparticles via Graph Neural Networks

Marta Monti, Alec Noppe, Olivier Lugier, Maria B. Brands, Bernd Ensing

Dissociative water adsorption is a key step in heterogeneous and electrochemical catalysis, but on finite Ni nanosystems the OH$^*$ and H$^*$ fragments can occupy many combinations of surface sites, making exhaustive density functional theory (DFT) screening impractical. We introduce a graph neural network (GNN) framework with an adsorbate-free representation: candidate O- and H-binding sites are defined by sets of Ni atoms (\textit{anchors}), and each site pair is encoded as a graph constructed solely from these anchors and their local Ni environment. Using only MACE-relaxed bare Ni geometries as input, separate GNNs predict the DFT-reference adsorption energies of OH$^*$+H$^*$ configurations and whether the proposed binding sites would be retained upon DFT relaxation, eliminating the need to construct or relax adsorbates during screening. Trained across Ni nanoclusters and Ni(111)/Ni(100) surfaces, the energy model achieves a mean absolute error of 0.031 eV on the internal validation set and demonstrates transferability to unseen Ni$_{55}$, Ni$_{91}$, and Ni$_{130}$ clusters and larger surface cells, particularly for relative-energy ranking, although quantitative accuracy varies across systems. These independent tests also support the retention classifier's use as a conservative filter for selecting configurations likely to preserve their proposed binding sites. Application to Ni nanoparticles containing up to $\sim$3000 atoms identifies favorable co-adsorption motifs and shows that lower O-site coordination generally stabilizes the dissociated state, whereas H-site coordination has a weaker influence. The framework thus offers an efficient route to screening adsorption energetics, with potential extensions to other metals and adsorbate combinations.

Nahual: A Sequence Model for Language and Atoms

Austin H. Cheng, Marcel Müller, Andreas Burger, Yeonghun Kang, Tsz Wai Ko, Marta Skreta

Generative models play a rapidly growing role in the design and simulation of molecules. The most widely used applications of generative models are in large language models (LLMs), owing to the natural flexibility of language for specifying and answering queries. However, generalist LLMs do not effectively handle large sets of 3D coordinates, whereas specialist generative models of 3D molecules cannot reach the controllability afforded by language. To bridge these capabilities, we propose Nahual, a decoder-only autoregressive diffusion model for any sequence of text and 3D molecules. To enable expressive autoregression on continuous coordinates, we propose next-token diffusion, whereby a causal transformer denoises each token while attending to the clean tokens before it, without increasing context size. We train Nahual on a curated set of twelve 3D chemistry tasks ranging from microsolvation to molecular conformer search to adsorption on metal surfaces, with associated evaluation metrics and baselines. The result is a single set of end-to-end-trained weights that simultaneously performs all tasks and surpasses state-of-the-art models specialized for crystal structure prediction and property-conditional molecule generation. Our results demonstrate the viability of scaling generalist decoder-only models for native multimodal understanding and generation across language and chemistry. Code is available at https://github.com/the-matter-lab/nahual.

A Density Functional Tight Binding Model for Rapid Screening of Spin-Orbit-Coupling-Driven Topological Properties in Bismuth

Nir Goldman, Ben Hourahine

We present a transferable Density Functional Tight Binding (DFTB) model for bismuth with explicit spin-orbit coupling that enables exploration of the structural degrees of freedom governing topological properties with minimal computational resources. We have used our DFTB model to run high-throughput calculations at a scale that would be significantly more expensive with standard Kohn-Sham Density Functional Theory (DFT) to determine a strain-buckling map of electronic and topological properties for bismuthene, where we identify regions of band gap closure and band inversion as a function of structural parameters. We show that our approach yields an accurate band structure for the rhombohedral phase, where small band gaps exist at or near time-reversal-invariant momentum (TRIM) points. We then demonstrate the transferability of our model to bismuth properties not included in our training data through determination of the rhombohedral phase phonon density of states and energetic ordering of additional bismuth solid phases. Finally, we have validated our topological predictions through comparison to DFT results for the optimized bilayer geometries, a band inversion order parameter, and the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant as a function of monolayer unit cell length. Our efforts establish DFTB as a rigorous approach for topological characterization while yielding approximately three orders of magnitude increase in computational efficiency relative to DFT. Our model can thus serve as a computational tool for topological materials discovery by enabling large-scale computational screening to address the potentially complex relationship between physical-chemical properties and the resulting topological classification.

Efficient Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials

Yury Lysogorskiy, Anton Bochkarev, Ralf Drautz

Machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) reach an accuracy close to quantum mechanical reference calculations at a small fraction of the cost. Efficiency is the purpose of MLIPs, yet strategies for efficient MLIPs have not been reviewed systematically. This review takes efficiency as its guideline. To compare MLIPs on a common footing, we represent them in a complete basis expansion, the Atomic Cluster Expansion (ACE) and its graph generalization (GRACE). The expansion is canonical, built from products of single-particle functions. Classical interatomic potentials, such as the embedded-atom method, and many modern MLIPs, including equivariant transformers, can be represented within this expansion. Much of the apparent diversity of MLIP architectures therefore reflects how non-linearity, layer coupling and optimization are handled rather than which interactions the models can represent. We show how the cost of an MLIP becomes linear in system size and, at fixed rank of the tensor decomposition, in model complexity, and we discuss the role of machine learning, which provides non-linear parameterizations and optimization. Because efficiency is meaningful only at a given accuracy, we also compare accuracy and cost. On the Matbench Discovery leaderboard, models with three to five layers approach the accuracy of deeper models, and one basis, truncated along the Pareto front of error and speed, spans more than two orders of magnitude in cost. Ultimately, training data determine the quality of any MLIP.

Swift Heavy Ion Tracks in Polymorphs of Titanium Dioxide: The Effects of Electron Beam and Crystal Structures

Xiaoting Jia, Lijun Xu, Ruslan A. Rymzhanov, Wenqiang Liu, Peipei Hu, Shengxia Zhang

Swift heavy ion (SHI) irradiation generates nanoscale damage in materials, offering opportunities for controlled modification of structural properties. Understanding the fundamental processes governing damage formation in altered regions remains a longstanding challenge. This work explores how crystal structure influences swift heavy ion-induced damage formation and evolution in rutile and anatase TiO2. Combining transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and molecular dynamics simulations, we investigate how differences in atomic arrangement govern track morphology and structural recovery. Although irradiation produces amorphous tracks in both polymorphs, their morphologies differ considerably. In rutile, tracks develop pronounced diameter variations, forming hourglass-like structures, whereas in anatase they retain a relatively uniform cylindrical shape. Molecular dynamics simulations reveal that these differences arise from the distinct recrystallization behavior of the two crystal structures during rapid cooling, leading to different pathways of track evolution and recovery. Particular attention is paid to electron beam induced effects during TEM characterization, which can substantially modify the structure and apparent morphology of ion tracks. Prolonged electron beam exposure promotes the recrystallization of initially amorphous tracks and surface hillocks, accompanied by the development of Fresnel contrast. These findings highlight the importance of understanding beam-induced transformations for the reliable interpretation of TEM observations of radiation-damaged materials.

What Do LLMs Use to Judge Synthesis Recipes? An Experimental Analysis on 240 Solid-State Syntheses

Derek van Tilborg, Laura-Bianca Pasca, Harry Sevi, Kyle Reeves, Thomas Loux, Aikaterini Flessa Savvidou

Inorganic materials discovery is increasingly limited by synthesis rather than by material design. Large language models (LLMs) are being adopted to propose synthesis conditions with little experimental evidence of their capabilities and biases. Here, we carry out an experimental synthesis benchmark of 240 experiments across eight known and unknown inorganic oxides and subsequently evaluate an LLM as a zero-shot ranker over fixed banks of solid-state synthesis recipes. For each pair of recipes for the same oxide target, `GPT-5.6-Sol` selects the recipe expected to yield higher target-phase purity. We aggregate these pairwise preferences using a regularized Bradley-Terry model to obtain a ranked list of synthesis recipes for a given target material. By comparison with the actual purity, obtained through X-ray diffraction, we measure `GPT-5.6-Sol`'s performance as a zero-shot ranker (0.72 pairwise accuracy). By selective removal of the information exposed to the model during this ranking, we conclude that ranking is driven predominantly by thermal features, with reaction chemistry modulating the effect. Ranking accuracy tracks the purity-temperature correlation by target (Spearman ρ= 0.91\), \(p = 0.002\), and on the six thermally-driven targets withholding all chemical identity hardly impacts performance (0.68) compared to withholding temperature (0.56). Ranking performance was higher on known than unknown targets, but the group-level difference was not statistically conclusive. The methodological choices highlight the need for more experimentally validated synthesis benchmarks and position LLMs as a thermally informed prior.

SkillBuffBench: Measuring What Agent Skills Contribute across Harnesses, Models, Skill Access Modes and Memory

Gengyue Dong, Ye Wei, Zheng Zhu, Yidong Han, Jiale Miao, Xiaojun Jia

Neither holds in deployment, where an agent chooses from whatever library happens to be installed and frequently carries memory from earlier work. We present SkillBuffBench, a benchmark and paired evaluation protocol that treats Skill access as a three-level factor (no Skill, a curated Skill, and automatic invocation from an installed library), crosses it with memory (off and on), with four agent harnesses and with multiple models (the state-of-the-art proprietary models from Anthropic, Google and OpenAI, and the state-of-the-art open-weight models from Chinese providers), every model being run under every harness, and evaluates the result on research tasks contributed by domain experts together with the Skills they use, currently in materials science and biology, with the suite extended on a rolling basis. Every run is marked by a deterministic verifier; uncertainty is reported with a paired, task-level bootstrap; and cost, agent rounds and wall-clock time are reported alongside the score rather than folded into it. This version of the paper describes the design of the benchmark, its evaluation protocol and its current tasks and Skills. The evaluation is in progress: results will be added once the runs are complete, and the task suite, results and analysis will continue to be updated on a rolling basis.

Dynamics and transport in LiF using classical and machine learned interatomic potentials

Harvey L. Devereux, Margaret-Ann Withington, Alin M. Elena, Cillian Cockrell, Kostya Trachenko

We perform molecular dynamics simulations of molten Lithium Fluoride, comparing the classical Buckingham and Born-Huggins-Mayer potentials with machine-learnt interatomic potentials - the MACE-MP-0b3 (small) foundation model potential, and a fine-tuned version thereof. We find that the MACE-MP-0b3, out-of-the-box, is able to accurately reproduce the experimental shear viscosity across the liquid state comparable with polarisable potentials despite not itself being polarisable. Previous viscosities computed using classical potentials are under-predicted, which has previously been attributed to a suppressed melting temperature. Prompted by this we compare the melting points of these potentials. We find that the machine-learnt potentials address the significant underestimation of the melting point of the classical potentials, though the refined potential and the foundation model differ significantly between themselves. We probe the microscopic structure and dynamics of each model to further elucidate the considerable change in viscosity between classical and machine-learnt potentials. We find that, despite their lower melting temperature, classical potentials produce considerably stiffer, higher frequency, single-particle and collective charge-transport dynamics on account of their fixed charges. However, in the long-wavelength limit, the many-body machine-learnt potentials feature more elastic collective motion that governs the diffusion of momentum and is therefore responsible for the enhanced shear viscosity.
