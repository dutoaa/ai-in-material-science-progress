Active Learning for Interatomic Potentials: A Review of Uncertainty-Driven Data Efficiency

Ilya V. Chepkasov, Alexander V. Shapeev, Alexander G. Kvashnin

Developing a machine learning interatomic potential (MLIP) that is accurate for the specific conditions under which it will be applied, rather than simply for the predefined configurations chosen by the model developer, is a significant challenge in atomistic simulation. Active learning (AL) addresses this challenge by closing the loop between candidate generation, data selection, reference calculation, and model updating. Here, we survey more than seventy studies, with an emphasis on work from 2020 to 2026, to understand how active learning has evolved from a niche, on-the-fly training technique for query-by-committee and Gaussian process force fields into a diverse family of data-efficient strategies. he reviewed examples include on-the-fly molecular dynamics, active data selection for fine-tuning pretrained models, kernel- and representation-based acquisition using frozen pretrained models, and reaction-aware sampling for chemical systems far from equilibrium. We explore the use of active learning in elemental and alloy mechanics, hydrogen and ion diffusion, ferroelectrics and effective Hamiltonians of functional materials, reactive and photochemical dynamics, high-entropy and compositionally disordered materials, as well as generative and autonomous material discovery pipelines. Drawing on a growing body of critical, methodologically self-aware literature, we also show that this achievement carries systematic caveats. Uncertainty estimates are not interchangeable across model architectures and are frequently the least reliable exactly where they matter most. We close by discussing the outlook for standardized, uncertainty-calibrated, and observable-targeted active-learning workflows in the foundation-model era.

Sorption Molecular Dynamics of Point Defects in 2D Materials with a Machine Learning Potential

Nils-Ruben Skogemyr, Albin Sjöstedt, Isak Wernroth, Isak Häggqvist, Isac Ljungwald, Hans-Ove Åkesson

Defect engineering is a crucial tool for tuning and tailoring the properties of two-dimensional (2D) materials. A key challenge is identifying which defects are kinetically stable at room temperature and sufficiently promising to warrant further investigation. However, identifying stable defects with suitable properties using first-principles calculations, such as density functional theory (DFT) is computationally demanding, effectively limiting the scope of high-throughput studies. In this work, we investigate whether the machine learning interatomic potential MACE can reproduce sorption trends (adsorption vs. absorption) previously obtained from high-throughput DFT calculations and extend the analysis to elevated temperatures. A total of 17,624 defect structures from the ADAQ database are simulated using MACE-accelerated molecular dynamics (MD) at both 0 K and 300 K. The results show that MACE successfully reproduces the overall sorption trends observed in DFT calculations at 0 K at a fraction of the computational cost. Simulations performed at 300 K further indicate that the qualitative sorption trends remain largely the same when including finite-temperature effects. These findings demonstrate that MACE-driven MD can be used as an effective high-throughput prescreening tool for defect-related studies in 2D materials, specially related to sorption. While conventional first-principles methods, such as DFT, remain necessary for accurate and quantitative validation, the use of MLIPs enables MD simulations of finite-temperature defect behavior at a fraction of the computational cost of DFT-based MD.

Oxygen on Rh(100) revisited with ultra-high precision

Lutz Hammer, Tilman Kißlinger, Margareta Wagner, Reinhard B. Neder, Michael Schmid, Ulrike Diebold

We have reanalyzed the 0.25 and 0.50 monolayer (ML) phases of oxygen on the catalytically important Rh(100) surface by quantitative low-energy electron diffraction (LEED), variable-temperature scanning tunneling microscopy (STM) and density functional theory (DFT). For both phases, the crystallographic structure could be determined by LEED structure analyses with single-digit picometer precision enabled by ultra-low Pendry R factors of R$_\mathrm{P}$ $\approx$ 0.07. For the (2$\times$2)-O phase at 0.25 ML we find oxygen adsorbed at fourfold hollow sites in accordance to earlier results [Surf. Sci. 207 (1988) 55], however, with a pronounced buckling within the second layer of the otherwise unreconstructed substrate. For the 0.50 monolayer phase, we recently identified a (2$\sqrt{2}$ $\times$ 2$\sqrt{2}$)R45$^\circ$-4O ground state structure [Phys. Rev. Res. 8 (2026) 023296], which decomposes into a large number of nano-domains already at temperatures below 100 K. In this paper we provide a detailed analysis of the atomic structure and the ordering behavior of this phase and resolve long-standing discrepancies with the original (2$\times$2)pg model [Phys. Rev. Lett. 82 (1999) 4874]. We further prove by short-range order simulations that the coherent superposition of scattering contributions of the nano-domains leads to a virtual extinction of diffraction spots, which has concealed the true periodicity and symmetry of this phase for so long.

Enabling large language model agents for high-fidelity many-body perturbation theory calculations

Benran Zhang, Byeongchan Lee, Zien Zhu, Chih-En Hsu, Zhenfa Zheng, Hung-Chung Hsueh

Many-body perturbation theory (MBPT) methods, including the GW approximation, the GW plus Bethe-Salpeter equation (GW-BSE) method, and GW perturbation theory (GWPT), among others, provide accurate first-principles descriptions of excited-state properties and multiparticle interactions of materials. However, their use remains limited due to complex multi-software workflows, interdependent physical parameters, and substantial theoretical expertise. Large language model (LLM) agents, which couple a language model to software tools, show potential in automating computational tasks, but MBPT remains an unaddressed challenge. We show that LLM agents tested in our work, including frontier models such as Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5, do not reliably construct MBPT workflows in one attempt, suggesting that correct setup requires expert knowledge and consistency that current models lack. Across tested model-harness combinations, out-of-the-box agents average 54%, 36%, and 19% on GW, GW-BSE, and GWPT workflow benchmarks, with strong model dependence and large run-to-run variation. Errors typically originate from cross-stage inconsistencies that surface only several stages after they are introduced. We therefore develop AI-Donkey, an agentic framework that combines expert-curated knowledge with deterministic execution to enable high-fidelity GW, GW-BSE, and GWPT calculations and analyses. With AI-Donkey, all tested agents, from frontier proprietary models to open-weight models, produce complete, runnable MBPT workflows with no observed run-to-run variation. We further demonstrate autonomous calculations and post-processing on 1D, 2D, and bulk materials, including the many-body enhancement of Fröhlich electron-phonon coupling in polar semiconductors. Our approach provides a general framework for knowledge engineering to enable reliable agentic workflows in computational materials science.

What Do Foundation Models for Market Microstructure Learn? A Controlled Study with Synthetic Limit Order Books

Fernando Acero

Autoregressive transformers trained on limit order book (LOB) message streams are increasingly proposed as foundation models for market microstructure. Because the generative process of real markets is unknown, these models can be judged only by the realism of their output, leaving open what they actually learn. We make the question answerable by inverting the setup: we train GPT-style models on order flow from simulators whose latent parameters are known, so that the Bayes-optimal predictor, the generating state, and the exact data entropy serve as references. Measured against them, next-token pretraining on order flow implements amortized Bayesian inference over market regimes. The learned predictive matches the exact Bayes optimum to within a millinat per token and approaches the oracle floor even on a nonstationary family whose latent state drifts within an episode, a regime that requires filtering. The latent regime is linearly decodable from frozen activations, well above baselines, so a frozen-model read-out calibrates simulator parameters more accurately than windowed maximum likelihood at a fraction of the cost. In our experiments these capabilities are not produced by scale: capacity and token volume saturate early, and it is the diversity of training regimes, not the token count, that determines generalization to unseen markets. On real NASDAQ data, zero-shot transfer is set by how well the simulator prior covers real flow: an enriched mixture nearly closes the gap to an in-domain baseline, and pretraining beats from-scratch training at every budget across held-out tickers. Known ground truth turns average next-token loss, which is uninformative near the entropy floor, into an exact comparison against the Bayes-optimal and oracle predictors.

A GENERIC-Informed Neural Network for Generative Rheology

Imanol García de Beristain, David Nieto Simavilla, Marco Ellero

We present GINNGER (GENERIC-Informed Neural Network for GEnerative Rheology), a framework for discovering thermodynamically admissible constitutive equations from microstructural flow data. Unlike existing approaches that learn constitutive operators, GINNGER reformulates constitutive discovery as the learning of the underlying thermodynamic generators from which constitutive equations emerge. Embedding the GENERIC formalism into the learning process restricts the search to constitutive models that satisfy the first and second laws of thermodynamics by construction, eliminating the need to prescribe phenomenological constitutive equations a priori. A second conceptual advance is a spectral formulation of constitutive discovery in the intrinsic eigenspace of the polymer conformation tensor. This provides a kinematics-independent thermodynamic state space in which the thermodynamic generators depend only on the instantaneous microstructural state, while flow kinematics acts solely as an external driver transporting the system through this space. Consequently, data from shear, extensional, and mixed flows contribute to the discovery of a single constitutive model that generalizes across deformation protocols and flow geometries. By learning thermodynamic generators rather than high-dimensional constitutive operators, GINNGER substantially reduces the complexity of the discovery problem while preserving thermodynamic consistency, material objectivity, and physical interpretability by construction. The framework is validated using high-fidelity simulations, accurately reconstructing constitutive behavior and predicting complex flow fields across previously unseen geometries. GINNGER establishes a new paradigm for constitutive discovery by combining generative, spectral, and thermodynamically grounded learning within a unified framework for data-driven modeling of complex fluids.

Physics-Informed Machine Learning for Response Tensors in Crystalline Magnets: Application to Kagome Metals

Yu-Hsuan Lin, Joel E. Moore

The prediction of response tensors from magnetic textures is a crucial challenge in the study of noncollinear magnets. Existing methods for constructing descriptors of a given response tensor are limited to first-order multipole expansions or restricted to simple magnetic structures. In this work, we construct a complete symmetry-invariant descriptor basis for arbitrary crystal structures and magnetic textures, providing interpretable features for machine learning modeling of response tensors. We modify the standard principal component analysis framework and derive uncorrelated order parameters that are hierarchically stable: extending the order parameter space to include higher-degree spin order introduces additional order parameters that are uncorrelated to the previous ones. We apply this framework to regression and classification of the anomalous Hall conductivity (AHC) in a magnetic kagome lattice. In particular, in the regime where AHC is well modeled by linear regression of the symmetry-invariant order parameters, we find that the squared regression coefficients, ranked by their magnitude, obey a Zipf-like power law: $β_{r}^2 \propto r^{-α}$. Our method provides a systematic route to construct interpretable order parameters of all spin orders that connect the symmetry of a magnetic crystal to its response tensors.

TOPilot can be used directly alongside topas-editor, via a stand-alone topilot-wizard, or via command line interfaces or desktop apps from major AI providers. TOPilot was developed and tested using Claude large language models, but can be configured to use other cloud or local models. topas-editor and TOPilot are freely available.

Interplay between high-entropy effects and the superionic transition in fluorite oxides: A liquid-like perspective

Fengnian Zhuang, Gaosheng Yan, Hong Chen, Yi Zhang, Wenshan Yu, Minglong Xu

Minor actinide (MA)-bearing mixed oxide (MOX), a prominent high-entropy ceramic (HEC), is a promising candidate for advanced fast reactor fuels. Using a hybrid Monte Carlo/Molecular Dynamics (MC/MD) approach, we reveal that Np and Am segregation establishes a distinct short-range order (SRO) that induces severe lattice distortion. We uncover a competitive mechanism between localized high-entropy blocking effects and collective superionic dynamics, where the anionic kinetic and structural evolution within the 2000-2800 K range is partitioned and governed by the Tammann crossover temperature and superionic transition temperature. In the low-temperature regime, severe lattice distortions induced by the SRO structures create localized electrostatic and strain-coupled potential wells, which spatially confine the O-ions and lead to their sluggish diffusion. The system exhibits increased dynamical heterogeneity as thermal activation alleviates these structural constraints. Crucially, these localized traps spontaneously transform into a highly interconnected, string-like polyhedral topological network. Driven by the maximization of configurational entropy, this structural change creates low-viscosity transport pathways that trigger confinement-enhanced diffusion. Consequently, a dramatic kinetic inversion occurs, where the O-ion mobility of SRO model surpasses that of random solid solution (RSS) model via an entropy-driven compensation mechanism. Finally, through Gibbs free energy minimization and defect analysis, structural and thermodynamic origins of the superionic transition in HEC are elucidated. This study unveils how local chemical ordering regulates ion transport under extreme conditions, providing theoretical support for the design of high-performance nuclear fuels with both superior radiation tolerance and optimized mass transfer capabilities.

We study the conditional distribution of limit-order volume submitted over the next five minutes, given the current order book and recent order flow. The task requires capturing price placement, total volume, and predictive uncertainty jointly. Coordinate-wise diffusion losses do not explicitly account for price displacement, while normalizing fields to unit mass removes information about total volume. Agreement on selected summary statistics also does not establish fidelity to the joint field distribution. We introduce OrderFlowDiT, a conditional Diffusion Transformer for unnormalized bid and ask volume-at-price fields, trained with an unbalanced optimal-transport (UOT) auxiliary loss. In the primary comparison on 1950 validation conditions across 30 U.S. equities, adding UOT reduces full-field transport cost by 45.8% and the transport energy score by 26.1% relative to training with mean squared error alone. Both measures improve for every stock. These findings support incorporating price geometry and volume into generative modeling, with field accuracy and predictive uncertainty evaluated together.

Bond-informed search for ordered configurations in multi-principal element alloys

Here we introduce MECx (Minimum Energy Configuration eXplorer), a framework that guides Monte Carlo searches with a surrogate energy model trained on configurations stratified in bond-count space. These configurations exhibit distinct pair-specific ordering, including B2- and B32-like motifs, and undergo progressive disordering as temperature increases. The MPEA-trained models also transfer to selected binary subsystems without retraining. The resulting ordered configurations agree with those obtained by direct DFT-based Monte Carlo and produce experimentally distinguishable superlattice reflections in simulated neutron diffraction patterns.

A Comprehensive Survey of Crystal Structural Prototypes: Construction, Classification, and Applications

Jianjun Hu, Ying Feng, Rongzhi Dong, Jeffrey Hu, Remy Rogers, Dipannoy Gupta

Crystal structural prototypes describe recurring atomic arrangements across chemically distinct materials. Prototype libraries are built through crystallographic curation, standardization, geometric matching, local-environment clustering, and periodic-graph analysis, and support chemical substitution, derivative enumeration, high-throughput screening, structure solution, crystal structure prediction (CSP), and generative design. This survey compares methods by the structural information they retain and the equivalence rules they impose, distinguishing crystallographic structure types, geometric realizations, chemistry-anonymous topologies, and chemical decorations. We review curated and automatically extracted prototype libraries (AFLOW, CSPD, LAE-ICSPD, EMFDTW, TYPIX, ICSD structure types), their source databases, and algorithms for assigning family membership. A GraphID-based analysis of samples from ICSD, the Materials Project, OQMD, and LeMat shows that the 100 most frequent families in each sample cover 30 to 67 percent of analyzed structures, while 72 to 82 percent of observed families are singletons. The GraphIDs of up to 76 percent of structures change when the neighbor tolerance is varied. In our comparison of 12 generative CSP models, template retrieval achieves the highest top-1 success rate. Removing a reduced-stoichiometry class from training introduces new failures in 50 to 78 percent of the test structures of that class, which measures sensitivity to stoichiometric coverage, not generalization to unseen prototypes. We relate these findings to earlier statistics of structure types, space groups, and periodic nets, and to protein-fold coverage studies. We propose independent-database coverage measurements, discovery-rate analyses, and family-held-out tests to assess how prototype reuse affects prediction accuracy, generalization, and novelty.

Material dependence of direct light-induced charge injection from metallic nanoparticles into adsorbates

Jordan Alan Edwards, Johannes Lischner

Direct injection of charge from metallic nanoparticles into adsorbed molecules via the chemical interface damping of localized surface plasmons has emerged as a promising avenue to avoid the rapid thermalization of hot carriers. In this paper, we develop an approach for predicting hot-electron and hot-hole injection rates from large spherical nanoparticles into adsorbed carbon monoxide (CO) molecules. The approach combines a detailed description of the adsorbate-metal interactions via ab initio density functional theory with a macroscopic electrodynamics treatment of the localized surface plasmon. We apply this approach to nine different metals including both traditional (Ag, Au, Cu) and emerging (Al) plasmonic metals as well as non-plasmonic metals (Ni, Rh, Pd, Ir, Pt). For all metals, hot-electron injection is larger than hot-hole injection. This is a consequence of the alignment of the CO frontier orbitals, with the CO lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) being closer to the metal Fermi energy than the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO). The largest electron injection rates in the optical photon ranges are obtained for Al, Ag, Cu and Au, with Cu and Au showing significant plasmonic enhancement. The non-plasmonic metals show weak electron injection as a consequence of their high LUMO energies relative to the metal Fermi level. The results of our study pave the way towards a rational design of metallic nanoscale photocatalysts for specific chemical processes.
