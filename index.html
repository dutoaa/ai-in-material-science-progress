Towards Multiscale Molecular Representation Learning through Geometry-Based Fragmentation for Polymer Property Prediction

Makoto Takamoto, Chaitanya Sampara, Viktor Zaverkin, Daniel Oñoro-Rubio, Mathias Niepert

It is well established that molecular substructures can contribute unequally to chemical and physical properties, with specific motifs or side chains often playing dominant roles. Fragment-based representations have therefore emerged as an effective strategy for incorporating chemically meaningful inductive biases, as exemplified by molecular fingerprints and fragment-based attention mechanisms. However, many existing fragmentation-based machine learning methods rely on predefined chemical rules, which may yield suboptimal fragmentation for previously unseen or chemically unfamiliar structures and often overlook the importance of multiscale molecular representations. To address these limitations, we formulate molecular fragmentation as a radius-constrained optimization problem inspired by the k-center formulation, where the number of clusters is not fixed a priori but emerges from the specified radius, and propose a geometry-based fragmentation method that partitions arbitrary molecular or material structures using largely geometric information, without relying on predefined chemical fragmentation rules. The method provides flexible control over fragment size, enabling the construction of multiscale fragment representations for scale-adaptive modeling. Building on this framework, we develop a geometric fragment-based attention mechanism that integrates multiscale structural information and incorporate it into a multimodal machine learning model combining p-SMILES representations with three-dimensional (3D) conformational information. Experiments on a benchmark polymer dataset show that incorporating 3D conformational information improves prediction accuracy for several properties over models based exclusively on p-SMILES representations. Explainable artificial intelligence analyses further provide insights into the physical relevance of the learned representations.

Inverse design of superconductors and semiconductors with differentiable foundation models

Cristóbal Méndez, Justin Tahmassebpur, Tomás A. Arias

Designing a crystal for a target property requires choosing its elements, stoichiometry, and atomic structure together, yet gradients exist only for the continuous structural variables. We introduce differentiable Equiformer fast effective-atom theory (DEFEAT), which lets a pretrained EquiformerV3 foundation model accept continuous species weights and site occupancies without retraining on fractional data, thereby enabling gradient-based materials optimization. Gradients from fine-tuned property models and a machine-learned energy penalty then guide updates to composition, atom count, atomic positions, and lattice vectors in a single optimization. In a search for phonon-mediated superconductors, density-functional perturbation theory on 45 structurally novel designs gives isotropic Allen-Dynes critical temperatures above 10 K for 39; the highest is 24.0 K for ZrTiNb2CN3, and H4LiPd3 (23.2 K) lies on the static DFT hull. In searches for photovoltaic absorbers, SCAN calculations confirm ten novel crystal structures with band gaps within 20% of a 1.34 eV target on their respective raw or empirically corrected scales, all within 70 meV/atom of the DFT hull, and show that the systematic gap error of the training functional propagates into the designs unless the objective corrects for it. Quasiharmonic free energies from machine-learned potentials further show that finite-temperature competition with solid phases can reorder candidates relative to the static hull.

ZeoBench: A Benchmark for Self-Supervised Learning on 3D Zeolite Representations

Aaron Sun, Yachan Liu, Ping Yang, Peng Bai, Subhransu Maji

Zeolites are an important class of porous crystalline materials with broad applications in gas storage, separations, and catalysis. Although hundreds of thousands of experimentally realized and hypothetical zeolite structures are known, identifying materials optimized for a target application remains challenging because property labels are expensive to obtain and available only for limited structure-molecule pairs. While prior work has demonstrated that 3D neural representations can predict zeolite properties effectively, it remains unclear which representation-learning strategies are most effective in the low-data regime relevant to materials discovery. We present ZeoBench, a benchmark for self-supervised learning on dense 3D representations of all-silica zeolites, and evaluate the resulting features on six existing downstream adsorption-property prediction tasks, across a range of training set sizes. Our study compares self-supervised 3D pretraining on volumetric zeolite data, adaptations of image-pretrained 2D ConvNets and vision transformers to 3D via multiview and channel-inflation strategies, and 3D ConvNets pretrained on out-of-domain volumetric datasets, alongside hand-crafted descriptors and supervised baselines. Using our pretraining and evaluation framework, we find self-supervised 3D representations outperform existing methods and achieve state-of-the-art performance across both in-domain label regimes and for the unseen domain of metal organic frameworks. Learned representations consistently outperform hand-crafted descriptors, and convolutional architectures are generally more effective than vision transformers in this setting. These results establish ZeoBench as a practical benchmark for 3D representation learning in zeolites and provide guidance for data-limited adsorption property prediction.

Native point defects in rock-salt and wurtzite MnSe: formation energies across the growth window and spin splitting in the A-type order

Tomás Rojas Solorzano, Dale Igram

Manganese selenide grows both as rock-salt $α$-MnSe, a type-II antiferromagnet, and as metastable wurtzite w-MnSe, whose magnetic order has not been measured. We compute the neutral Mn and Se vacancies and the two antisites of both polymorphs with density functional theory plus a linear-response Hubbard $U$, between the Se-rich limit bounded by MnSe$_2$ and the Mn-rich limit set by the measured formation enthalpy. In $α$-MnSe the Mn vacancy is lowest over most of the window (0.92 eV under Se-rich conditions), consistent with its p-type conduction. In w-MnSe, the Se antisite is lowest under Se-rich conditions, narrowly in the A-type order and clearly in the stripe order, and the Se vacancy is lowest under Mn-rich conditions. The Se vacancy has nearly the same formation energy in both polymorphs, whereas defects that change the number of Mn atoms differ by 0.4-1.1 eV, although DFT+$U$ places wurtzite below rock salt, contrary to experiment. A spin-degenerate stripe order lies 14 meV per formula unit below the altermagnetic A-type order of w-MnSe, and the formation energies shift between these orders by up to 0.24 eV, most for the Mn vacancy. Three of the four defects leave an uncompensated moment of 3-5 $μ_\mathrm{B}$. In the A-type order, band unfolding shows that the sign-reversing splitting of the most strongly split band, about 230 meV and 3 eV below the valence-band maximum, keeps its peak height within the 8% scatter of the analysis at 3% defects, while points on its shoulders move by up to about 70 meV. Band-edge states are not resolved.

SteelAgent: LLM-Orchestrated, Physics-Informed Steel Property Prediction with Audited Generalization and Calibrated Uncertainty

Aleksandr Volkov, Roman Sultimov, Mikhail Kuzin, Yury Maximov

Steel property models can perform well on random test splits even when they extrapolate poorly to unfamiliar alloys. SteelAgent couples metallurgical descriptors and machine-learning models with a language-model interface for property prediction, uncertainty reporting and inverse design. We examine how the choice of held-out unit changes the conclusions drawn from SteelBench tensile-strength evaluations. For gradient boosting, pooled out-of-fold $R^2$ fell from 0.859 on a random split to 0.113 when steel families were held out. Empirical coverage of nominal 90% prediction intervals ranged from 91.0% under random splitting to 37.6% on held-out sources. The effect of physics-derived features depended on the evaluation. They increased gradient-boosting $R^2$ by 0.2534 in a 17-family holdout analysis and decreased it by 0.358 in a separate four-grade laboratory analysis. We report group-level errors and a synthetic regression experiment illustrating how calibration can deteriorate on held-out groups. These results motivate reporting the evaluation population alongside a property estimate or compliance score.

Spectral symmetry fingerprints in equivariant representations

Jiacheng Cen, Anyi Li, Ning Lin, Zihe Wang, Ji-Rong Wen, Wenbing Huang

Symmetry governs the organization of physical structures, while symmetry breaking reveals their transformations. In geometric deep learning, symmetry imposes rank constraints on equivariant representations, limiting the expressive capacity of equivariant graph neural networks (GNNs). Here we show that these constraints can instead be exploited: the singular-value spectra of equivariant representations provide a unified fingerprint of structural symmetry. We introduce spectral symmetry fingerprints (SymFP), which enable discrete point-group identification through rank-template matching and continuous symmetry analysis by quantifying spectral energy beyond target rank constraints. SymFP is orientation invariant and architecture agnostic, enabling a parameter-free GNN implementation. Its spectra can be reused across multiple target groups, and the resulting leakage scores satisfy subgroup monotonicity. Across synthetic structures, molecules, coordination complexes and large biological assemblies, SymFP accurately identifies point groups, tracks controlled symmetry breaking and reveals local and degree-resolved symmetry changes that can be obscured by global geometric measures. These results establish the spectral structure of equivariant representations as a general framework for characterizing symmetry across physical systems.

An All-Atom Model of the Resorcinol-Formaldehyde Aerogel Skeleton from DFT and Molecular Dynamics: Validation and Iron Incorporation

Shoeb Athar, Jérémy Guazzagaloppa, Philippe Jund

Resorcinol-formaldehyde (RF) aerogels are lightweight, ultra-porous thermal superinsulators whose complex amorphous structure has not yet been described at the all-atom level. Here, we build an all-atom model of the RF aerogel skeleton by combining density functional theory (DFT) and classical molecular dynamics (MD), validate it against experimental infrared and density measurements, and use it to study the incorporation of iron dopants. The RF base unit was optimized with Gaussian, DMol$^{3}$ and SIESTA, and its calculated infrared spectrum agreed with experiment. Comparing classical force fields with DFT for branched systems of two to four base units (140-278 atoms) led to the choice of COMPASS II. A 15,012-atom system of 54 branched RF units, not cross-linked to each other, aggregated into a single cluster in the canonical (NVT) ensemble and reached an equilibrium density of 1.296 g.cm$^{-3}$ in the isobaric-isothermal (NPT) ensemble, in agreement with the experimental skeletal density (1.3 g.cm$^{-3}$). Then, 5 wt$\%$ iron (109 atoms) was added to the equilibrated structure: during 1 ns of NPT simulation, the fraction of iron atoms embedded in the skeleton rose from 63$\%$ to 71$\%$, and the Fe-C radial distribution function peaked at 3 Å. The model thus provides an experimentally supported starting point for screening fillers and dopants in RF gels, with prospective application to their thermoelectric functionalization, which we have previously demonstrated experimentally with basalt and PANOX fibres. Future extensions could address skeleton properties relevant to thermal insulation and the pyrolysis of RF gels into carbon and metal-doped carbon aerogels.

Physics Informed Priors for Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials with Uncertainty Quantification

George I. Simmons, Thomas D. Swinburne, Thomas Hudson, James R. Kermode

Standard uncertainty quantification methods for machine learning interatomic potentials often fail to quantify the effects of model misspecification despite its prominence as a source of error. To combat this, misspecification-aware uncertainty schemes have been gaining attention, pointwise optimal parameter set regression (POPS-Regression) among them. However, no guarantees on the physicality of the posterior distribution are given and uncertainty estimates can be conservative compared to the true error. To combat this, we present a model misspecification aware scheme that builds on POPS-Regression by allowing users to specify physical priors that are enforced across the obtained ensemble of models. This allows the user to incorporate misspecification-aware uncertainties without compromising on physical properties. The scheme is applicable to any physical quantity that can be represented by a convex set of parameters and so is robust for a wide variety of static properties which can help inform dynamical simulations. Here, we demonstrate the scheme for phononic vibrations and elastic properties. The scheme is also applied to both linear and message-passing neural network potentials by exploiting the linearity available in both architectures; for neural network potentials we approximate the readout layer as a linear model based on fixed non-linear features. The versatility of the method provides an alternative to hand-crafted or active learning approaches to data-set construction to correct physical failures by adding data thus reducing the computational overhead for designing potentials for specific applications.

Jackknife-conformal uncertainty for linear ACE potentials: calibrated forces and silicon properties compared with Bayesian and POPS baselines

James R. Kermode

Machine-learned interatomic potentials are misspecified, so Bayesian posterior variances understate their errors. For linear atomic cluster expansion (ACE) potentials we derive calibrated uncertainties from one misspecification-robust parameter covariance, the delete-one-configuration jackknife. Scaled by one conformal quantile from a held-out part of the training set, it gives per-atom force uncertainties close to their target coverage on held-out silicon data and on cracks left out of the fit; screw dislocations left out are under-covered by the smaller of the two models. With one scale fitted to the forces of a held-out test split, the same parameter distribution is propagated to 21 properties of silicon, from elastic constants to defect and surface energies, by implicit differentiation through each relaxation and by committees, and is tested against density functional theory (DFT) without tuning on any property. For a smaller and a larger model the intervals cover most properties, and the larger model's are about 4 times tighter. The Bayesian posterior with one calibrated scale has the wrong shape: it covers fewer properties and has far lower joint evidence. Pointwise optimal parameter sets (POPS), once their hypercube is numerically defined, performs comparably on the properties, but its per-atom force regions are not calibrated to a target (0.95 and 0.84 at a nominal 0.90). Where the jackknife fails is relaxed point defects, whose training cells are all hot snapshots: a bias that every configuration shares is invisible to a jackknife, but it can be measured from the training labels, and as a discrepancy term, devised after the property results were seen, it brings the coverage close to nominal.

From local learning to global electronic properties in large-scale systems

Yu Cheng, Zhichang Fu, Yechen Chen, Yonggang Li, Weiqing Zhou, Shengjun Yuan

Machine learning from local atomic environments offers an efficient route to electronic structure, but evaluating global electronic properties in large systems remains computationally demanding. Here we combine real space charge density learning with stochastic propagation using Kohn-Sham Hamiltonians constructed from the predicted densities. The framework avoids target system self-consistent iterations and explicit diagonalization, while hierarchical blocking enables dense grid inference within limited GPU memory. We demonstrate density of states calculations for one million Si atoms and optical and transport calculations for over one hundred thousand atoms, without training separate models for each property. This framework extends the practical scale of electronic structure calculations within Kohn-Sham DFT, opening opportunities to investigate how atomic structure governs electronic, optical and transport properties at length scales relevant to real materials and devices.

Does a Trading Policy Need to Know What It Trades? Microstructure Invariance and Zero-Shot Transfer of Execution Policies

Hieu Tran, Hong Yu

Execution algorithms are calibrated asset by asset, because the queues, trade rates and bid-ask spreads they face differ by orders of magnitude across assets. For reinforcement learning this is zero-shot generalisation across contexts of very different scales, and microstructure invariance proposes a normalisation fixed by theory: measured in each asset's own units of time, size and price, markets look alike. In an exact order-by-order replay of public Nasdaq data, we train policies once, on 954 stocks in 2019, to choose between waiting in the queue and crossing the spread, and evaluate them unchanged on 1,964 unseen stocks in 2020-21, 2,629 stocks in 2025-26, crypto pairs and a second exchange. First, a policy that sees a stock only through its recent trade rate, trade size, spread and price increment (tick) costs within 0.015 spreads per share (a fifth of what learning saves) of a policy whose training data included the stock, and the stock's own history does not help it; it comes within a similar margin of policies trained on the second exchange when every episode counts equally, and within a wider one on crypto. Second, in mean cost before fees, invariant units beat raw units, z-scores and asset descriptors, but by under a basis point: most of their advantage is information and a common cost scale that a learner can also take as plain inputs, and none of the representations tested extrapolates to unseen spread regimes. Third, a near-zero transfer gap to a pooled comparator is weak evidence that a representation is right: we show that such a comparator detects only a small share of what a poor representation loses, and confirm it in synthetic markets and on pendulums.

PolyGPT: A Decoder-Only Transformer for Polymer Electrolyte Design

Tanmay Sarkar Akash, Siddhartha Das

Designing polymer electrolytes requires control over both material properties and chemical structure. Here, we present PolyGPT, a Transformer with 38.3 million parameters that generates polymer repeat units from optional property targets, requests to include or exclude a specified chemical feature, and an electrolyte-domain condition. Using the model selected by validation loss, we compared unconditional generation with requests for electrolyte-associated structures, a low glass-transition temperature (Tg), or both. Unconditional and electrolyte-only generation achieved structural validity of 99.46% and 99.59%, respectively. However, electrolyte conditioning increased repetition: uniqueness among valid outputs fell from 99.99% to 51.74%. At a Tg target of 246.11 K, we compared generation with and without electrolyte conditioning, and adding the electrolyte condition slightly reduced the predictor-estimated mean absolute error from 26.97 to 26.07 K, but the fraction of all attempts yielding distinct valid polymers within 50 K of the target fell from 82.35% to 32.31%. When we also required absence from the training catalog, these yields were 59.90% and 19.71%, respectively. The lower yield persisted with a tighter 25 K tolerance. Domain conditioning also weakened control of inclusion requests: the mean fraction of attempts producing valid polymers containing the requested chemical feature fell from 94.27% to 31.27%. Across nine checkpoints, earlier models sometimes produced more distinct, catalog-unseen polymers meeting the Tg target: 10,000 checkpoint model with electrolyte conditioning and 30,000 checkpoint model without it. Together, these results show why property error alone is insufficient for evaluating conditional polymer generation. It must be considered alongside repetition, training-catalog novelty, and whether combined design requests are satisfied.

Triboelectric nanogenerators across length scales: linking materials, multiscale modelling and applications

MD Tanzib Ehsan Sanglap, Ans Al Rashid, Abdullah Sayam, Injamamul Arief, Andrei G. Shvarts, Lukasz Kaczmarczyk

Triboelectric nanogenerators (TENGs) convert low-frequency mechanical energy into electricity and enable self-powered sensing. Their performance depends on interactions among material properties, surface morphology, contact mechanics, charge generation and external circuits. This review examines these interactions across length scales to assess how material and interface descriptions support predictive device design. Polymeric, inorganic, composite and natural materials are discussed in relation to charge generation and retention, mechanical compliance, durability and application requirements. Modelling approaches span molecular dynamics and first-principles calculations, rough-surface contact mechanics, device-scale electrostatics and equivalent circuits. Their capabilities are assessed through the inputs they require, the quantities they predict and the experimental evidence used for validation. Particular attention is given to prescribed surface charge density, idealised geometries and nominal contact-area assumptions, which constrain predictions under realistic loading conditions. Applications are organised by macroscale, milliscale and microscale device dimensions to examine requirements for materials, architecture and integration. The review identifies evolving real contact area and contact-dependent charge generation and decay as critical links between interfacial behaviour and electrical output. A staged validation strategy is proposed for contact mechanics, electrostatics and circuit response, with independent testing beyond calibration conditions. These priorities guide the development of multiscale models and reliable TENG systems.

Interplay of Ni Order and Local Moments in Intercalated NixNbS2

Gaurav Pransu, Naveen Singh Dhami, Wojciech Sas, Yuki Utsumi Boucher, Bruno Gudac, Ana Smontara

Magnetic ions intercalated into the van der Waals gaps of layered transition metal dichalcogenides provide a system where local moments interact strongly with a metallic host. How the order of the intercalant sublattice affects their magnetism is important but rarely addressed, although disorder is hard to avoid, in particular in thin films and heterostructures. Here this question is examined in single crystals of Ni$_x$NbS$_2$ with $0.10 \leq x \leq 0.57$, whose composition and Ni order were determined by energy-dispersive x-ray spectroscopy and synchrotron x-ray diffraction. Anisotropic magnetic susceptibility measurements reveal three regimes. Long-range antiferromagnetic order appears only for $0.25 \leq x \leq 0.33$, where the Ni ions form the $2 \times 2$ and $\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3}$ R$30^\circ$ superstructures, and $T_\mathrm{N}$ reaches 96 K at $x = 1/3$. In this range the effective moment, $\approx 2.5 μ_\mathrm{B}$ per Ni, agrees with DFT+$U$ calculations, in which the Ni spin is partly delocalized onto the surrounding S ligands. For $x \leq 0.18$ the dilute, randomly distributed Ni ions remain paramagnetic down to 2 K with a reduced moment. For $x \geq 0.37$, where Ni-Ni contacts are unavoidable, long-range order gives way to a short-range ordered state below 10-15 K with a further reduced moment. As calculations for ordered Ni arrangements find the Ni state nearly independent of $x$, even at $x = 1/2$, the reduction of the moment is attributed to a disorder-enabled reduction of the charge transfer from Ni to the NbS$_2$ layers. The magnetism of Ni$_x$NbS$_2$ is thus governed by the (dis)order of the Ni sublattice rather than by the Ni concentration alone.

Symmetry-Gated Sliding: Decoupling Interlayer Translation from Polarization Reversal in Boron Nitride Ferroelectrics

Yinan Wang, Teruyasu Mizoguchi

Electric-field-driven polarization switching in sliding ferroelectrics depends on how local interlayer motion is accommodated by the surrounding structure. Yet the relationship between sliding, domain-wall (DW) propagation, and collective domain evolution under changing vector fields remains unclear. Here, we show that local symmetry governs the coupling between field-induced forces and interlayer sliding, while DW connectivity determines how local motion contributes to net domain conversion. These effects are examined across three structural scales, from monodomains and isolated DWs to reconstructed moire networks. We investigate bilayer hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN) using molecular dynamics with a machine-learning interatomic potential that incorporates field-dependent electric enthalpy. An initially monodomain bilayer switches through directional wrinkling and interlayer sliding, while the barrier along a constrained flat path remains finite. Pre-existing DWs propagate with velocities governed by their shear and tensile character, and an additional in-plane field tunes wall velocity and width according to its orientation. In reconstructed triangular moire networks, shared junctions and boundary curvature mediate domain evolution and recovery after field removal or reversal. Most strikingly, a rotating in-plane field translates the layers and displaces the moiré nodes by approximately 1 AA yet leaves the AB/BA domain-area ratio without detectable change, because threefold connectivity cancels the contributions of individual walls. We term this response polarization-silent sliding. These results distinguish local sliding, DW translation, and net domain conversion as different responses to electric fields and establish how local structural symmetry and DW connectivity shape their selective control.

Conformationally frustrated epitaxy at heteromolecular interface

Jakub Planer, Pavel Procházka, Tomáš Krajňák, Veronika Stará, Daniela Hrubá, Zdeněk Endstrasser

Precise control of molecular heteroepitaxy offers a route to multilayer architectures with well-defined interfaces. However, the role of intramolecular flexibility in epitaxial matching remains insufficiently understood. Here, we investigate the heteroepitaxy of intact 1,3,5-tris(4-carboxyphenyl)benzene (BTB) on a molecular $δ$-layer composed of fully deprotonated BTB molecules on Ag(111). Combining scanning tunneling microscopy, low-energy electron microscopy and diffraction, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy, and density functional theory, we identify the key interactions controlling molecular packing and epitaxial growth. We show that intermolecular bonding motifs are preserved on the molecular template, but their lattice parameters and molecular conformations change as compared to a silver surface. Individual molecules exhibit distinct appearances in scanning tunneling microscopy, consistent with site-dependent conformational variations identified by theory. Our calculations reveal that the competition between molecule-substrate interactions and conformational energy produces different compromise geometries, resulting in conformational frustration at the epitaxial interface. Crucially, constraining BTB into a planar geometry changes the preferred lateral adsorption positions, demonstrating that molecular flexibility directly modifies the energetic preferences for epitaxial registry. Comparison with more rigid molecular overlayers further supports this interpretation. Our findings reveal that molecular flexibility contributes to epitaxial matching by allowing unfavorable molecule-substrate interactions to be compensated by energetically favorable conformations while preserving the intermolecular network.

Equivariant Deep Learning in Direct-Product Space for Electronic-Structure Hamiltonian Prediction

Shi Yin, Zujian Dai, Xinyang Pan, Lixin He

Most existing equivariant architectures for Hamiltonian prediction propagate features in direct-sum spaces of irreducible spherical tensors, whereas the target Hamiltonian is naturally an operator block in a direct-product representation over local orbital and spin bases. This representation mismatch fragments contiguous Hamiltonian blocks, obscures direct manipulation of orbital couplings, and organizes features into irregular angular-momentum components that require numerous transformations and are poorly aligned with efficient hardware execution and memory management. To address both the representation mismatch and computational bottleneck, we propose DP-Learner, an equivariant learning framework that performs message passing directly on Hamiltonian-like operator blocks in the direct-product space. Theoretically, we propose Lie-generator-based directional embedding and invariantly weighted left--right products and contractions that preserve equivariance to SO(3), SU(2), and spatial inversions, without explicit Clebsch--Gordan decomposition or inverse reconstruction. Methodologically, we instantiate this principle as a Transformer architecture, in which Hamiltonian-like feature matrices or tensors evolve through equivariant operators in the direct-product space, while non-linear invariant scalar routing controls structured gating over matrix units, directional propagation, attention, and environment-embedded context interactions. We further construct MP-HAM-110K, a large-scale Hamiltonian dataset comprising more than 110,000 material structures covering 68 elements with both Hamiltonian and wavefunction data. Experiments on MP-HAM-110K and Materials-HAM-SOC demonstrate that DP-Learner dramatically improves Hamiltonian prediction accuracy and inference efficiency over the state-of-the-art method.

Complex Martensitic Microstructures in ${\rm Ti}_{76}{\rm Nb}_{22}{\rm Al}_{2}$

John M. Ball, Francesco Della Porta, Tomonari Inamura

We analyze the unusual microstructure resulting from the cubic to orthorhombic phase transformation in the shape-memory alloy ${\rm Ti}_{76}{\rm Nb}_{22}{\rm Al}_{2}$. This seems to represent an approximate experimental realization of a deformation having a finite number of incompatible deformation gradients, which has been known to exist only as a theoretical possibility. Because the middle eigenvalue $γ$ of the transformation strain is very close to 1, the phase transformation in this alloy takes place via nucleation of exact austenite-martensite interfaces rather than, as usual for other alloys, by classical austenite-martensite interfaces separating the austenite from a finely-twinned laminate of martensite. According to the moving mask approximation of Della Porta this leads to the question whether a microstructure can be formed from the set $H$ of the twelve matrices ${\mathbf V}_i$ on the martensite energy wells that are rank-one connected to the identity, even though these matrices are mutually incompatible. We show that when $γ=1$ and the transformation is volume preserving, such a microstructure is possible that does not deform the grain boundary. This is a consequence of $H$ forming a degenerate $T_{12}$ configuration in the sense of Kirchheim. In fact there are such microstructures supported on just 8 of the 12 matrices in $H$. However there is no deformation satisfying the same boundary condition whose gradient belongs to $H$ almost everywhere. An additional prediction, confirmed experimentally, is that the macroscopic deformation gradient equals the identity everywhere. We also analyse the approximate compatibility of the ${\mathbf V}_i$. Although mutually incompatible, they are nearly compatible, with some pairs much more nearly compatible than others, these pairs corresponding to interfaces that are more frequently observed.

Reversible Interchain Associations Dominate Plateau Elasticity in Conjugated Polymers

Juncheng Zheng, Xiaobing Zuo, Xiaodan Gu, Shiwang Cheng

The modulus plateau is widely regarded as a hallmark of topological entanglement in homopolymer melts and has been used to identify entanglement in semiflexible conjugated polymers. Using regiorandom poly(3-alkylthiophenes) as a model system, we show that their plateau elasticity relaxes through an Arrhenius process decoupled from segmental and terminal dynamics, reorganizes persistently under mechanical perturbation, and varies systematically with side-chain length. These behaviors are hard to reconcile with a purely topological-entanglement picture and instead support a transient network dominated by reversible interchain associations, consistent with π-π interactions between conjugated backbone segments. The results thus demonstrate that plateau elasticity alone does not uniquely identify topological entanglement in conjugated polymers. More broadly, they identify the strength and lifetime of interchain association as molecular design parameters linking backbone packing to stress relaxation, viscoelastic properties, and processability in conjugated polymers, complementing their established role in electronic structure and charge transport.
