Materials research with a general-purpose agent

Paulo R. Pires, Théo Cavignac, Tiago F. T. Cerqueira, Hai-Chen Wang, Silvana Botti, Miguel A. L. Marques

Over the last decade we have built Alexandria, an open database of about 15 million density-functional calculations of crystals. It includes PBE, PBEsol, SCAN and TB-mBJ results, structures relaxed at pressures up to 150 GPa, dielectric tensors and about 145,000 electron-phonon calculations, together with more than 400 million structures relaxed with machine-learned potentials. On these data we trained a generative model for crystal structures, a universal interatomic potential and graph neural networks for more than ten properties, from band gaps to superconducting critical temperatures. From the convex hull we computed a reaction graph of the 17.4 billion reactions between its 193,416 stable compounds. Here we give all of this to a general-purpose language-model agent. Working unattended, the agent screens the database for a solar absorber, drops a candidate whose synthesis releases too little energy, and writes a synthesis plan with precursors, crucible and atmosphere. Asked about the well-studied Ba-Zr-S system, it finds an unreported sulfide and designs the experiment that would confirm or rule it out. Asked for an air-stable alternative, it designs one and tests it against new structures from the generative model. With web search alone, the same agent proposes what the literature already discusses. Put together, the data, the models and the reaction graph let a general agent do what none of them does alone: go from a question in plain words to an experiment that a laboratory can run and test.

SiC-BN composites with extreme thermal and environmental stability

Abhijit Biswas, Eugenio Garcia-Granados, Saman Zare, Mozhdeh Mirakhory, JungHun Park, Shuvodeep De

Ceramic materials that maintain structural integrity and resist thermal-shock under extreme conditions are critical for many applications. Here we show that spark plasma sintered composites containing equal weight fractions of hexagonal silicon carbide (6H-SiC) and boron nitride (h-BN) exhibit exceptional stability under extreme high-temperature oxidizing conditions and unusually stable temperature-independent thermal conductivity across a broad temperature range. Under high-velocity oxygen-fuel torch exposure in air, it withstands prolonged heating and thermal-cycling at surface temperatures reaching >1500 °C, whereas pure 6H-SiC and h-BN rapidly fractures or erodes. Microstructural evaluation shows the formation of a multi-phase surface silica layer that further protects the material from oxidation. Finite-element analysis heat-flux map shows that intrinsic porosity mitigates thermal stress, while ReaxFF molecular dynamics simulations reveal thermally driven interfacial reconstruction and the formation of a Si-N/C-B-rich bonding network, enhancing the interfacial cohesion at elevated temperatures. This lightweight, easily machinable ceramic composite will be a promising structural material in extreme environment applications.

Brute-force molecular dynamics simulation of aging in amorphous Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$

Elisa Petroni, Dario Baratella, Omar Abou El Kheir, Andrea Redaelli, Marco Bernasconi

Chalcogenide alloys such as the prototypical Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$ compound are used in phase change electronic memories due to their ability to undergo a fast and reversible transition between the amorphous and crystalline phases upon heating. The amorphous phase is subject to aging that leads to an increase in the electrical resistance, which is detrimental to the operation of the devices. Using a machine learning interatomic potential, we have investigated the structural relaxations responsible for aging in amorphous Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$ in molecular dynamics simulations at temperatures slightly below the glass transition. The aging simulation, lasting about 100 ns, follows an equilibration of 24 ns at 400 K. During aging we observed an enhancement of the Peierls distortion in axial bonds which is a typical feature of these alloys that we relate to the widening of the electronic band gap computed within Density Functional Theory in smaller aged amorphous models. We also observed a marked reduction of the atomic mobility with an increase of the bond lifetimes during aging. However, we do not see a reduction in homopolar bonds, which are expected to be absent in the ideal aged glass and which should be removed on a much longer time scale.

Quantum Material Generation via Coherent Hamiltonian Search

Jiang Yuhan, Matthew Otten

Discovering new materials with targeted properties is a central goal of materials science, yet the space of candidates is vast. Data-driven generative models learn the distribution of known materials, but they extrapolate poorly, and a generated structure is not known to be stable. A material's properties follow from its Hamiltonian, and a quantum computer can calculate them from it. We introduce the quantum material generative algorithm (QMage), a coherent search, guided by the Hamiltonian, over crystalline configurations of atomic composition, unit cell structure, and atomic positions, for one whose calculated properties meet the targets to within a given error tolerance. The computation is a single coherent quantum circuit in which each configuration's Hamiltonian is block-encoded, its target eigenstate isolated and properties evaluated, and the satisfying configurations amplified by fixed-point search and measured. One framework covers many targets, from formation energy and band gaps to elasticity and conductivity. We design the algorithm for the mature fault-tolerant era and estimate its cost in logical qubits and Toffoli depth from a classically routine benchmark to targets where established methods are unreliable. The approach generates candidate materials from first principles, rather than generalizing from those known, and could enable materials discovery when the target properties are difficult to predict classically.

Competing Octahedral Tilting and $B$-Site Off-Centering Govern the Phase Diagram of CsGe$_{1-x}$Sn$_x$Br$_3$ Perovskite Alloys

Apinya Ngoipala, Erik Fransson, Paul Erhart, Julia Wiktor

Lead-free halide perovskites based on Ge and Sn avoid the toxicity of Pb, but the two cations favor different structural distortions. The stereochemically active Ge $4s^2$ lone pair drives polar $B$-site off-centering, whereas the larger Sn$^{2+}$ cation favors octahedral tilting. Here, we map how these two instabilities compete across the full composition range of CsGe$_{1-x}$Sn$_x$Br$_3$ using large-scale molecular dynamics simulations with a charge-aware machine-learned interatomic potential trained on hybrid-functional data. At low temperature the alloy exhibits a morphotropic phase boundary between $x=0.60$ and $0.65$ that separates a ferroelectric monoclinic $Cc$ phase from a monoclinic $P2_1$ phase with mixed antipolar and polar off-centering, with both the tilt pattern and the off-centering order changing discontinuously. The composition-temperature phase diagram reproduces the known transition sequences and temperatures of both end members and reveals an intermediate tetragonal $I4/mcm$ phase near the boundary that has no counterpart in either parent compound. Above the transition temperatures the $B$ cations remain locally off-centered but orientationally disordered. The structural transition at the boundary causes a jump in the band gap at low temperature, whereas at $350$ K the gap decreases smoothly with Sn content. These results establish $B$-site mixing as a means to tune polar and rotational order in lead-free halide perovskites.

Distilling an atomistic foundation model is all you need for large-scale molecular dynamics of semiconductor interfaces

Feiyu Zhou, Wei Qiu, Fei Shuang, Yilun Liu, Shengying Yue, Penghua Ying

State-of-the-art atomistic foundation models provide broadly transferable energy and force predictions, but their direct deployment in large-scale molecular dynamics remains computationally costly. Here we examine whether their knowledge can be distilled into compact potentials while retaining the physical responses of heterogeneous semiconductor interfaces. From 128 pretrained models spanning 22 model families, we select a fixed EquiformerV3 teacher and train atomic cluster expansion (ACE) and neuroevolution potential (NEP) students without target-specific teacher fine-tuning, with both simulation speed and accessible system size improved by several orders of magnitude relative to the teacher model. Matched training configurations and architectures enable direct comparisons with students trained on density-functional-theory (DFT) reference labels. For 79,920-atom BAs/GaN interfaces, direct distillation preserves termination-dependent thermal conductance and dominant spectral features, with conductance differing by at most 6.6% from DFT-trained counterparts. In 29,792-atom Cu/diamond specimens loaded at 500 K, tensile strength and loading work differ by 2.8% and 1.5%, respectively. Exploratory SiC/a-C/SiC simulations retain the principal thermal bottleneck and the direction of the annealing response. Furthermore, a four-model committee retrieves 42-56% of the highest-error teacher configurations with a 10% selection budget and improves high-error force fitting, but does not consistently improve downstream properties. These findings demonstrate that direct distillation can preserve key interfacial thermal and mechanical responses in compact interatomic potentials, opening a path towards large-scale, high-throughput simulations of heterogeneous interfaces.

Edge-scaling softening of a universal machine-learned potential: point-defect formation energies in a coronene graphene quantum dot

Andrii Tuzhykov, Vladimir Soloviev, Arnold Kiv, Andrii Bielinskyi

Universal machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) are increasingly used as computationally efficient surrogates for density functional theory (DFT) in high-throughput nanomaterials screening. Systematic softening has been reported for several universal MLIPs when high-energy and far-from-equilibrium configurations are underrepresented in training data. We examine how this behavior appears at under-coordinated edge sites in graphene quantum dots (GQDs), where a vacancy can have pronounced molecular-radical character. Using coronene (C$_{24}$H$_{12}$), a hexagonal GQD with $D_{6h}$ symmetry and three carbon classes, we benchmark SevenNet (7net-omni-i12) against spin-polarized PBE DFT (GPAW) for monovacancy, divacancy, and Stone-Wales defects. Beyond the full site- and bond-resolved landscapes, we perform a controlled matched-reconstruction comparison in which 28 vacancy and divacancy configurations are scored by both engines using self-consistent chemical-potential reservoirs. In this dataset, SevenNet underestimates all 28 vacancy and divacancy formation energies, and the mean deficit increases with under-coordination within each defect family: 0.30-0.74 eV for monovacancies and 0.65-1.11 eV for divacancies as the number of broken edge C-H bonds increases. By contrast, the mean Stone-Wales energies differ by only 0.013 eV. SevenNet also preserves the ordering of three hydrogen-dependent reconstruction basins and reproduces $D_{6h}$-equivalent sites to within a few meV. The within-class symmetry spread therefore provides a practical diagnostic of basin-sensitive relaxations. Within the present coronene benchmark, these results support MLIP-based structure screening and preliminary ranking, while absolute vacancy energies and multi-basin edge configurations still require DFT validation.

Inter-Nanoparticle Repulsion Effect on Self-Organization in Polymer Nanocomposites: A Langevin Dynamics Simulation Study

Paawan Chandrakanth, Nikos Ch. Karayiannis, Subhalaxmi Das, Supriya Roy

Demixing through liquid-liquid phase separation is relevant to nanoparticle (NP) aggregation in polymer nanocomposites (PNCs), biomolecular compartmentalization, and liquids embedded in elastic networks. Mixing-demixing phase behavior depends on factors, such as particle size distribution, system dimen-sionality, intermolecular interactions, and relative concentrations. Here, using Langevin Dynamics simu-lations, we systematically investigate the effect of inter-nanoparticle repulsion on the self-assembly of PNCs under extreme confinement. A nanoparticle assembly, in the form of a compact droplet, is immersed in a polymer matrix of semiflexible, rod-like chains. By varying the nanoparticle-nanoparticle repulsion strength, while fixing all other interactions, we identify four distinct regimes with respect to the self-assembled NP morphologies: droplet, slab, crystalline droplet, and dispersed. Cluster-based analysis reveals a progressive loss of nanoparticle droplet integrity, leading to fragmentation within the dispersed regime. Local order of the nanoparticle assembly and global order of polymer chains are gauged through refined descriptors, quantifying the degrees of crystallinity and of nematic order, respectively. Interest-ingly, the slab regime exhibits negligible nanoparticle crystallization yet demonstrates the strongest polymer nematic ordering. In contrast, the crystalline droplet displays extensive triangular close-packed crystallites accompanied by a significant reduction in polymer nematic order, owing to its rhomboid shape. In the dispersed regime, nanoparticle fragmentation facilitates efficient mixing and local smectic-like ordering of the polymer chains. These findings demonstrate that fine-tuning inter-NP repulsions can control self-organization and the resulting morphologies, providing insights into PNCs with tailored functionality.

UniHam: A Large-Scale SOC-Complete Dataset and Benchmark for Hamiltonian Learning in Materials

Yuewen Huang, Pin Chen, Yutong Lu

Accurate prediction of electronic Hamiltonians would enable broad property inference while avoiding the high computational cost of Density Functional Theory (DFT). However, progress toward general-purpose materials foundation models is limited by a data bottleneck: existing Hamiltonian datasets are typically small, lack structural diversity, and often omit essential relativistic physics such as spin--orbit coupling (SOC). We therefore construct UniHam, a large-scale Hamiltonian dataset and benchmark suite comprising 100,000+ DFT-computed complex-valued Hermitian Hamiltonians with full SOC, covering 72 elements and a wide range of crystal geometries and symmetries (spanning diverse lattice types and space-group families). Building on UniHam, we benchmark two representative state-of-the-art models under a standardized protocol and introduce complementary evaluation metrics that jointly assess three dimensions: (i) Hamiltonian reconstruction accuracy, (ii) out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization across composition/symmetry shifts, and (iii) the ability to support downstream property prediction from the predicted Hamiltonians. Experiments on UniHam demonstrate that the proposed benchmark and metrics effectively differentiate model capabilities, revealing intrinsic SOC- and element-dependent failure modes, large variations in compositional OOD robustness, and the necessity of spectral-level evaluation to assess whether Hamiltonian predictions reliably support downstream electronic-structure properties. Overall, UniHam provides a reproducible, SOC-complete benchmark that can sharpen model comparisons and accelerate the development of next-generation foundation models for quantum materials.

Strain-dependent Dynamics of Interactions of Iodine-vacancy pairs in CsPbI3: A DFT and Machine-Learning Molecular Dynamics Study

Donggeun Kang, Seongju Lee, Yonggoon Jeon

Iodine-vacancy migration and association are important factors in the structural stability of halide perovskites. Although neighboring vacancies have been suggested to form energetically favorable pairs, their stability and reconfiguration under finite-temperature lattice deformation remain unclear. Here, we combine machine-learning molecular dynamics (MLMD) and density functional theory (DFT) to investigate neighboring iodine vacancies in tilted CsPbI3 under isotropic and biaxial strain. The MLMD trajectories reveal a nonmonotonic dependence of short-time hopping fractions on strain, with strong isotropic compression favoring point-like separation and tension promoting pair-preserving relay hopping. Notably, biaxial compression produces distinct orientation-dependent responses: vacancy pairs initially aligned with the compressed plane preferentially undergo separation, whereas those perpendicular to the plane predominantly exhibit relay hopping. Complementary static calculations using DFT and the SevenNet-0 potential yield repulsive interactions for all configurations examined. These findings highlight the importance of finite-temperature dynamics and strain-dependent local geometry in understanding vacancy-pair stability and reconfiguration in halide perovskites.

ORCHID-SAGE: Fully Reproducible CALPHAD Assessment from Evidence to Phase Diagram

Raymundo Arróyave

Knowledge of phase stability is the first requirement for materials design, and predicting the conditions under which new materials can be synthesized has become a key capability of self-driving laboratories (SDLs). For inorganic materials, notably alloys, these predictions come from thermodynamic databases built with the CALPHAD method. Building a database, however, requires expert judgment at many steps, and because these decisions are rarely recorded, others can use a database but cannot regenerate or update it. Machines can already collect the data, build a first model with machine-learned interatomic potentials and refine it against experiments. The assessment itself, the sequence of judgments that turns these parts into a reliable database, is still carried out by human experts. We argue that it can be automated reproducibly only if the expert knowledge behind an assessment is encoded in formal protocols, so that an assessment delivers a record of its construction and not only its parameters. We introduce ORCHID-SAGE, a standard that organizes this record into reproducible and automatable protocols. In this first attempt, applied to Cr-Ni, the database was regenerated from its record by a script on a second machine, by an AI agent working only through the Model Context Protocol (MCP) and by a refit from public sources only, with the same predictions. Recorded in this way, a database need no longer be fixed at publication but can be updated by AI agents as SDLs and other sources provide new evidence, while the judgment remains with people and on the record.

MOTIF: LLM-Defined Material Taxonomies for OOD Property Prediction via Invariant Feature Selection

Stable ring-polymer molecular dynamics without Fourier transforms: a linear-scaling bead-space Cayley propagator

Ring-polymer molecular dynamics (RPMD) builds zero-point energy and tunnelling into classical trajectories and is widely used for bimolecular rate coefficients. At low temperature it requires many beads, and the stiff ring-polymer springs then limit the integrator. The Cayley modification of the free ring-polymer step removes this limit, but it has so far been formulated mode by mode, which requires Fourier transforms of every degree of freedom at every step. Here we show that, in bead coordinates, the Cayley step reduces to one cyclic tridiagonal solve for the midpoint of the step, with a matrix that depends only on the number of beads $P$, the temperature and the time step, and not on the atomic masses. The bead-space step is exactly the normal-mode Cayley map: it conserves the free ring-polymer energy, preserves the free ring-polymer Boltzmann distribution, and is stable for any $P$ whenever $ΩΔt<2$ for the highest physical frequency $Ω$. A Thomas recursion with a rank-two Woodbury correction solves it in about $18P$ operations per degree of freedom without pivoting, with a condition number bounded by $1+(ω_PΔt)^2$, and the inverse matrix decays geometrically with bead separation, so the step is local in bead index. Implemented in the open-source PyRPMD package, the propagator reproduces normal-mode Cayley to $1.5\times10^{-15}$ for up to 2048 beads and its cost grows linearly with $P$. For direct RPMD (dRPMD), which has so far integrated the springs explicitly, these properties raise the stability limit on the time step 23--29-fold in published low-temperature settings; at \SI{20}{K} with 3072 beads, the Cayley step conserves the ring-polymer energy as well as velocity Verlet does with a time step about ten times larger, while leaving the low-lying internal modes that govern spurious resonances essentially unchanged.
