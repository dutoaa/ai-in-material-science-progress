UniHam: A Large-Scale SOC-Complete Dataset and Benchmark for Hamiltonian Learning in Materials

Yuewen Huang, Pin Chen, Yutong Lu

Accurate prediction of electronic Hamiltonians would enable broad property inference while avoiding the high computational cost of Density Functional Theory (DFT). However, progress toward general-purpose materials foundation models is limited by a data bottleneck: existing Hamiltonian datasets are typically small, lack structural diversity, and often omit essential relativistic physics such as spin--orbit coupling (SOC). We therefore construct UniHam, a large-scale Hamiltonian dataset and benchmark suite comprising 100,000+ DFT-computed complex-valued Hermitian Hamiltonians with full SOC, covering 72 elements and a wide range of crystal geometries and symmetries (spanning diverse lattice types and space-group families). Building on UniHam, we benchmark two representative state-of-the-art models under a standardized protocol and introduce complementary evaluation metrics that jointly assess three dimensions: (i) Hamiltonian reconstruction accuracy, (ii) out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization across composition/symmetry shifts, and (iii) the ability to support downstream property prediction from the predicted Hamiltonians. Experiments on UniHam demonstrate that the proposed benchmark and metrics effectively differentiate model capabilities, revealing intrinsic SOC- and element-dependent failure modes, large variations in compositional OOD robustness, and the necessity of spectral-level evaluation to assess whether Hamiltonian predictions reliably support downstream electronic-structure properties. Overall, UniHam provides a reproducible, SOC-complete benchmark that can sharpen model comparisons and accelerate the development of next-generation foundation models for quantum materials.

Strain-dependent Dynamics of Interactions of Iodine-vacancy pairs in CsPbI3: A DFT and Machine-Learning Molecular Dynamics Study

Donggeun Kang, Seongju Lee, Yonggoon Jeon

Iodine-vacancy migration and association are important factors in the structural stability of halide perovskites. Although neighboring vacancies have been suggested to form energetically favorable pairs, their stability and reconfiguration under finite-temperature lattice deformation remain unclear. Here, we combine machine-learning molecular dynamics (MLMD) and density functional theory (DFT) to investigate neighboring iodine vacancies in tilted CsPbI3 under isotropic and biaxial strain. The MLMD trajectories reveal a nonmonotonic dependence of short-time hopping fractions on strain, with strong isotropic compression favoring point-like separation and tension promoting pair-preserving relay hopping. Notably, biaxial compression produces distinct orientation-dependent responses: vacancy pairs initially aligned with the compressed plane preferentially undergo separation, whereas those perpendicular to the plane predominantly exhibit relay hopping. Complementary static calculations using DFT and the SevenNet-0 potential yield repulsive interactions for all configurations examined. These findings highlight the importance of finite-temperature dynamics and strain-dependent local geometry in understanding vacancy-pair stability and reconfiguration in halide perovskites.

Xuyu Dong, Yixuan Li, Jianguo Huang, Feng Chen, Yiyun Zhou, Bo An

Large language models and the multi-agent systems built around them could accelerate materials discovery by connecting scientific knowledge with experimental decisions. Existing benchmarks establish capabilities in domain knowledge, multimodal interpretation and scientific tool use. As language models increasingly support materials research workflows, their ability to distinguish closely related material states and reason accurately with quantitative evidence remains insufficiently evaluated. These abilities are essential for comparing material responses, assessing mechanisms and maintaining reliable judgements as experimental evidence accumulates. We introduce MatScope to evaluate three connected capabilities. M1 tests unaided, condition-specific reasoning through 200 tasks spanning ten research functions, using aluminium and high-entropy alloys as representative material systems. Fine-grained response estimates, comparisons between similar states and quantitatively supported choices remain substantially weaker. Related literature improves selected estimates, while condition and baseline mismatches limit transfer. Progressive observations calibrate some downstream responses and destabilize earlier accurate predictions. MatScope establishes measurable targets for developing reliable scientific reasoning in future self-evolving materials laboratories and materials-aware world models.

MOTIF: LLM-Defined Material Taxonomies for OOD Property Prediction via Invariant Feature Selection

Gwangseon Jang, Sieun Yun, Dong Ho Lee, Kyong-Ha Lee, Chanuk Lim, Jin Woo Choi

Out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization remains a central challenge in materials property prediction, where models must extrapolate to novel chemistries beyond the training distribution. While large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong scientific reasoning, their direct use for quantitative prediction remains limited. To address this, we systematically evaluate multiple roles for LLM assistance, finding that only taxonomy-based grouping consistently improves OOD generalization over a strong kNN+GBT baseline. Building on this, we here propose MOTIF, a framework that leverages LLMs not as predictors, but as generators of candidate taxonomies that partition materials into chemically meaningful environments. These taxonomies are evaluated via training-only pseudo-OOD validation, and only those that improve generalization are retained. Invariant feature selection is then applied within each accepted taxonomy, followed by gated aggregation across taxonomies. We evaluate on 2D halide perovskites, where bandgap prediction must generalize across distinct organic spacer families. On this benchmark, MOTIF reduces mean OOD MAE from 0.122 to 0.111 eV. Under a frozen dev/test protocol, the deployable configuration further improves errors from 0.123 to 0.117 eV (electronic bandgap) and from 0.181 to 0.173 eV (optical bandgap). Beyond this primary task, we validate transferability across additional materials datasets, including 3D perovskite solar cell efficiencies and Matbench benchmarks with structure-based splits. Across domains, MOTIF consistently improves robustness under distribution shift, outperforming expert-defined groupings, rule-based heuristics, and clustering baselines, while remaining competitive with strong classical selection methods. These results demonstrate that LLMs are most effective not as predictors, but as tools for discovering structure in data.

Our results motivate developing TFMs further as surrogates for active learning in materials discovery.

At ambient pressure, we systematically vary the electron correlation, i.e., on-site Coulomb interaction to study its effect on band gap and structural parameters. Increasing the value of $U$ enhances the band gap of all three compositions while producing only a slight variation in structural parameters. The effect of hydrostatic pressure is also studied for the same compositions. The estimated band gap values at ambient pressure closes at approximately 39, 42, and 53 GPa for $x$ = 0 (for $U_\mathrm{Co}$ = 5 eV and $U_\mathrm{Ir}$ = 2 eV), 0.5 (for $U_\mathrm{Co}$ = 5 eV and $U_\mathrm{Ir}$ = 2 eV), and 1 (for $U_\mathrm{Co}$ = 4 eV), respectively, indicating pressure-induced insulator-to-metal transitions. These results show that electron correlation stabilises the insulating state, whereas hydrostatic pressure favours the metallic state by closing the band gap. These findings highlight the tunability of correlated oxides through electron correlations and pressure, offering insights for materials design.

Quantum Embedding from Molecular to Crystalline Environments at the Cost of a Gas-Phase Calculation

Manas Sharma, Marek Sierka

We present a direct-space formulation and implementation of quantum density functional theory (DFT) embedding for molecular active subsystems embedded in frozen environments (hosts) of arbitrary dimensionality, from finite molecular hosts to extended systems. The host is computed once without the embedded molecule (the guest) and is represented only by its electron density fitted in an atom-centered auxiliary Gaussian basis together with its nuclear charges with the periodic electrostatics separated into an explicit near-field and a continuous fast-multipole far-field operator. The method allows the removal of host atoms from this representation to create vacancies or other voids without requiring a supercell creation. Analytic nuclear gradients are also implemented, enabling structural relaxation of the active subsystem. We demonstrate the versatility of the approach through applications to adsorbates on periodic surfaces and molecules embedded in bulk solvent environments. For CO on MgO(001), we show that DFT-in-DFT converges rapidly and reproduces the periodic interaction energy within 0.44 kJ/mol while reducing the cost by a factor of 27. Real-time time-dependent embedded DFT (RT-TDDFET) of methylenecyclopropene in periodic water is showcased to produce a ~0.6 eV solvatochromic blue shift, while strong-field propagation of CO2 on hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) yields substrate-induced effects at the cost of a gas-phase propagation. Coupling the embedding scheme to excited-state correlated wave function theory methods such as CC2 yields a solvent shift within 0.01 eV of supermolecular CC2 references, while reducing the computational cost by over 500 times.

Defects and Structural Evolution in CrSBr: Machine-Learning Potentials for Atomistic Simulations

Arsalan Hashemi, Daniil Kruklinskii, Xiaoya Chang, Tapio Ala-Nissila, Mikko Karttunen, Arkady V. Krasheninnikov

The layered van der Waals semiconductor CrSBr combines intrinsic magnetic order with semiconducting and optical properties, making it a promising material for multifunctional electronic and photonic applications. Understanding how defects and structural transformations affect its properties under experimentally relevant conditions, however, remains challenging for first-principles simulations due to the relevant length and time scales. Here, we develop and benchmark machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) for the Cr-S-Br system, enabling large-scale atomistic simulations of CrSBr with accuracy approaching that of density functional theory (DFT). The charge-aware variant qNEP achieves near-DFT accuracy in predicting lattice constants, formation enthalpies, vacancy energetics, and the anisotropic pressure response of o-CrSBr. Nudged elastic band calculations reveal that pre-existing S vacancies lower the Cr Frenkel-pair migration barrier by up to 1.1 eV, providing an atomistic mechanism for beam-induced Cr redistribution and structural transformations. Displacement threshold calculations further show that Br is the most susceptible species to electron-beam-induced displacement, identifying the relative ease of removing different atomic species. Together, these results establish a validated computational framework for investigating atomic-scale disorder and structural transformations in CrSBr, providing a basis for defect engineering in materials for future magneto-optoelectronic applications.

High Pressure Material Phase Identification with Gaussian Kernel Neural Networks

Cameron Ryan, Yasantha Pasindu Hetti Kankanamalage, Shuai Zhang

Phase identification is a computational task involving the classification of material structure using the positions of atoms. Using data from Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations, our task is to identify the material phase of a set of atoms, based on how the simulations predict their positioning relative to each other. By leveraging existing research on mechanistic neural network theory, particularly Neural Tangent Kernels (NTK), we develop a new and efficient method for classifying simulated material samples. Our method involves defining a set of "atom detectors" that are given positions around the origin in cartesian coordinates. The sum of Gaussians centered at each atom is calculated at each atom detector, and is used as the input to a neural network. We present theoretical and experimental evidence to show that this method is highly effective and efficient at the task. Our method obtains better or similar accuracy scores to commonly used methods while having a major computational speedup.

Learning Electronic Band Structures Across the s, p, d Blocks with an Empirical Pseudopotential Network

Qiang Zhu

Electronic band structures govern how crystals conduct electrons and absorb light. Density-functional theory (DFT) computes them through costly self-consistent iterations, while existing machine-learning models either predict isolated scalar properties or output Hamiltonians that still require DFT solvers. Here we present EpNet, a learned empirical pseudopotential for crystals spanning 63 elements across the \textit{s}, \textit{p} and \textit{d} blocks. An equivariant neural network builds its Hamiltonian from physics-motivated local potentials and nonlocal projectors. Trained solely on band energies, EpNet predicts complete band structures directly from atomic coordinates. On unseen chemical systems, its median errors are \winAll\ eV for band energies and \gapAll\ eV for band gaps. On 600,000 structures from an independent database, its band-gap errors are overall less than half those of existing models, at about 1\% of DFT cost. EpNet thus enables fast screening of electronic properties for materials discovery and electronic-structure simulations of large systems.

Makoto Takamoto, Chaitanya Sampara, Viktor Zaverkin, Daniel Oñoro-Rubio, Mathias Niepert

It is well established that molecular substructures can contribute unequally to chemical and physical properties, with specific motifs or side chains often playing dominant roles. Fragment-based representations have therefore emerged as an effective strategy for incorporating chemically meaningful inductive biases, as exemplified by molecular fingerprints and fragment-based attention mechanisms. However, many existing fragmentation-based machine learning methods rely on predefined chemical rules, which may yield suboptimal fragmentation for previously unseen or chemically unfamiliar structures and often overlook the importance of multiscale molecular representations. To address these limitations, we formulate molecular fragmentation as a radius-constrained optimization problem inspired by the k-center formulation, where the number of clusters is not fixed a priori but emerges from the specified radius, and propose a geometry-based fragmentation method that partitions arbitrary molecular or material structures using largely geometric information, without relying on predefined chemical fragmentation rules. The method provides flexible control over fragment size, enabling the construction of multiscale fragment representations for scale-adaptive modeling. Building on this framework, we develop a geometric fragment-based attention mechanism that integrates multiscale structural information and incorporate it into a multimodal machine learning model combining p-SMILES representations with three-dimensional (3D) conformational information. Experiments on a benchmark polymer dataset show that incorporating 3D conformational information improves prediction accuracy for several properties over models based exclusively on p-SMILES representations. Explainable artificial intelligence analyses further provide insights into the physical relevance of the learned representations.

Inverse design of superconductors and semiconductors with differentiable foundation models

Cristóbal Méndez, Justin Tahmassebpur, Tomás A. Arias

Designing a crystal for a target property requires choosing its elements, stoichiometry, and atomic structure together, yet gradients exist only for the continuous structural variables. We introduce differentiable Equiformer fast effective-atom theory (DEFEAT), which lets a pretrained EquiformerV3 foundation model accept continuous species weights and site occupancies without retraining on fractional data, thereby enabling gradient-based materials optimization. Gradients from fine-tuned property models and a machine-learned energy penalty then guide updates to composition, atom count, atomic positions, and lattice vectors in a single optimization. In a search for phonon-mediated superconductors, density-functional perturbation theory on 45 structurally novel designs gives isotropic Allen-Dynes critical temperatures above 10 K for 39; the highest is 24.0 K for ZrTiNb2CN3, and H4LiPd3 (23.2 K) lies on the static DFT hull. In searches for photovoltaic absorbers, SCAN calculations confirm ten novel crystal structures with band gaps within 20% of a 1.34 eV target on their respective raw or empirically corrected scales, all within 70 meV/atom of the DFT hull, and show that the systematic gap error of the training functional propagates into the designs unless the objective corrects for it. Quasiharmonic free energies from machine-learned potentials further show that finite-temperature competition with solid phases can reorder candidates relative to the static hull.

Strain Controlled Hydrogen Interstitial Site Preference and Hydride Phase Suppression in Epitaxial Vanadium

Xin Xiao

Understanding hydrogen behavior in transition metals is crucial for energy storage and sensing technologies. Here we combine density functional theory (DFT) calculations with in-situ electrical measurements to reveal how epitaxial strain governs hydrogen interstitial site selection and phase stability in vanadium. Our calculations demonstrate a robust thermodynamic preference for tetrahedral (T) over octahedral (O) sites, with the energy difference modulated by lattice anisotropy through strain-tunable H 1s-V 3d orbital hybridization. Experimentally, epitaxial V films on MgO(001) exhibit a critical thickness-dependent crossover: 50 nm films show characteristic first-order alpha-beta hydride transitions, while 10 nm films display continuous solid-solution behavior with complete transition suppression. This crossover is governed by the progressive relaxation of epitaxial strain with increasing film thickness: the 10 nm films retain strong coherent strain that flattens the free-energy landscape and suppresses hydride nucleation, whereas the 50 nm films approach the strain-relaxed bulk limit where long-range elastic interactions restore first-order phase behavior. Finite-size truncation of elastic fields and interfacial electronic reconstruction further reinforce this dimensional crossover. Our work establishes a unified framework connecting atomic-scale orbital physics to mesoscopic phase thermodynamics, providing design principles for strain-engineered metal-hydride systems.

DFT-Agent Bench: Scaffold Effects and Execution Provenance in Autonomous DFT

Shijie Liu, Jin Yang, Weilin Wan, Jingtao Han, Jia LI, Tianqi Deng

Large language models are increasingly deployed as autonomous agents for first-principles materials simulation. We report empirical findings from DFT-Agent Bench, a benchmark of 200+ Quantum ESPRESSO tasks spanning relaxation, electronic structure, equation-of-state, and elastic-tensor pipelines over multiple material tiers. Each task carries a QE-derived ground truth with public-reference cross-checks where available, and a provenance audit checks whether the required QE execution contract was met. Under one execution harness, we compare a near-bare ReAct baseline with a bundled v5.1 scaffold on two models. The complete scaffold improves bands+DOS by +0.08/+0.11 and d-electron vc-relax by +0.17 on both models, while high-scoring relaxation and equation-of-state cells leave little room for improvement. The elastic result is model-dependent (0.18 to 0.40 for deepseek-v4-pro and 0.62 to 0.59 for claude-opus-4-8). A fixed 100-task analysis with retrieval disabled supports the aggregate pattern: the fully frozen DeepSeek comparison gains +0.17, while the Opus comparison gains +0.10 against a historical pool baseline (p < 0.001). The provenance gate catches incomplete or unmarked executions that can receive high rubric scores; it is an execution-contract check, not a test for intentional fraud. These results characterize the evaluated DFT workflows. The anonymous review artifact contains the frozen diagnostic slice, prompts, scorer, and solver revisions: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/231e31re91287h/README.md.

X-ray parametric down-conversion probes radiation-induced dynamics in hybrid perovskite MAPbBr3

S. Sofer, O. Sefi, G. J. Man, H. N. Barad, N. P. Jasti, D. Cahen

We report minute-timescale time-resolved measurements of X-ray parametric down-conversion (PDC) of X-rays into ultraviolet photons in a MAPbBr3 single crystal under continuous irradiation. Despite the known radiation sensitivity of hybrid perovskites and the high photon flux required to detect the intrinsically weak PDC signal, the nonlinear response remains measurable throughout the irradiation sequence. Both PDC and Bragg diffraction exhibit a slow decay consistent with diffusion-driven damage dynamics on timescales reported for bromide-vacancy and proton migration. However, despite their similar decay rates, the PDC and Bragg signals evolve toward markedly different steady states, while an additional fast decay component is observed in the Bragg response but absent in the PDC signal. Density functional theory calculations identify N 2s states of the methylammonium cation as the most likely contributors to the measured PDC response and show that their Fourier component is only weakly affected by bromide-vacancy formation. These results establish nonlinear X-ray diffraction as a channel-selective probe of radiation-induced dynamics and demonstrate that radiation damage can couple differently to nonlinear and linear X-ray responses.

Hydrogen Thermodiffusion in Tungsten: Trapped Hydrogen Distorts Heat of Transport Estimation from Total Concentration Profiles

Ville Jantunen, Daniel R. Mason, Sanjeet Kaur

We investigate hydrogen diffusion in tungsten under thermal gradients in the presence of trapping sites using multiscale simulations. Using molecular dynamics simulations, we find that the heat of transport, which quantifies the coupling between temperature gradients and hydrogen transport, for hydrogen in tungsten is positive (thermophobic) at lower temperatures but changes sign near $1000$ K. We investigate the origin of the heat of transport and find that harmonic transition state theory with elastic thermal-expansion corrections is insufficient to explain the observed behaviour, consistent with either a breakdown of local thermodynamic equilibrium or non-negligible anharmonic contributions. We further extend previous studies of hydrogen thermodiffusion by including the effect of trapping sites in both simulations and analytical models. We show that when trapped hydrogen accounts for a significant fraction of the total hydrogen concentration, the heat of transport inferred from measurements of the total concentration is dominated by the temperature dependence of the trapped hydrogen population and no longer reflects the gradient in the mobile hydrogen concentration. Consequently, experimentally measured heats of transport cannot in general be used directly in continuum-scale models that explicitly account for trapping and detrapping. In such models, the heat of transport should be based on mobile hydrogen concentrations only to avoid incorporating trapping effects twice.

Discovery of Kondo-Kramers Nodal Line Topological State in strongly correlated material CeAuAl$_3$

Hari Bhandari, Kevin Allen, Kuan-Sen Li, Yuan Fang, Arushi, Karthik Rao

Kramers nodal lines (KNLs) are predicted in all achiral, non-centrosymmetric crystal structures, and yet there are very few experimental realizations of KNLs, only in weakly correlated systems. Especially, their realization in strongly correlated systems remains unexplored. Here we investigate KNL topology in the non-centrosymmetric, achiral Kondo lattice CeAuAl$_3$, using magnetotransport, thermodynamic measurements, density functional theory (DFT), and tight-binding toy model calculations. We find that CeAuAl$_3$ hosts KNLs in the $\mathitΓ\text{-}Z$ and $X\text{-}P$ directions, protected by time-reversal and mirror symmetries. Breaking of these crystalline symmetries, along with time-reversal symmetry via spin polarization, lifts the KNL degeneracy, which, in turn, generates Berry curvature resulting in an intrinsic anomalous Hall effect (AHE). More importantly, CeAuAl$_3$ shows large AHE not only below the magnetic ordering temperature $T_N$ but rather below the Kondo temperature. This underscores the role of the Kondo correlations in bringing the KNLs at the Fermi surface rather than at accidental energies, as would be expected without correlations. Our work identifies a new topological state, Kondo-Kramers Nodal Line, with CeAuAl$_3$ being the first compound explicitly identified to host this new correlated topological state, and demonstrating the expected AHE associated with it. More generally, our work outlines the pathway for finding Kondo-KNL systems in all compounds that combine achiral, non-centrosymmetric structure with Kondo correlations.

Quantum benchmarks for magnetic energies and transition temperatures

Mojtaba Alaei, Ilia Mikhailov, Alireza Qaiumzadeh, Artem Oganov

Predicting magnetic ordering temperatures requires accurate exchange interactions and an appropriate treatment of spin thermodynamics. Here we assess these requirements through complementary quantum Monte Carlo benchmarks. For five insulating antiferromagnets, we compare ferromagnetic-antiferromagnetic energy differences from diffusion Monte Carlo (DMC) and density functional theory with references reconstructed from inelastic neutron scattering (INS) exchanges. DMC underestimates these references but achieves a mean absolute error comparable to the best-performing functionals tested. Separately, we directly test the previously proposed (S+1)/S rescaling of classical Monte Carlo temperatures against quantum stochastic series expansion simulations for 17 compounds using corresponding INS-derived Heisenberg Hamiltonians. Rescaling reduces the mean absolute classical-quantum temperature difference from 81.7 to 8.2 K, with a mean absolute percentage difference of 5.9 %. These results establish a direct quantum benchmark for the rescaling within the tested models and emphasize that improvements in electronic magnetic energy scales should be accompanied by attention to quantum spin thermodynamics.

Dopant Cluster Engineering for Tunable Room-Temperature Ferromagnetism in 2D Transition Metal Dichalcogenides

Shivangee, Sachin Grewal, Akash Singh

Two-dimensional transition metal dichalcogenides (2D-TMDs) offer a promising platform for realizing dilute magnetic semiconductors. However, simultaneously achieving large magnetic anisotropy energy (MAE) and high Curie temperature (T$_\text{c}$) remains a major challenge. In this work, we employ DFT+U to systematically investigate the thermodynamic stability and magnetic properties of 2D-MoSe$_2$ and 2D-WSe$_2$ doped with transition metal atoms. We found that the paired dopant configurations are more stable than previously attributed isolated dopants. Despite their nearly identical crystal structures, 2D-MoSe$_2$ and 2D-WSe$_2$ show contrasting properties upon doping. All paired dopants in 2D-MoSe$_2$ exhibit long-range ferromagnetic coupling (FMC) primarily due to carrier-mediated exchange interactions, whereas those in 2D-WSe$_2$ show weak exchange coupling. Interestingly, 2Mn$_\text{2W}$ shows a transition from less stable antiferromagnetic coupling (AFMC) in the short range to highly stable FMC in the long-range. The combined effect of spin-orbit coupling, exchange interactions, and orbital hybridization gives rise to a large MAE with maximum in-plane values of $-$3.5 meV for 2V$_\text{2Mo}$ and $-$3.2 meV for 2Mn$_\text{2W}$. Monte Carlo simulations estimate T$_\text{c}$ $\simeq$ 440 K for 2Fe$_\text{2Mo}$ and ensure its high-temperature stability in 2D-MoSe$_2$. Our results establish dopant clustering as an effective route to engineer high-temperature ferromagnetism in 2D-TMDs and highlight their potential for nano-spintronic applications.
