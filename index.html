Validating Graph Neural Network Feature Attribution Through Local Perturbations: A Study on CoMoFeNiCu Decomposition Enthalpy

Miguel Tenorio, James Chapman

Graph neural networks (GNNs) show promise for predicting materials properties, but interpretability remains a challenge, particularly understanding whether attributed feature importance reflects physical reality or statistical artifacts. Here, we present a methods validation study that deliberately overfits a physics-aware GNN ensemble to probe the robustness of feature-ranking approaches. Using the CoMoFeNiCu high-entropy alloy system with three stoichiometries, we train GNNs to predict decomposition enthalpies from DFT-calculated structures while explicitly constraining output weights to encode feature importance. By design, our dataset is limited (approximately 900 binary and 4,500 ternary decomposition pathways) to control compositional complexity and enable precise interrogation of local chemistry effects. We validate that feature rankings capture meaningful local-environment information through a series of atomic swap perturbations: perturbing individual atomic sites and re-ranking features reveals spatial decay consistent with the GNN's interaction cutoff, confirming that weights encode neighbor-shell effects rather than global stoichiometric artifacts alone. Results demonstrate that feature attribution methods can remain physically interpretable even under overfitting, provided validation is rigorous. This work establishes a foundation for trustworthy interpretability in materials GNNs and sets the stage for scaling to multiple chemistries and testing generalization across compositional spaces.

Materials research with a general-purpose agent

Paulo R. Pires, Théo Cavignac, Tiago F. T. Cerqueira, Hai-Chen Wang, Silvana Botti, Miguel A. L. Marques

Over the last decade we have built Alexandria, an open database of about 15 million density-functional calculations of crystals. It includes PBE, PBEsol, SCAN and TB-mBJ results, structures relaxed at pressures up to 150 GPa, dielectric tensors and about 145,000 electron-phonon calculations, together with more than 400 million structures relaxed with machine-learned potentials. On these data we trained a generative model for crystal structures, a universal interatomic potential and graph neural networks for more than ten properties, from band gaps to superconducting critical temperatures. From the convex hull we computed a reaction graph of the 17.4 billion reactions between its 193,416 stable compounds. Here we give all of this to a general-purpose language-model agent. Working unattended, the agent screens the database for a solar absorber, drops a candidate whose synthesis releases too little energy, and writes a synthesis plan with precursors, crucible and atmosphere. Asked about the well-studied Ba-Zr-S system, it finds an unreported sulfide and designs the experiment that would confirm or rule it out. Asked for an air-stable alternative, it designs one and tests it against new structures from the generative model. With web search alone, the same agent proposes what the literature already discusses. Put together, the data, the models and the reaction graph let a general agent do what none of them does alone: go from a question in plain words to an experiment that a laboratory can run and test.

Crystal Structure Prediction from Search to Learning: A 30-Year Expedition

Jianjun Hu, Ying Feng

Crystal structure prediction (CSP) determines periodic atomic or molecular arrangements from chemical composition and physical conditions. Over three decades, CSP has evolved from chemical construction and global energy optimization to hybrid approaches combining symmetry, learned potentials, adaptive search, and deep generative models. This survey reviews method, software, review, and benchmark records from 1996 to October 2026, covering inorganic and molecular crystals, including formula-conditioned diffusion, flow, and autoregressive models that preserve composition. We classify methods by candidate construction and exploration: template and discrete construction, random search, evolutionary search, swarm and differential evolution, energy-landscape exploration, adaptive machine-learning-guided search, and learned generation. We distinguish representation, relaxation, ranking, and physical qualification. Benchmarks reveal no universally dominant approach. In a 46-target leaderboard snapshot, the best-performing template method achieves higher top-1 recovery than leading generative models, but paired tests show no statistically significant differences from several generative competitors. We also audit predictions for 68 experimentally examined materials, including 52 with structures confirmed by diffraction or microscopy. Predictions for 23 of the 68 preceded experiment, mostly through first-principles global search. Within the reviewed evidence, we find no independently confirmed discovery in which a deep generative model predicted the structure before experiment. We conclude with the principal limitations of current CSP algorithms and priorities for future research.

A Machine-learning-aided Workflow for Sampling Small Polaron Dynamics in Energy Materials

Tavinder Singh, Harald Oberhofer

One of the defining characteristics of most energy materials, used as e.g.~battery electrodes, photovoltaics, or photocatalysts, is their ability conduct charges such as electrons and holes. The exact mechanism of this conductivity depends on the material, but is often dominated by the existence of mobile small polarons, i.e.~electrons or holes strongly localised through the polarisation of their environment. Computationally, small polarons can be treated with reasonably accuracy through the use of self-interaction corrected or hybrid density functional theory (DFT) together with local geometry optimisation. Similar approaches can be employed to determine the energy barriers to movement of the polarons through hopping. Unfortunately, realistic energy materials tend have very large numbers of symmetry inequivalent polaronic configurations and correspondingly large numbers of hopping barriers, making them hard to tackle explicitly. In this work we therefore extend the computational reach of DFT calculations with machine-learned (ML) surrogate models, which then feed into a kinetic model to quantitatively estimate the conductivity of a battery anode material (Li$_4$Ti$_5$O$_12$ or LTO) at realistic, defected conditions. We calculate the conductivity at room temperature to be 6.7 $\times$ 10$^{-3}$ S/cm which lies well in the experimental range. To this end, we only needed a total of 154 polaron barrier calculations, out of 3649 symmetry inequivalent ones, achieving an over 20-fold increase of computational efficiency. Overall, our work demonstrates how DFT-trained ML surrogates can be used to quantitatively and efficiently sample the dynamics of small polarons in energy materials.

Zhennan Zhang, Shaobo Yu, Haoting Zhang, Yu Han, Zhiyue Guo, Junjie Wang

Autoregressive language models offer a unified route to crystal generation and inverse design, but their performance depends on how periodic structures are serialized. Symmetry equivalent and closely related crystals can receive different atomic orders under coordinate-based serialization, introducing variability unrelated to their structural connectivity. Here, we present CGTransformer (Crystal Graph Transformer), an autoregressive generative framework that addresses this challenge via a graph-embedded sorting strategy. Using quotientgraph topology and barycentric embeddings, Element Symmetry Graph (ESG) converts periodic crystal networks into topology-guided one-dimensional sequences that retain key local and symmetry-related structural information. CGTransformer achieves high generation validity and the highest single shot crystal structure prediction (CSP) match rate among the compared MP-20 baselines (63.24%), while same backbone comparisons show improved structure recovery over coordinate-based ordering. Furthermore, we demonstrate property-conditioned inverse design for wide-bandgap semiconductors, where ESG achieves a much higher stable, unique and novel (S.U.N.) rate and stronger target-property alignment than the coordinate-based baseline. These results establish topology-guided sequence construction as an effective route to improving crystal generation and property-directed design at fixed model capacity.

Enrichment-Oriented Virtual Screening of Perovskite Solar Cells through Reinforcement Learning

Yezeyuan Zheng, Ziyang Hu, Maojun Sun. Ruijian Han, Jian Huang, Guanhua Chen, Jie Liu

Experimental optimization of perovskite solar cells is constrained by combinatorial diversity in absorber compositions, device layers, and processing, which makes virtual screening attractive for prioritizing recipes before fabrication. We develop a surrogate-guided reinforcement-learning framework that casts recipe generation as virtual screening, enriching candidate shortlists near high-performing experimental neighbors. A frozen Tabular Prior-data Fitted Network (TabPFN) guides recipe generation, while a reference pool of 890 measured devices enables neighborhood-based assessment. Five-fold evaluation on 1,846 devices shows top-ten selection raises mean power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 19.68\% to 23.74\%. Three learned policies are compared with six baselines under 3,000 surrogate evaluations per run. In top-100 shortlists, Proximal policy optimization (PPO) and recurrent PPO (RPPO) achieve neighborhood-supported hit rates of 23\% and 24\% (7.9- and 8.3-fold Pure Random's 2.9\%), versus 10\% (3.4-fold) for the baselines with the highest supported hit rate (L-SHADE and Ax BO, tied) at $K = 100$. Gains coexist with weak-support fractions. Of 9,000 recipes, 65.4\% match a composition family at similarity 0.80, falling to 11.7\% at 0.90. Results support enrichment-oriented prioritization but not experimental performance, seed robustness, or matched-budget advantage.

Brute-force molecular dynamics simulation of aging in amorphous Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$

Elisa Petroni, Dario Baratella, Omar Abou El Kheir, Andrea Redaelli, Marco Bernasconi

Chalcogenide alloys such as the prototypical Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$ compound are used in phase change electronic memories due to their ability to undergo a fast and reversible transition between the amorphous and crystalline phases upon heating. The amorphous phase is subject to aging that leads to an increase in the electrical resistance, which is detrimental to the operation of the devices. Using a machine learning interatomic potential, we have investigated the structural relaxations responsible for aging in amorphous Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$ in molecular dynamics simulations at temperatures slightly below the glass transition. The aging simulation, lasting about 100 ns, follows an equilibration of 24 ns at 400 K. During aging we observed an enhancement of the Peierls distortion in axial bonds which is a typical feature of these alloys that we relate to the widening of the electronic band gap computed within Density Functional Theory in smaller aged amorphous models. We also observed a marked reduction of the atomic mobility with an increase of the bond lifetimes during aging. However, we do not see a reduction in homopolar bonds, which are expected to be absent in the ideal aged glass and which should be removed on a much longer time scale.

SiC-BN composites with extreme thermal and environmental stability

Abhijit Biswas, Eugenio Garcia-Granados, Saman Zare, Mozhdeh Mirakhory, JungHun Park, Shuvodeep De

Ceramic materials that maintain structural integrity and resist thermal-shock under extreme conditions are critical for many applications. Here we show that spark plasma sintered composites containing equal weight fractions of hexagonal silicon carbide (6H-SiC) and boron nitride (h-BN) exhibit exceptional stability under extreme high-temperature oxidizing conditions and unusually stable temperature-independent thermal conductivity across a broad temperature range. Under high-velocity oxygen-fuel torch exposure in air, it withstands prolonged heating and thermal-cycling at surface temperatures reaching >1500 °C, whereas pure 6H-SiC and h-BN rapidly fractures or erodes. This lightweight, easily machinable ceramic composite will be a promising structural material in extreme environment applications.

Quantum Material Generation via Coherent Hamiltonian Search

Jiang Yuhan, Matthew Otten

Discovering new materials with targeted properties is a central goal of materials science, yet the space of candidates is vast. Data-driven generative models learn the distribution of known materials, but they extrapolate poorly, and a generated structure is not known to be stable. A material's properties follow from its Hamiltonian, and a quantum computer can calculate them from it. We introduce the quantum material generative algorithm (QMage), a coherent search, guided by the Hamiltonian, over crystalline configurations of atomic composition, unit cell structure, and atomic positions, for one whose calculated properties meet the targets to within a given error tolerance. The computation is a single coherent quantum circuit in which each configuration's Hamiltonian is block-encoded, its target eigenstate isolated and properties evaluated, and the satisfying configurations amplified by fixed-point search and measured. One framework covers many targets, from formation energy and band gaps to elasticity and conductivity. We design the algorithm for the mature fault-tolerant era and estimate its cost in logical qubits and Toffoli depth from a classically routine benchmark to targets where established methods are unreliable. The approach generates candidate materials from first principles, rather than generalizing from those known, and could enable materials discovery when the target properties are difficult to predict classically.

Graph diffusion transformer for interpretable generation and inverse design of disordered atomic structures

Jiani Hu, Chenyan Wang, Yongkun Yang, Guanyu Hou, Jihaochen Zhang, Lei Liu

Atomic disorder offers a potent degree of freedom to tailor material properties, yet the resulting vast configuration space obscures structure-property relations and hinders rational inverse design. Here we introduce Graph Amorphous Diffusion Transformer (GaDiT), a generative model that combines dynamic periodic graphs, masked conditioning and an interval-weighted training-noise distribution. GaDiT generates high-quality disordered structures while accelerating sampling by two orders of magnitude compared with existing corrector-based approaches, as demonstrated across two-dimensional amorphous carbon and three-dimensional amorphous silicon and silica. Moreover, GaDiT supports conditional generation under single or combined structural features, physical properties and preparation conditions in a unified model, and enables inverse design that expands accessible structure-property ranges beyond the training distribution. To interpret these structures and the trained network, we propose a conditioned atomic response mapping analysis, revealing both the global structural descriptors and the local atomic environments that govern target properties. Overall, GaDiT establishes a general framework for interpretable generation, inverse design and structure-property discovery for disordered atomic structures.

Pressure dependent DFT study of mechanical, thermal, electronic, magnetic, and optical properties of EuCd2As2: a magnetic semimetal

Salauddin Muhammad Anis, M. A. Afzal, R. S. Islam, S. H. Naqib

We present a comprehensive spin-polarized density functional theory (DFT) investigation of the pressure-dependent physical properties of EuCd2As2, a magnetically and topologically rich compound with potential applications in spintronics and optoelectronics technologies. We have investigated the structural, mechanical, thermophysical, acoustic, electronic, magnetic, and optical properties of this material at pressures of 0, 2, 4, 6, and 8 GPa. The calculated lattice parameters at zero pressure are in good agreement with experimental values. The mechanical stability of EuCd2As2 is maintained over the investigated pressure range. The elastic constants, bulk modulus, shear modulus, and Young's modulus are found to be tunable with applied pressure. The compound undergoes a brittle to ductile transition at higher pressures. The Debye temperature, melting temperature, and Grüneisen parameter are found to be strongly pressure-dependent. Phonon thermal conductivity has been evaluated. The electronic properties of EuCd2As2 were studied through band structure and density of states (DOS). The effect of pressure up to 8 GPa was investigated for the ferromagnetic (FM) phase. At 0 GPa, the calculated FM band structure exhibits no band gap at the Fermi level, which confirms the semimetallic nature of EuCd2As2. The net magnetic moment of 6.96 μ_B per unit cell was obtained for this compound indicating its potential for spintronics application. The optical properties, including the dielectric function, refractive index, absorption coefficient, optical conductivity, reflectivity, and loss function, are calculated. The calculated optical spectra indicate strong absorption in the visible and ultraviolet regions, suggesting that EuCd2As2 may have potential for optoelectronic applications.

Machine-learning charge density for electronic materials and devices simulation

Qingyun Zhang, Yang Hu, Aixia Zhang, Zehua Sun, Jiawei Yan, Xiaohao Zhou

We present a machine-learning (ML) charge density model, DeltaChargeNet, integrated with the full-potential tight-binding muffin-tin orbital (FP-TB-MTO) method for electronic structure and quantum transport simulations of materials and devices. The ML model predicts electron density from atomic structures with low linear-scaling cost, while FP-TB-MTO provides an accurate tight-binding representation of the system Hamiltonian. To achieve high-fidelity ML density for complex devices with interfaces and disorder, we train an equivariant graph convolutional network to predict $Δρ$-the difference between DFT and atomic superposition densities-under a charge neutrality constraint required for inhomogeneous systems. A secondary ML residual charge model compensates for errors in the primary $Δρ$ model. The predicted density is then used within the full-relativistic FP-TB-MTO framework combined with the non-equilibrium Green's function method. We validate our method on bulk HgCdTe disordered alloys (including vacancy defects) and Au/HgCdTe/Au disordered devices. The maximum band energy error is below 10 meV for bulk HgCdTe. For devices, the charge neutrality constraint suppresses long-wavelength charge bias that would otherwise distort the electrostatic potential by 0.68-1.88 eV near the device center, while residual compensation corrects the remaining 0.16-0.46 eV potential mismatch, essential for reproducing the correct potential barrier profile. Together, these corrections reproduce the layer-resolved potential, including interface steps and bulk-like plateaus, within 0.1 eV of fully self-consistent DFT. Finally, we demonstrate quantum transport in a 11220-atom infrared detector. By combining first-principles accuracy with linear-scaling inference, DeltaChargeNet enables large-scale device simulations without the full DFT self-consistent field loop.

Diffusion Tree Sampling for Inference Time Adaptation of Crystal Generative Models

Daniel T. Levy, Vineet Jain, Tara Akhound-Sadegh, Sékou-Oumar Kaba, Siamak Ravanbakhsh

Diffusion-based generative models have become strong priors over plausible crystal structures, but turning that prior into promising candidates requires steering toward several conflicting properties at once. Fine-tuning is expensive and produces a new model for every new objective, while differentiable guidance is not applicable to many real-world tasks. We instead adapt these generative models at inference time using Diffusion Tree Sampling (DTS), a Monte Carlo tree search procedure over the denoising trajectory. We make two technical contributions on top of DTS that are particularly relevant for AI-for-science applications. First, we show how to use existing property-conditional generative models (e.g., models fine-tuned for classifier-free guidance) as guided proposals inside DTS, without distorting the target distribution. Second, we extend DTS to multi-objective sampling, so that a single search produces samples spanning an entire Pareto front. We instantiate both adaptations on de novo crystal generation: improving the stability of crystals generated by different models, using pretrained conditional models to generate crystals belonging to rare space groups or with high bulk modulus, and generating crystals along a Pareto front of high dielectric constant and high bandgap materials. Across these tasks, DTS-based inference-time adaptation successfully improves model outputs without modifying the models themselves.
