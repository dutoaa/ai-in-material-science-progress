Improved screening of doped ZrO$_2$ catalysts for CO$_2$ hydrogenation from graph-based machine learning

Karsten Walz Jensen, Mie Andersen, Raffaele Cheula

Dopants can tune the activity of zirconia-based CO$_2$ hydrogenation catalysts through changes in adsorbate and transition-state (TS) energetics. Computing these quantities with density functional theory (DFT) becomes expensive when several dopants, reaction intermediates, and TSs must be screened. Here, we further develop the graph-based machine learning method Wasserstein Weisfeiler-Lehman Gaussian Process Regression (WWL-GPR): we introduce additional electronic-structure and local-environment features relevant for oxide surfaces and use the resulting model to predict adsorption and TS energies on ZrO$_2$(101) surfaces doped with Al$^{3+}$, Cd$^{2+}$, Ga$^{3+}$, In$^{3+}$, and Zn$^{2+}$. Adsorbate-surface structures are represented as attributed graphs that combine bonds connectivity and features. We benchmark WWL-GPR against thermodynamic scaling relations (TSR) for reaction intermediates and Bronsted-Evans-Polanyi relations (BEP) for TSs, and then propagate the predicted energies to microkinetic CH$_3$OH-yield estimates. WWL-GPR consistently improves the prediction of reaction intermediate energetics relative to linear scaling approaches and provides TS estimates that are competitive with BEP-based models. These improvements become especially relevant when the predicted energetics are propagated through the reaction network. Microkinetic calculations show that WWL-GPR-derived energies give CH$_3$OH yields closer to DFT-derived yields than the linear-relation workflow. Degree-of-rate-control analysis links the activity errors to dopant-dependent rate-controlling steps, including HCOO* formation, H$_2$COOH* dissociation, and H$_3$CO* formation. These results support WWL-GPR as a data-efficient surrogate for connecting atomistic energetics with catalyst-screening observables on doped oxide surfaces.

Ceramic Materials: From Atomic Bonding and Processing to Microstructure, Functional Properties, and Biomedical Applications

Jairo Rondon, Juan Valera, Alex Velez Cruz, Irela Perez Magin

Ceramic materials are inorganic, predominantly nonmetallic solids whose performance is governed by the interplay among atomic bonding, crystal structure, processing, microstructure, and functional properties. Their ionic and covalent bonding provides high hardness, thermal and chemical stability, wear resistance, and diverse electrical behavior, but also limits plastic deformation and increases sensitivity to flaws, porosity, grain boundaries, and residual stresses. This review examines ceramics through the processing--microstructure--property--performance relationship, emphasizing the effects of crystal chemistry, defects, grain size, porosity, and phase stability on mechanical, thermal, electrical, and biological behavior. Conventional sintering, sol--gel processing, and additive manufacturing are discussed as key routes for controlling densification, porosity, geometry, and composition. Biomedical ceramics, including alumina, zirconia, calcium phosphates, hydroxyapatite, tricalcium phosphate, and bioactive glasses, are also reviewed. Emerging topics include nanoceramics, smart ceramics, composites, multimaterial manufacturing, defect engineering, immunomodulatory bioceramics, and sustainable processing. Major challenges remain in brittle fracture, processing heterogeneity, degradation control, scale-up, and the porosity--strength trade-off. Future advances will require integrating advanced manufacturing, multiscale characterization, predictive modeling, and data-driven design.

$C_4$-Equivariant Flow Matching on Anisotropic Power-Diagram Graphs for Microstructure Generation

Dawid Lipinski, Jixiang Qing, Henry Moss

Acquiring realistic microstructure data through Electron Backscatter Diffraction (EBSD) is costly and time consuming, often relying on specialised equipment. As microstructures strongly influence material properties, generating realistic samples is essential for modelling the behaviour of polycrystalline materials. We introduce a generative model for synthesising realistic polycrystalline microstructures using flow matching and graph neural networks. By representing microstructures as anisotropic power diagrams, our model learns a compact geometric parametrisation and can render generated samples at arbitrary pixel resolution. A $C_4$-equivariant architecture incorporates rotational symmetry directly into the model, ensuring that rotations of the input noise produce corresponding rotations of the generated microstructure. We also demonstrate how training-free guidance can be used to generate complex microstructures, based on user defined objective function. In particular, we generate microstructures resembling a copper weld, cast metal slab, 3D-printed stainless steel and heterogeneous lamella titanium.

Drude, Where's My Charge? End-to-End Learning of Polarizable Drude Force Fields for Polymer Electrolytes

Artur Lyssenko, KyuJung Jun, Rafael Gomez-Bombarelli

Molecular dynamics simulations of solid polymer electrolytes require force fields that are fast enough to converge ion transport over hundreds of nanoseconds, yet accurately describe the ion-polymer interactions that govern it. Fixed-charge force fields meet the first requirement but neglect electronic polarization, an effect often approximated by scaling ionic charges using empirically chosen factors. Here we present AutoDrude, a differentiable framework that parametrizes polarizable Drude force fields directly from DFT energies, forces, and dipoles, with no empirical tuning and at only 1.20 times the cost of a fixed-charge simulation. For PEO-LiTFSI, the dipole-constrained (hard-constraint) AutoDrude model reproduces the experimental ionic conductivity as accurately as the best charge-scaled model (17% versus 16% mean absolute percentage error). We compare the polarizable forcefield to fixed charge ones models by fitting a fixed charge model. Unlike the charge-scaled models tested here, however, AutoDrude also reproduces the dilute-limit cation transference number, its increase with salt concentration, and the absence of Li-TFSI contact ion pairs at low concentration. A penalty-based (soft-constraint) variant yields a closer fit to the DFT reference yet underestimates the conductivity by up to an order of magnitude, demonstrating that closer agreement with DFT training data alone does not guarantee accurate ion transport. These results indicate that although charge scaling can reproduce ionic conductivity the explicit inclusion of polarization is necessary to capture the concentration dependence of cation transport in PEO-LiTFSI.

Dynamic Chemical Changes of Ti3C2Tx MXenes in Halide Perovskite Precursor Environments

Kirill Sobolev, Nagaprasad Reddy Samala, Mariela J. Pavan, Sharon Hazan, Vladimir Ezersky, Shakked Schwartz

The integration of two-dimensional Ti3C2Tx MXenes into Halide Perovskite (HaP) optoelectronics has shown immense potential for enhancing device performance, yet the fundamental chemical interactions within the precursor ink (a critical stage determining the final material state) remain largely unexplored. This study presents a comprehensive investigation into the chemical stability and surface evolution of Ti3C2Tx MXenes when exposed to the individual and combined components of a model methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI3) solution. By combining detailed X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS) analysis with Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations, we decouple the complex interactions between MXenes and the ink constituents. We demonstrate that elemental iodine (I2) and polyiodides (In-) act as aggressive oxidizers, triggering rapid degradation and dissolution of the MXene flakes. Conversely, lead iodide (PbI2) induces a slower oxidation process driven by the strong adsorption of Pb2+ ions onto oxygen terminated sites. In stark contrast, methylammonium iodide (MAI) exhibits a stabilizing effect: it adsorbs onto the MXene surface via hydrogen bonding, effectively passivating the flakes and retarding degradation. In addition, our results reveal that the sequence of precursor addition governs the MXene surface state, requiring extra caution, but also enabling further tunability. These findings provide a critical view on how MXene integration with materials, in general, and HaP in particular, should be perceived, providing a fundamental for their rational engineering.

VN-GraphGG: von Neumann--Mullins--based graph grain growth simulation

Yuntong Huang, Zhihao Li, Shuyang Dai, Chang Ma, Yang Xiang

Efficient simulations of grain growth are essential for investigating the process-structure-property relationships in polycrystalline materials. We introduce a two-dimensional von Neumann-Mullins-based graph grain growth simulation (VN-GraphGG), which couples the grain-scale kinetics with microstructure reconstruction. The polycrystalline microstructure is represented as a graph with grain and junction nodes. The grain-area evolution on the grain graph is governed by the von Neumann--Mullins (VN) law, while a graph neural network is trained to reconstruct the microstructure from the graph. This separation allows kinetic coefficients to be varied without retraining the neural reconstruction model. Topological changes during grain growth such as disappearance of grains and changes in neighbor relations are handled straightforwardly through reconstruction and postprocessing. Simulations demonstrate accurate predictions of VN kinetics and coarsening statistics, and significant speedup over the phase-field simulations. The proposed method provides a foundation for efficient grain growth simulation based on grain-scale kinetics, opening the way to incorporate a broader range of physical mechanisms. An application example is presented for abnormal grain growth, in which kinetic heterogeneity significantly influences the grain microstructure evolution.

Specialized machine learning force fields for materials dynamics

Yue Wu, Haoyu Wan, Yuan Tian, Deng Pan, Mingwei Chen

Machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) are transforming atomistic simulations by accessing unprecedented length and time scales. While pretrained equivariant graph neural networks achieve robust zero-shot performance for near-equilibrium properties across broad chemical spaces, their translation to complex materials dynamics remains fundamentally challenged by out-of-distribution reactive states, representation biases, and computational scaling limits. In this Review, we examine how physics-driven specialization extends the applicability of MLIPs to complex dynamical systems. We systematically evaluate the structural trade-offs in MLIP design: the undersampling of highly strained configurations, the prohibitive computational overhead of high-order message-passing architectures, and the necessity of nonlocal interactions for open and field-coupled systems. Through four demanding application contexts - electrified interfaces, compositionally fluctuating open systems, multiphase evolution, and large-scale fracture - we establish a framework for observable-specific validation. Highlighting the complementary roles of universal foundation models and task-specific potentials, we emphasize that targeted adaptations must be rigorously benchmarked against intended observables. We conclude with a roadmap for developing physically consistent, hardware-aware force fields that seamlessly connect electronic-structure accuracy to macroscopic materials phenomena.

AC susceptibility, together with magnetic relaxation and memory measurements, confirms slow spin dynamics characteristic of a cluster glass state. Collectively, these results demonstrate that competition between ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic interactions in NiRuMnGa leads to magnetic frustration and stabilizes a cluster glass state.

Resolving the interfacial mechanical landscape of monolayer NbSe2 grown by confined epitaxy

Ye Wang

Confined epitaxy has recently emerged as a novel methodology for controlled growth of high-quality monolayer two-dimensional (2D) materials. However, mechanistic understanding remains limited to analysing nucleus adsorption and diffusion barriers. Here, I discover that van der Waals (vdW) confinement not only affects the specific adsorption and diffusion of nuclei on confined substrates but also induces strong coupling to atomic forces at the interface. Using artificial intelligence (AI) assisted density functional theory (DFT) calculations, I study how vdW confinement couples to the atomic motion of monolayer NbSe2 grown between c-plane sapphire and hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN). The results indicate that interfacial mechanics may explain the success of confined epitaxy. The findings suggest that tuning interfacial mechanics, and related vdW mechanochemistry, could be a promising route to high-throughput, high-quality materials synthesis.

Dislocation mediated twin boundary faceting in $β$-Sn

The deformation and strengthening mechanisms in $β$-Sn are of significant interest because Sn-based alloys are widely used as solder materials.
