Automated Materials Data and Knowledge Extraction using Machine Learning and Large Language Models

Aravindan Kamatchi Sundaram, B. Pabitramohan Prusty, Rohit Batra

Over the past decade, substantial efforts have been devoted to developing machine learning (ML) methods for extracting materials data and, where possible, materials knowledge directly from the scientific literature. This review traces the evolution of these methods from heuristic, rule-based pipelines through natural language processing (NLP) models and transformer-based encoders to, lastly, large language models (LLMs) and agentic systems capable of extracting materials information across multiple modalities, including text, tables, and figures. Beside construction of large, queryable materials databases and knowledge graphs that support downstream materials discovery, we discuss how these advances have enabled new capabilities, such as, generic question answering across materials science, AI co-scientists and chatbots for research assistance, and closed-loop optimization and discovery when integrated with autonomous laboratories. We critically assess the datasets generated using these approaches, their extraction accuracy and computational costs, and the respective strengths and limitations of different methodologies, and even provide practical guidelines for developing more reliable and scalable materials data extraction pipelines. Finally, we identify key open challenges, including the extraction of full materials structure-property-processing-performance tetrahedron at the sample level, the scarcity of reliable benchmark datasets for systematic evaluation, and the resolution of non-standard materials terminology across different document modalities.

Vacancy-enhanced Li solid-solution anodes for solid-state batteries: computational screening enabling high critical current density

Arseniy S. Burov, Nadezhda A. Ashikhmina, Nurlan Zhanabayev, Anton O. Boev, Aleksandra D. Radina, Ilya V. Chepkasov

Sluggish vacancy-mediated self-diffusion in lithium metal limits the critical current density (CCD) of Li anodes in solid-state batteries by promoting interfacial void formation during stripping. To enhance the effective vacancy concentration while preserving the bcc Li host, we screen 50 substitutional solutes using density functional theory (DFT) within a funnel based on automatic phase diagram construction, solution energies, solute-vacancy binding energies, and experimental solubility limits. Using an adaptation of the Lomer model for the dilute limit, we show that only Ag, Ge, and Cd solid solutions are predicted to reach $1~\mathrm{mA~cm^{-2}}$ within their solubility limits. The thermodynamically estimated CCD of Ag at $\sim 9$ at.% reaches $\sim 4~\mathrm{mA~cm^{-2}}$ at a theoretical capacity of $1522~\mathrm{mAh~g^{-1}}$. Migration barrier calculations indicate that the enhancement may be partially offset by vacancy trapping, necessitating further validation via molecular dynamics simulations. Notably, Hume-Rothery rules alone prove insufficient as prefilters, whereas machine-learning models trained on the DFT data can eliminate most unsuitable solutes early in the funnel and extend the workflow to related solid-solution searches.

GBClaw: An agentic AI framework for atomistic grain boundary research and mechanistic discovery

Yazhuo Liu, Kunqing Ding, Xiaoguo Gong, Zifeng Wang, Yichen Yang, Yifeng Che

Agentic artificial intelligence (AI) systems hold immense potential for automating complex workflows in materials discovery and design, but extending them to domain-intensive mechanistic investigations remains challenging. Here, we present GBClaw, an agentic AI framework for investigating the structure and deformation of general grain boundaries (GBs). Although general GBs constitute the vast majority of real-world interfaces in polycrystalline materials, they remain understudied because their structural complexity and interfacial mismatch complicate experimental and modeling research. GBClaw integrates large language models (LLMs) into a modular architecture comprising an orchestrator, specialized agents, domain-specific tools, simulation interfaces, and hierarchical memory. Through dynamic replanning and iterative workflow refinement, the framework effectively connects systematic simulations with targeted mechanistic analysis. Applied to 14 general asymmetric tilt GBs in nickel, GBClaw characterized interfacial defect structures and identified diverse deformation modes across these incommensurate boundaries. Comparing boundary sliding with and without secondary GB misfit dislocations revealed substantial differences in shear strength, underscoring the critical role of defect hierarchies in sliding resistance. Furthermore, researcher-guided follow-up calculations traced an anomalously high shear resistance to metastable boundary structures. This work demonstrates that combining agentic AI with domain expertise bridges high-throughput computational screening with deep physical understanding of complex material interfaces.

Microstructural Generative Inverse Models for Additive Manufacturing Digital Twins

Pablo Luna Falcon, Jason Mayeur, Amirkoushyar Ziabari, Vincent Paquit, Patxi Fernandez-Zelaia

Metal additive manufacturing (AM) offers substantial advantages to the fabrication of complex structural components, but its widespread adoption is slowed due to complexities in the process-structure-property relations which is the cause for uncertainty and drives risk. Accurately quantifying these complex relations solely via experimental workflows is time consuming and costly. Integrated computational materials and engineering (ICME) frameworks address this challenge by leveraging advanced computational tools, such as crystal plasticity finite element models (CPFEM), to predict critical mechanical properties directly from microstructural data. However, these frameworks require a large datasets that are often difficult to obtain, and their predictive accuracy depends on the availability of realistic AM microstructure representations. Generative deep learning models are a promising approach towards synthesizing AM microstructures which contain complex morphological and crystallographic features. In this work, we demonstrate that DDPMs can reliably synthesize digital-twin orientation-imaging micrographs, and we support this claim with quantitative evidence of the fidelity of the generated samples. Additionally, we establish a classifier-free guidance framework for solving complex inverse problems such as property-to-structure and process-to-structure mappings, and we showcase a potential application on a qualification-relevant uncertainty quantification problem. Our work highlights the potential of AI-based generative modeling as a key component of computational frameworks for accelerating AM materials qualification and certification.

Screening strongly correlated materials in solid oxide cells with quantum simulation

Ouns El Harzli, Jelle Bor, Linde Wester, Leonid Zhukov, Hanl Park, Jean-François Bobier

We develop an end-to-end framework for assessing a quantum-computing application in industrial materials science: the screening of strongly correlated oxygen-electrode materials for SOFCs and SOECs. We formulate the materials problem in terms of interface observables including oxygen-vacancy formation energies, vacancy migration barriers, and electronic conductivity. We describe how these observables can be obtained using a diagrammatic GW-based embedding, either GW+DMFT or self-energy embedding theory (SEET), applied to a DFT-screened shortlist. The atomistic electrode/electrolyte interface defines the electronic structure problem from which the correlated impurity models are constructed. Quantum impurity solvers would provide the finite-temperature impurity Green's functions required by the embedding. The computational challenges that classical solvers may encounter as the interacting active space grows, together with the polynomial scaling of quantum Hamiltonian simulation for efficiently represented discretized models, open an opportunity for quantum advantage. We identify the benchmarks needed to assess this opportunity through convergence of vacancy and migration energetics and comparison with the best applicable classical solvers. We provide illustrative logical qubit counts and identify the thermal state preparation, simulation, measurement, and repeated-solve costs needed for a complete resource assessment. We connect microscopic observables to macroscopic engineering metrics using a continuum model for mixed ionic--electronic conducting oxygen electrodes that couples oxygen surface exchange, solid-state oxygen transport, electrode microstructure, and finite electrode thickness. Finally, we estimate the potential worldwide business value of improved electrode materials under SOFC and SOEC deployment scenarios informed by public technical targets and electrolyser deployment data.

Multiscale Modeling of Zr Corrosion: Predicting Microstructures of Oxide Films

K. Starkey, L. Kahle, M. Christensen, V. Eyert, A. El-Azab, C. B. Geller

A first-principles-informed multiscale modeling approach is reported for oxide formation during corrosion of zirconium. The present simulations reproduce the columnar corrosion film microstructure and grain size distribution seen in micrographs of oxidized Zr surfaces. This structural feature emerges from a hexagonal ZrO layer adjacent to the $α$-Zr/ZrO$_{2-x}$ interface, which is dynamically stabilized by continuous heterogeneous grain nucleation and grain boundary mass diffusion. This ZrO layer is identified as a significant oxygen diffusion barrier and a potential contributor to sub-parabolic pretransition corrosion kinetics. The simulations also reproduce the undulatory nature of the metal/oxide interface, leading to regions with tensile stress components capable of nucleating cracks, despite the overall state of compressive hydrostatic stress within the oxide film. First principles density functional theory (DFT) computations of fundamental ZrO$_x$ properties were combined with molecular dynamics simulations using a machine-learned potential based on a DFT training set to provide extensive input to an advanced phase field model for oxide formation and its microstructure, as implemented in the new MedeA PhaseField module. The phase field simulations reported herein incorporated computed stoichiometry-dependent thermochemical data, anisotropic elastic properties, interface energies, bulk and grain boundary mass transport processes, multiphase heterogeneous grain nucleation, and strain energy-dependent phase stabilities.

Rationalizing charge transfer: a single-molecule case study across three distinct surfaces

Benjamin Lowe, Jan Berger, Martin Ondráček, Oleksandr Stetsovych, Shayan Louie, Marco Lozano

Organic molecules are versatile building blocks for the formation of complex low-dimensional nanostructures on surfaces. Recently, interest has grown in translating on-surface synthetic protocols from noble metal surfaces to insulating surfaces, which offer technological relevance, and to magnetic or superconducting surfaces, which offer new routes to correlated phases. It is important, therefore, to understand how these alternative surfaces may alter the properties of adsorbates through processes like charge transfer and screening. Evidence for charge transfer is often obtained experimentally via scanning tunneling microscopy (STM) and spectroscopy (STS), and theoretically via density functional theory (DFT). In this work, we systematically compare the properties of isolated macrocyclic emeraldine (Me$_6$) molecules on three different surfaces: metallic Au(111), a semiconducting ferromagnetic monolayer of FeCl$_2$, and a cleaved superconducting surface of bulk 2H-NbSe$_2$. We show that STS and DFT alone are insufficient to quantify charge transfer for these cases. Instead, we complement these methods with Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) measurements and model Hamiltonian calculations to demonstrate that on Au(111) and FeCl$_2$, Me$_6$ molecules donate a single electron to the substrate, whereas on NbSe$_2$ two electrons are transferred from molecule to substrate. Our work provides insight into the interplay of charge transfer, screening, and molecule-substrate coupling, and develops experimental and theoretical methods for comparing the properties of adsorbates across different surfaces for future studies.

The combined results suggest that the shorter EH side chains modify the out-of-plane packing environment and facilitate vertical electron transport. These results establish a structure-property relationship between side-chain structure, molecular order, exciton coupling and vertical mobility, providing a molecular design basis for optimising organic semiconductors through side-chain engineering.

Unifying Low-Frequency Raman and Infrared Signatures across Perovskites

Ethan Berger, Erik Fransson, Apinya Ngoipala, Lucas Svensson, Priyanka Deswal, Mehmet Baskurt

Perovskites have been studied for a wide range of applications, from oxide-based ferroelectrics to halide-perovskite photovoltaics. Despite their diverse functionalities, these materials share strong lattice anharmonicity arising from structural instabilities such as polar off-centering and octahedral tilting. These anharmonic distortions strongly influence the low-frequency vibrational response and can be observed in infrared (IR) and Raman spectra. However, low-frequency IR and Raman signatures are typically investigated separately, hindering the development of a unified understanding of vibrational spectroscopy across perovskite classes. In this work, we use machine-learning-based molecular dynamics to simulate the IR and Raman spectra of oxide perovskites (SrTiO$_3$ and BaTiO$_3$), halide perovskites (CsPbBr$_3$, CsSnBr$_3$, and CsGeBr$_3$) and double halide perovskites (Cs$_2$AgAlBr$_6$ and Cs$_2$AgBiBr$_6$). By comparing materials exhibiting polar, tilt-driven and mixed instabilities, we find that polar off-centering leads to enhanced low-frequency IR and dielectric responses, whereas nonpolar modes, particularly octahedral tilting, are associated with Raman central peaks. The strength of the low-frequency features also depends on how strongly the underlying modes couple to polarization or dielectric susceptibility. Finally, we show that the dynamics of these instabilities play an important role, with slow polar reorientations shifting the dielectric response to very low frequencies and overdamped tilting modes producing persistent Raman central peaks. These results establish a unified framework connecting low-frequency IR and Raman spectra to the symmetry, coupling and dynamics of their underlying anharmonic lattice instabilities.

First-principles modeling of ordering in Fe-rich Fe-Si alloys

Daniil M. Khodachenko, Andrei V. Ruban, Franco Moitzi, Oleg E. Peil, Vsevolod I. Razumovskiy

The atomic ordering in Fe-rich Fe-Si alloys strongly influences their magnetic properties and mechanical behavior. While general features of long-range atomic ordering are known from the experimentally established phase diagram in this composition range, detailed atomic configurations and driving forces behind them remain speculative. Here, we address this gap by using effective cluster interactions (ECI) obtained from density functional theory (DFT) calculations and subsequent statistical thermodynamics modeling to unravel the interplay between magnetism, composition, and temperature in body-centered cubic (bcc) Fe-Si alloys up to 20 at% Si. Our analysis reveals that the ordering behavior is governed by a strong coupling between magnetic order, lattice expansion, and Si concentration, with specific ECI shells switching sign or magnitude as the magnetic state changes, which is reflected in the phase equilibria and ordering behavior of Fe-Si alloys. In particular, we demonstrate that D0$_3$ ordering is, in fact, primary in the totally ordered ferromagnetic state, while the B2 type of ordering is promoted in the paramagnetic state at elevated temperatures. The temperature-dependent evolution of these ordering patterns, obtained from Monte Carlo simulations based on the magnetically resolved ECIs, reproduces the experimentally observed phase equilibria and short-range order characteristics. By explicitly linking the magnetic state with the effective interactions and macroscopic phase behavior, this work establishes a predictive framework for an ab initio-based description of ordering in Fe-rich Fe-Si alloys and highlights the decisive role of magnetism in ordering phenomena in this system.

STAR-XPC: Set Transformer for Estimating X-ray Transmission in Polymer Composites

Can Erer, H. Birkan Yilmaz, Nebahat Aral

Evaluating the energy transmission ratio of an X-ray beam through a polymer composite is a challenging task in radiation shielding studies. The reference solution with Monte Carlo transport simulation is highly accurate but computationally expensive: each of our reference simulations is capped at $4\times10^9$ photon histories and a 20,000 s (over 5 hours) time limit, while closed-form analytical methods fail to represent such complex scenarios. We present an artificial neural network model that maps the unordered multi-set of molecular constituents, their mass fractions, and four bulk physical descriptors directly to the transmission ratio $I(t)/I_0$ in 1-3 milliseconds. The model is a Set Transformer, which makes it permutation invariant by construction and accepts mixtures of different sizes up to 11 constituents within a fixed-size padded set. Each constituent is embedded with a chemistry-pretrained transformer language model, ChemBERTa. In our proposed method, training data are produced by an automated PENELOPE/penEasy pipeline using the radiation source at 30, 80, 100, and 120 kV tube voltages over polyethylene-based shielding composite slabs of thicknesses spanning 0.15-2.5 mm. After preprocessing, the dataset contains $n = 4697$ simulated configurations on which a leakage-free, jointly stratified 10-fold cross-validation is performed, attaining mean squared error MSE = $(1.12 \pm 0.56)\times10^{-4}$, normalized-MSE percentage NMSE% = 0.0294% $\pm$ 0.0147%, and $R^2 = 0.9988$. The corresponding per-sample percentage errors give an alpha-trimmed ($α= 0.10$) absolute percentage error (APE) of 1.66% and a median APE of 1.15%. Our model's inference is several orders of magnitude faster than the underlying Monte Carlo simulation (sub-millisecond per query vs. several hours for the reference run) with effective accuracy in predicting $I(t)/I_0$.

Probing the limits of forward screening and inverse design of crystal materials

Zhilong Song, Zijun Pang, Lixue Cheng

Crystal materials are designed either by screening known crystals for a target property or by generating new crystals with that property. Both routes are judged against public databases of known crystals, but these databases name the same crystal differently. Several materials announced as new by recent generative and autonomous discovery campaigns have since proved to be known phases or close variants of them. The limits of both routes remain unmeasured. Here we probe them by matching the crystals of 55 databases by structure into one self-extending database of 14.6 million crystals and 131.8 million property values, each traced to its source. Only 4.5% of its crystal-property pairs carry a value. To fill the empty pairs, we developed CGTNet, an equivariant graph transformer with state-of-the-art accuracy on several tasks of two public benchmarks. Trained on each task, it forms a library of property models whose predictions we release. The reach of forward screening has been underestimated. Known crystals hold tens of thousands of ultra-incompressible candidates without a recorded bulk modulus, and a post hoc audit of synthesis evidence finds among them ReN2, whose bulk modulus was measured at 428 GPa. The novelty claimed for inverse design has been overestimated. At an equal verification budget, a stiffness-conditioned generator returned more stiff hits than screening (100 versus 55), but all contained osmium and 82 repeated recorded compositions. Integrating and completing the databases of known crystals therefore moves the limits of both routes: it widens what screening can reach and sets the reference against which generated crystals are judged new.

Nitrogen vacancy cluster-mediated ferroelectric domain growth in wurtzite AlN

Zijian Zhou, Kan-Hao Xue, Ziqi Pang, Heng Yu, Shanzhong Xie, Hang Liu

Aluminum nitride-based ferroelectrics combine high remanent polarization, thermal stability, weak size effects, a wide bandgap and back-end process compatibility, making them promising for high density non-volatile memory. However, their high coercive field ($E_\mathrm{c}$) and unclear polarization switching mechanism limit further applications. Theoretical predictions indicate preferential reversed domain growth along the polar axis, whereas experiments show nearly isotropic growth or preferential growth perpendicular to this axis. Here, we combine density functional theory, Deep Potential molecular dynamics and experimental characterization to determine how nitrogen vacancies ($V_\mathrm{N}$) regulate domain growth and $E_\mathrm{c}$. In ideal crystal, strong lattice coupling along the polar axis drives preferential domain growth in this direction. $V_\mathrm{N}$ locally weakens this coupling and promotes polarization nucleation. The spatial distribution of $V_\mathrm{N}$ and the temporal sequence of vacancy induced switching jointly determine the direction and morphology of reversed domain growth. We further show that scandium doping promotes $V_\mathrm{N}$ formation, thereby enhancing defect mediated nucleation and reducing $E_\mathrm{c}$. These findings establish a multiscale mechanism governing thin-film domain-growth kinetics and macroscopic $E_\mathrm{c}$, reconciling experimental observations with ideal crystal simulations and providing defect engineering principles for low-voltage, reliable and scalable ferroelectric devices.
