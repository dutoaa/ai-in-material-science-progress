A Practitioner's Guide to Materials Theory and Computation in the Age of Big Data and Artificial Intelligence

Jack Yang

Computational modelling has become a cornerstone of materials science, with advances in atomistic simulations, high-throughput computation, and artificial intelligence transforming how materials are understood, discovered, and designed. These developments build upon generations of theoretical innovations that have translated complex mathematical and physical principles into practical predictive tools. However, the growing diversity of computational methods presents challenges for practitioners seeking a coherent understanding of their theoretical foundations. Drawing upon the author's research experience, this monograph provides a practitioner-oriented, pedagogical overview of computational materials science, unified through the concepts of potential-energy landscapes and statistical sampling. It explores how these concepts underpin structural stability, phase behaviour, and electronic and magnetic properties, through a systematic treatment of empirical force fields, density-functional theory, many-body electronic-structure methods, statistical sampling, and property prediction. It further examines high-throughput workflows, materials databases, and the probabilistic foundations of artificial intelligence. Particular emphasis is placed on atomistic representations, graph neural networks, and generative models, highlighting the roles of symmetry, group theory, differential geometry, and stochastic processes in modern materials modelling. By connecting established physical theories with emerging data-driven approaches, this monograph offers a unified perspective on computational materials science and identifies opportunities and challenges toward physics-informed, efficient, and reliable materials discovery.

IANN: A Unified Framework for Equivariant InterAtomic Neural Networks with User-Friendly Implementation

Changzhi Ai, Xueping Qin, Dimosthenis Sokaras, Frank Abild-Pedersen, Johannes Voss

Machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) now approach density functional theory (DFT) accuracy at a fraction of its cost, but the practical benefit is limited by software fragmentation: each state-of-the-art architecture is released as a separate codebase, with its own data format, configuration system, training loop and molecular-dynamics interface. Comparing two architectures on the same dataset, or moving a trained model into a production simulation, therefore requires substantial re-engineering. We introduce IANN (InterAtomic Neural Network), an open-source PyTorch framework that reimplements equivariant graph neural networks - PaiNN, NequIP, MACE, Allegro, EquiformerV2, EquiformerV3 and UMA - behind a unified data object, a unified trainer and a single deployment route, so that the architecture is a one-word change in a configuration dictionary. Forces, stresses and virials are obtained for every architecture from one analytic differentiation of the energy with respect to the edge vectors of the atomic graph, exact under periodic boundary conditions. Distributed training is configured automatically from the SLURM, PBS or MPI environment, so the same script runs unchanged from a workstation to a multi-node GPU allocation. Self-describing checkpoints serve directly as ASE calculators, as ensembles reporting uncertainties, or as TorchScript models run inside LAMMPS through bundled C++ pair styles with single- and multi-GPU inference. Trained on a common dataset of millions of structures, the architectures can be compared under identical conditions. We further release PaiNN foundation models trained on 41.9 million curated DFT structures from MPtrj, Alexandria, OMat24, MatPES, OC20, OC22, OC25, MPr2SCAN and MatPESr2SCAN, spanning the PBE, RPBE and r2SCAN functionals. Additionally, IANN provides an agent interface to facilitate integration with autonomous agents.

Adsorbate-Free Screening of Dissociative Water Adsorption on Nickel Nanoparticles via Graph Neural Networks

Marta Monti, Alec Noppe, Olivier Lugier, Maria B. Brands, Bernd Ensing

Dissociative water adsorption is a key step in heterogeneous and electrochemical catalysis, but on finite Ni nanosystems the OH$^*$ and H$^*$ fragments can occupy many combinations of surface sites, making exhaustive density functional theory (DFT) screening impractical. We introduce a graph neural network (GNN) framework with an adsorbate-free representation: candidate O- and H-binding sites are defined by sets of Ni atoms (\textit{anchors}), and each site pair is encoded as a graph constructed solely from these anchors and their local Ni environment. Using only MACE-relaxed bare Ni geometries as input, separate GNNs predict the DFT-reference adsorption energies of OH$^*$+H$^*$ configurations and whether the proposed binding sites would be retained upon DFT relaxation, eliminating the need to construct or relax adsorbates during screening. Trained across Ni nanoclusters and Ni(111)/Ni(100) surfaces, the energy model achieves a mean absolute error of 0.031 eV on the internal validation set and demonstrates transferability to unseen Ni$_{55}$, Ni$_{91}$, and Ni$_{130}$ clusters and larger surface cells, particularly for relative-energy ranking, although quantitative accuracy varies across systems. These independent tests also support the retention classifier's use as a conservative filter for selecting configurations likely to preserve their proposed binding sites. Application to Ni nanoparticles containing up to $\sim$3000 atoms identifies favorable co-adsorption motifs and shows that lower O-site coordination generally stabilizes the dissociated state, whereas H-site coordination has a weaker influence. The framework thus offers an efficient route to screening adsorption energetics, with potential extensions to other metals and adsorbate combinations.

Nahual: A Sequence Model for Language and Atoms

Austin H. Cheng, Marcel Müller, Andreas Burger, Yeonghun Kang, Tsz Wai Ko, Marta Skreta

Generative models play a rapidly growing role in the design and simulation of molecules. The most widely used applications of generative models are in large language models (LLMs), owing to the natural flexibility of language for specifying and answering queries. However, generalist LLMs do not effectively handle large sets of 3D coordinates, whereas specialist generative models of 3D molecules cannot reach the controllability afforded by language. To bridge these capabilities, we propose Nahual, a decoder-only autoregressive diffusion model for any sequence of text and 3D molecules. To enable expressive autoregression on continuous coordinates, we propose next-token diffusion, whereby a causal transformer denoises each token while attending to the clean tokens before it, without increasing context size. We train Nahual on a curated set of twelve 3D chemistry tasks ranging from microsolvation to molecular conformer search to adsorption on metal surfaces, with associated evaluation metrics and baselines. The result is a single set of end-to-end-trained weights that simultaneously performs all tasks and surpasses state-of-the-art models specialized for crystal structure prediction and property-conditional molecule generation. Our results demonstrate the viability of scaling generalist decoder-only models for native multimodal understanding and generation across language and chemistry. Code is available at https://github.com/the-matter-lab/nahual.

A Density Functional Tight Binding Model for Rapid Screening of Spin-Orbit-Coupling-Driven Topological Properties in Bismuth

Nir Goldman, Ben Hourahine

We present a transferable Density Functional Tight Binding (DFTB) model for bismuth with explicit spin-orbit coupling that enables exploration of the structural degrees of freedom governing topological properties with minimal computational resources. We have used our DFTB model to run high-throughput calculations at a scale that would be significantly more expensive with standard Kohn-Sham Density Functional Theory (DFT) to determine a strain-buckling map of electronic and topological properties for bismuthene, where we identify regions of band gap closure and band inversion as a function of structural parameters. We show that our approach yields an accurate band structure for the rhombohedral phase, where small band gaps exist at or near time-reversal-invariant momentum (TRIM) points. We then demonstrate the transferability of our model to bismuth properties not included in our training data through determination of the rhombohedral phase phonon density of states and energetic ordering of additional bismuth solid phases. Finally, we have validated our topological predictions through comparison to DFT results for the optimized bilayer geometries, a band inversion order parameter, and the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant as a function of monolayer unit cell length. Our efforts establish DFTB as a rigorous approach for topological characterization while yielding approximately three orders of magnitude increase in computational efficiency relative to DFT. Our model can thus serve as a computational tool for topological materials discovery by enabling large-scale computational screening to address the potentially complex relationship between physical-chemical properties and the resulting topological classification.

Energetics of Coherent Heterophase Interfaces: Separating Chemical and Strain Contributions

Adolfo Salgado-Casanova, Jacob Pietryga, Yi Xia, Chris Wolverton

Coherent interfacial energies govern the formation and evolution of multiphase microstructures, but their calculation is complicated by the elastic energy required to accommodate lattice mismatch. Here, we construct a high-throughput DFT database of 365 coherent FCC/FCC, BCC/BCC, and HCP/HCP interfaces involving 41 metallic elements and lattice mismatches of up to 25%. We use the supercell approach, consisting of constructing superlattices of different thicknesses to separate the coherency strain energy from the chemical contribution to the interfacial energy. We classify the results according to the long-range ordering tendency of each binary system and the sign of the calculated interfacial energy. Most phase-separating systems exhibit positive interfacial energies, whereas most compound-ordering systems exhibit negative values, reflecting favorable unlike-atom bonding. Exceptions show that the chemical preference at a particular interface need not coincide with bulk phase behavior: coherency strain can oppose ordering, and stable compounds can adopt structures distinct from the calculated superlattices. No clear correlation of interfacial energy with lattice mismatch is observed, whereas coherency strain energy increases with mismatch. For phase-separating systems, the lowest interfacial energies generally occur along the closest-packed orientations, while this trend is absent for ordering systems. Comparisons with reported interfacial energies and binary phase diagrams show qualitative consistency, although quantitative agreement varies with reference states, structural constraints, and temperature. The database provides a systematic reference for metallic coherent heterophase interfaces and demonstrates the importance of separating strain and chemical energy contributions when interpreting alloy behavior.

Large-scale Monte Carlo/Molecular Dynamics simulations with Atomic Cluster Expansion potentials in LAMMPS

Lorenzo Piersante, Anirudh Raju Natarajan

Monte Carlo/molecular dynamics (MCMD) simulations are widely used to study the properties of materials at the mesoscale. Simulations with hundreds of thousands of atoms usually rely on empirical potentials because MCMD with machine-learning interatomic potentials is impractical at this scale in existing implementations. Here, we introduce a hybrid MPI/OpenMP scheme for MCMD with Atomic Cluster Expansion (ACE) potentials in LAMMPS. Monte Carlo moves are evaluated from local energy changes and carried out in parallel over MPI ranks, while molecular dynamics is parallelized over OpenMP threads. At a fixed number of CPUs, the wall time of the Monte Carlo moves decreases by a factor equal to the number of MPI ranks. Larger simulation cells also reduce the statistical errors of free energy calculations because thermal fluctuations decrease with system size. We demonstrate the scheme by calculating free energies of the HCP and BCC phases in the strongly anharmonic Hf--Ti system and by simulating zirconium segregation in a nanocrystalline copper sample of 670,000 atoms. For Cu--Zr, an ACE potential and an empirical potential predict similar grain boundary compositions but different amounts of zirconium dissolved in the grains. The scheme makes MCMD simulations with machine-learning interatomic potentials practical at the length scales of microstructure.

Validating Graph Neural Network Feature Attribution Through Local Perturbations: A Study on CoMoFeNiCu Decomposition Enthalpy

Miguel Tenorio, James Chapman

Graph neural networks (GNNs) show promise for predicting materials properties, but interpretability remains a challenge, particularly understanding whether attributed feature importance reflects physical reality or statistical artifacts. Here, we present a methods validation study that deliberately overfits a physics-aware GNN ensemble to probe the robustness of feature-ranking approaches. Using the CoMoFeNiCu high-entropy alloy system with three stoichiometries, we train GNNs to predict decomposition enthalpies from DFT-calculated structures while explicitly constraining output weights to encode feature importance. By design, our dataset is limited (approximately 900 binary and 4,500 ternary decomposition pathways) to control compositional complexity and enable precise interrogation of local chemistry effects. We validate that feature rankings capture meaningful local-environment information through a series of atomic swap perturbations: perturbing individual atomic sites and re-ranking features reveals spatial decay consistent with the GNN's interaction cutoff, confirming that weights encode neighbor-shell effects rather than global stoichiometric artifacts alone. Results demonstrate that feature attribution methods can remain physically interpretable even under overfitting, provided validation is rigorous. This work establishes a foundation for trustworthy interpretability in materials GNNs and sets the stage for scaling to multiple chemistries and testing generalization across compositional spaces.

On these data we trained a generative model for crystal structures, a universal interatomic potential and graph neural networks for more than ten properties, from band gaps to superconducting critical temperatures. Asked for an air-stable alternative, it designs one and tests it against new structures from the generative model. With web search alone, the same agent proposes what the literature already discusses. Put together, the data, the models and the reaction graph let a general agent do what none of them does alone: go from a question in plain words to an experiment that a laboratory can run and test.

A database of optical and thermal-radiative behaviour of materials under solar radiation

Vasiliki E. Alexopoulou, Nikolaos Karalis, Konstantinos Asimakopoulos, Andrei Totu, Constantin Sandu, Paul-Peter Sotiriadis

Solar-matter interactions are important in many scientific and engineering applications. Modeling them needs consistent optical and thermal-radiative properties over the full solar spectrum. Here we present SpaderOptics, a database for 21 materials: metals, oxides, semiconductors, polymers, ceramics, and carbon. It compiles wavelength-dependent refractive indices and extinction coefficients from the literature. Each literature source is kept separate, together with its original conditions. One physics-based calculation chain turns these data into reflectivity, transmissivity, absorptivity, and emissivity for different incidence angles, slab thicknesses, and temperatures. Combined with the Air Mass Zero (AM0) solar spectrum, the properties give absorbed, reflected, and transmitted solar flux for concentrated sunlight. Equilibrium temperatures are given for both a theoretical lumped body and for a slab body, in which thermal conduction is considered. All data are limited to the solid state, up to a material-specific upper temperature limit. Currently, the database applies to extraterrestrial (AM0) sunlight only. A graphical App is also provided for exploring and visualizing the database.

Metallic or insulating magnetic phases of transition-metal oxides in light of the Slater vs Mott controversy

Xiuwen Zhang, Jia-Xin Xiong, Alex Zunger

Magnetic d-electron oxides usually have a long-range-ordered ground state and an above-ground-state disordered para phase. The historical Slater-Mott controversy still reverberating to date pertains to understanding the origin of the insulating vs metallic nature of these two phases. Slater suggested that the insulating character evolved from the long-range order (LRO) antiferromagnetic (AFM) cell-doubling, leading to energy band repulsion and thus to forming a band gap. The reliance on LRO to explain gapping suggests that the absence of LRO in the spin-disordered para-phase would deprive the system from band repulsion, thus predicting a metallic paramagnetic (PM) phase. This, however, conflicts with numerous current observations. The alternative Mott-Peierls-Hubbard view abandons the mean-field-like band structure view emphasizing instead the local interelectronic repulsion U as a strong correlation signature of gapping both at the presence or absence of LRO. However, U is not an easily measured or calculated quantity in periodic systems, a situation often leading in practice to treating U as an adjustable parameter. The current work uses a parameter-free mean-field-like approach (Density Functional Theory, DFT), allowing energy-lowering symmetry breaking. This is done by considering local magnetic motifs as building blocks of the macroscopic AFM or PM phases. It is found that even when such motifs are isolated electronically from each other (i.e. vanishing LRO) by embedding them in quantum barriers (such as magnetic NiO6 octahedra surrounded locally by wide-gap MgO) they exhibit an intrinsic band gap. Thus, the origin of the band gaps in AFM or PM phases need not be LRO or strong correlation and can be understood within the mean-field-like band structure approach with symmetry breaking.

Crystal Structure Prediction from Search to Learning: A 30-Year Expedition

Jianjun Hu, Ying Feng

Crystal structure prediction (CSP) determines periodic atomic or molecular arrangements from chemical composition and physical conditions. Over three decades, CSP has evolved from chemical construction and global energy optimization to hybrid approaches combining symmetry, learned potentials, adaptive search, and deep generative models. Numerical experiments on a short-fibre reinforced composite, a polyurethane foam and a sand-based binder jet microstructure demonstrate robust convergence and competitive accuracy compared with established voxel discretizations.
